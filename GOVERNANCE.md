# Editorial governance

OIBOK is an open research publication with its own identity. Intent & Effect is the founding steward and initial publisher, responsible for the official publication, infrastructure, release history and initial editorial process.

**Founded and maintained by Intent & Effect.**

## Stewardship and participation

The project welcomes external research, criticism and practical contributions. Intent & Effect authorises official releases. External contributors can propose evidence, corrections and changes through the contribution process. The founding relationship is disclosed wherever the publication is presented.

OIBOK has not established a separate legal entity, accredited standards body or formal academic journal. A broader editorial board or community representation may be developed as participation grows; appointments and responsibilities will be published when they exist.

## Editorial responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Founding steward | Maintain the official identity and infrastructure, appoint maintainers and authorise releases |
| Editorial maintainers | Assess proposals, preserve conceptual consistency, maintain research registers and record decisions |
| Subject-matter reviewers | Provide specialist assessment for a stated scope, with their role, relevant interests and independence accurately described |
| Contributors | Submit evidence, critique, field observations and publication improvements under the contribution policy |

These are responsibilities within the editorial process. They do not imply that a separate team or independent review body exists for every role. Where one person performs several roles, the decision record should make that clear.

## How editorial decisions are made

Decisions consider the research question, methodological quality, relevance, conceptual clarity, competing explanations, traceability, rights, accessibility and effects across chapters. Commercial interest, popularity and alignment with the current model are not substitutes for evidence.

For a material proposal, the record should identify:

- The question, affected passage and source version.
- The evidence considered, including contrary evidence and important limitations.
- The reviewers and relevant conflicts of interest.
- The decision, reasons, unresolved questions and date.
- The affected registers and intended release.

Possible outcomes are accept, accept with changes, defer, reject with reasons, or retain as an open question. Uncertainty can remain visible in a released synthesis; it should not be converted into a settled finding by editorial wording.

## Conflicts and independence

Authors and reviewers disclose material financial, employment, funding, institutional or other interests that could influence the work. Intent & Effect's founding and commercial role is disclosed openly. Where practicable, a reviewer with a material conflict should step aside from the affected decision.

Editorial review and internal consistency checking have a different scope from independent academic peer review or empirical validation. Review descriptions must state what was done, by whom and with what independence. If independent review is unavailable, that limitation remains visible.

## Reconsidering a decision

Contributors can request reconsideration in the relevant [GitHub issue](https://github.com/IntentEffect/OIBOK/issues), identifying a factual error, process concern or new evidence. The response should address the stated grounds and record the outcome. An uninvolved reviewer should assess the request where possible. If the project cannot provide independent reconsideration, it should say so.

Do not place sensitive personal information or confidential complaints in a public issue. For a process concern that is unsuitable for a public issue, contact [info@oibok.org](mailto:info@oibok.org). Include enough context to identify the matter without sending unnecessary personal or confidential information.

## Changes to the model

A proposed change to the working definition, three transformations, six capabilities, chapter boundaries or other central claims requires:

1. A clear reason for the change and a comparison with the existing account.
2. Supporting and contradictory evidence, counterexamples and competing explanations.
3. Critical review proportionate to the change.
4. An impact analysis across chapters and research registers.
5. Reconciliation of affected terms, propositions, diagnostics, references and open questions.
6. An authorised, versioned release explaining the change.

The current architecture uses **Orient, Coordinate and Improve** as three connected transformations and **Sense, Know, Choose, Mobilise, Learn and Adapt** as six operational capabilities. These remain a working model whose empirical status must be described accurately.

## Releases and corrections

OIBOK 1.0 is the research baseline released on 15 August 2026. Its chapter components retain version 0.3 because the release promoted those exact component files. The release completion record governs their released status.

The proposed 1.1 amendments remain proposals until an authorised release incorporates them. Publishing a new website or toolkit does not silently replace the 1.0 research baseline.

Maintain archived releases, dates, source manifests and release notes. Correct material errors openly and identify superseded claims. Use a new research version for substantive changes to evidence or interpretation. Keep website presentation corrections and practical toolkit versions distinguishable from research editions.

## Copyright and official identity

Contributors retain copyright and license original publication contributions under CC BY-SA 4.0. Intent & Effect does not acquire contributor copyright through stewardship. Reuse and adaptation follow the [licensing policy](/licensing/).

People may create and share independent adaptations under the applicable licence. Attribution must not imply official OIBOK status or endorsement. The publication licence does not grant trademark rights in OIBOK or Intent & Effect names and marks.

## Evolving this policy

Governance changes should be proposed publicly, explain their purpose and effect, and be recorded in version history. The founding steward authorises changes during the initial stewardship period. Any future transfer of responsibilities should identify who is accountable for editorial decisions, infrastructure and the official release.

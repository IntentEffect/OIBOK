# Research methodology

OIBOK brings together literature investigation, conceptual analysis, synthesis across disciplines, critical review and versioned publication. Its purpose is to make claims about Organisation Intelligence explicit, traceable and open to challenge.

## The status of the current research

The 1.0 release contains ten chapter components, supporting research registers, a documented adversarial review, bibliographic corrections and a final check of consistency between chapters and derived material. It is a literature-grounded working synthesis. Independent field validation of the complete construct remains future research.

The current evidence does not establish a validated OI scale, maturity model, composite score or certification scheme. Internal consistency establishes that records reconcile within a defined scope; it does not establish that the proposed model predicts organisational outcomes.

The process below is the editorial standard for continuing work. It is not a claim that every existing chapter was produced through a preregistered systematic review. The 1.0 release record also notes that stable older references were structurally reconciled without every source being independently reopened.

## 1. Define the inquiry

State the research question, organisational phenomenon, unit and level of analysis, relevant contexts, conceptual boundaries and possible counterexamples. Identify the chapter that owns the claim, related propositions and existing open questions.

Describe what would count against the proposed explanation. Distinguish organisation-level capacity from the performance of one individual, AI system, task or episode.

## 2. Find relevant evidence

Consider empirical studies, systematic reviews, theoretical work, authoritative reports, standards, preprints and documented field cases. Prefer original sources for claims about methods and findings. Use commentary to locate a question or source, then inspect the underlying evidence where available.

For a substantial review, record databases or search services, queries, search dates, inclusion criteria, exclusions and coverage limitations. State whether the full text, an abstract or another account was inspected. Record publication dates separately from data-collection periods and later revisions.

## 3. Assess the evidence

Assess how well each source can support the particular claim being made. Publication venue or peer-review status is one consideration among several.

| Question | What the assessment should examine |
| --- | --- |
| Does the study address the claim? | Research question, conceptual definitions, level of analysis and fit with the OIBOK claim |
| How credible is the finding? | Design, measures, sample, comparison, uncertainty, missing data and plausible sources of bias |
| What explains the result? | Causal identification, confounding, selection, reverse causation and competing mechanisms |
| Where might it apply? | Organisational setting, task, workforce, scale, culture, time and other limits to transferability |
| Can the work be checked? | Methods, data or code availability, replication, corrections and conflicts of interest |
| How current is it? | Subsequent evidence, retractions, model or product changes, and dependence on a particular provider or deployment |

Use a reasoned judgement with stated limitations. Avoid treating a single numerical quality score as a substitute for methodological appraisal. Record null results, failures and contrary evidence alongside positive findings.

## 4. Separate findings from interpretation

Keep five things distinguishable: what the source found, what its authors infer, what OIBOK infers, the values or normative commitments involved, and hypotheses that require testing.

Compare alternative accounts before changing a chapter. A result associated with AI use might also depend on task selection, prior skill, process redesign, training, extra supervision or a changed evaluation method. Identify which explanations the evidence can distinguish and which remain open.

Relate new concepts to established terminology. Explain the added explanatory or practical value of a new term rather than introducing a new label for an existing idea.

## 5. Make the proposed change explicit

Identify the existing passage and version, proposed wording, supporting evidence and confidence limits. State whether the finding supports, qualifies, contradicts or replaces a current claim. Explain implications for the owning chapter and connected chapters.

A material research proposal should include:

- Source title, authors, identifier and stable link.
- Publication and study dates, source version and access level.
- Methods, sample, relevant findings and limitations.
- Competing explanations and unresolved research gaps.
- Chapter, proposition and open-question mapping.
- Previous coverage and the meaningful development, if any.
- Proposed editorial action, reviewer, decision and release reference.

## 6. Reconcile connected material

Approved changes must be carried through the glossary, proposition and evidence register, diagnostic material, traceability matrix, open-question register and source index where affected. Preserve stable identifiers and make superseded claims traceable.

A change to a chapter can alter the meaning of a diagnostic prompt or practical tool. Review those implications explicitly without assuming that every research construct should become a product feature or assessment measure.

## 7. Invite critical review

Review citation accuracy, methods, counterexamples, conflicting evidence and limits of generalisation. Seek specialist and independent input where feasible. Record the review scope and relevant interests.

Editorial review is not automatically formal academic peer review. AI-generated criticism can help find questions but does not establish reviewer independence or resolve them. Authors and maintainers remain responsible for the final judgement.

## 8. Test the model and tools in practice

Field studies should examine persistence across organisational episodes, differences from adjacent constructs, the quality of transitions between capabilities, causal alternatives and transferability. Use designs appropriate to the claim and report unsuccessful or ambiguous results.

The practical toolkit provides methods for piloting. A completed worksheet or a successful case does not validate the complete model. Record the initial situation, intended mechanism, alternatives, outcomes, adverse effects and what changed in subsequent practice. Plan the evaluation before the intervention where possible.

Open research questions include measurement reliability, multilevel effects, cultural and jurisdictional variation, small-business applicability, power and rights, and whether AI augments, substitutes for or erodes retained human capability.

## 9. Publish traceable versions

Publish dated editions with release notes, source manifests, rights notices and preserved earlier versions. Describe what changed, why it changed and which claims remain uncertain. A proposed amendment remains distinguishable from an accepted release.

Correct inaccurate citations, factual errors and retracted evidence openly. Preserve an accessible record of material corrections and the versions affected.

## 10. Maintain an evidence watch

Prioritise substantive new findings, replications, corrected results, retractions and authoritative reports. Compare each candidate with the source manifest and previous coverage. Revisit a previously covered source when there is a meaningful development, and identify that development clearly.

For each retained finding, summarise what changed, assess evidence quality, identify competing explanations and research gaps, map it to the relevant OIBOK chapter, and propose an editorial disposition. A watch item can be recorded as no change, further investigation, proposed amendment or correction. Inclusion in an evidence watch does not automatically change the publication.

Recheck time-sensitive AI evidence before a major release and when a relevant lifecycle event occurs. Keep a clear distinction between observed capabilities in a particular study and claims about lasting organisational capability.

## AI-assisted research and writing

AI may assist discovery, organisation, drafting, translation and editorial checks. Material assistance should be disclosed, together with the verification undertaken. Authors and maintainers remain accountable for source accuracy, intellectual provenance, confidentiality and rights.

Verify citations against real primary sources and check that those sources support the attributed claims. Retain uncertainty when full-text access or independent verification is unavailable. Avoid presenting generated synthesis, review or consistency checking as empirical evidence.

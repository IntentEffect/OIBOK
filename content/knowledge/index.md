## What does OIBOK propose?

A customer accepts a quote on Monday. By Thursday she still has no booking. In this fictional example, sales can see her acceptance in an inbox and scheduling can see an empty slot. What happens between those two desks illustrates three ideas at the centre of OIBOK.

**Orient.** Sales and scheduling trace the last two bookings. One acceptance reached the booking queue through a phone call. The other stayed in the inbox because the person covering sales thought a colleague had passed it on. The two records give the team a plausible explanation to check. If scheduling had already seen both acceptances and was waiting on a technician, the same delay would call for a different response.

**Coordinate.** The people doing the work agree who can mark a job ready, who accepts it and who owns a query about missing details. They follow the next booking far enough to see whether a scheduler can act and the customer receives a date.

**Improve.** The team checks subsequent bookings for delays, repeat calls and extra checking work. A shorter wait might also reflect a quieter week. They compare similar jobs and return to the question when another colleague covers sales. That later case helps them judge whether the change has become part of everyday practice.

These activities can overlap. A returned booking can send the team back to its original explanation. The model names six capabilities involved in this work: **Sense, Know, Choose, Mobilise, Learn and Adapt**. The [interactive model](/model/) shows how they connect. The [OIBOK Guide (draft 0.6.2)](/guide/read/) explains how to investigate a case.

### Where the ideas come from

Earlier researchers asked how organisations use what their people know. In Chapter 10’s account, Wilensky examines how hierarchy affects which warnings get heard. Matsuda considers both the process of becoming informed and the intelligence it produces. Halal connects knowledge to adaptation, Albrecht asks how people put collective capacity to work towards a shared purpose, and Yolles examines how organisations diagnose problems and adapt. [Research lineage and references](/knowledge/integrated-and-responsible-organisational-intelligence/#171-explicit-organisational-intelligence-lineage).

OIBOK brings those questions together around a piece of work you can follow. In the booking case, you can trace the customer’s signal, the team’s interpretation, the authority to act, the outcome and the next colleague’s ability to use what was learned. OIBOK proposes that the connections between those steps help explain why capable teams can still produce a poor result.

### Why there is no overall score

A team might book jobs faster by sending incomplete details to technicians. A single number would conceal who gained time and who absorbed the cost. Combining measures would also require evidence about what to measure, how to weight it and whether the result means the same thing in different organisations. OIBOK 1.0 offers a working synthesis. Independent validation of the whole model is pending. Its practical starting point is a specific claim you can check, such as whether customers received a usable booking date sooner. [Evidence and limits](/research/).

### Eleven working propositions

The propositions play different roles. The first defines the capacity OIBOK is trying to explain. Others propose how it might work. The tenth sets out a judgement about responsible conduct. The short readings below follow the order of the [full propositions and their evidence basis](/knowledge/integrated-and-responsible-organisational-intelligence/#18-integrated-higher-order-proposition-architecture).

| Proposition | In everyday work |
| --- | --- |
| 01 · Persistent organisation-level capacity | A capability belongs to the organisation when people can draw on it across occasions, including after a particular person or tool is replaced. |
| 02 · Selective attention | The organisation needs a way to recognise a clue that matters and get it to someone who can examine it. [Evidence note](/research/integrated-evidence-notes/#02--selective-attention) |
| 03 · An informed account | People making sense of a situation need to know where the evidence came from, whose perspective is missing and what remains disputed. |
| 04 · Authority to commit | Someone with the right to decide must be able to make a commitment, explain it and hear a challenge. |
| 05 · Carrying out the decision | People doing the work need to carry the decision’s purpose and limits across handoffs, including when conditions change. |
| 06 · Feedback about consequences | Check what happened to those affected, how the work was carried out and what else might explain the result. |
| 07 · Learning that persists | A lesson becomes available to the organisation when another person can retrieve it and use it on a later case. [Evidence note](/research/integrated-evidence-notes/#07--persistent-learning) |
| 08 · Adapting capability | Use that learning to decide which practices to keep, develop, combine or retire, and record why. |
| 09 · Quality of the connections | OIBOK proposes that a weak connection can undermine strong individual capabilities. Trace where useful knowledge or authority was lost between steps. [Evidence note](/research/integrated-evidence-notes/#09--quality-of-the-connections) |
| 10 · Responsible exercise | People affected need a voice, a way to challenge decisions and a clear account of who is answerable for the consequences. |
| 11 · AI within organisational work | Assess an AI contribution in the workflow where people use it. Check its sources, authority limits, human oversight, outcomes and how work continues if it is withdrawn. [Evidence note](/research/integrated-evidence-notes/#11--ai-within-organisational-work) |

**Working definition · Research release 1.0, 15 August 2026.**

> Organisational Intelligence is the persistent organisation-level capacity to form an orientation from relevant signals and experience, coordinate collective response, and improve future response through feedback.

Read [Chapter 10](/knowledge/integrated-and-responsible-organisational-intelligence/) for the research argument, counterexamples and open questions. The [glossary](/knowledge/glossary/) defines the terminology.

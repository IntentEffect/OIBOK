# Body of knowledge

OIBOK brings together a literature-grounded account of how organisations orient, coordinate and improve. Use the chapters to examine a particular problem, understand the proposed model and follow the evidence and its limits.

> Organisational Intelligence is the persistent organisation-level capacity to form an orientation from relevant signals and experience, coordinate collective response, and improve future response through feedback.

This is the working definition in **OIBOK 1.0, released 15 August 2026**. It describes a proposed organisation-level construct. The synthesis is not independently field validated and does not provide a validated intelligence score, maturity measure or certification.

## Start with a question

Explore the [interactive OIBOK model, draft 0.2](/model/) for a visual map of the capabilities and their connections. For practical application, read [the OIBOK Guide, draft 0.4](/guide/). Both are drafts for practitioner review, based on the research release below.

For an overview of the complete model, start with [Chapter 10](/knowledge/integrated-and-responsible-organisational-intelligence/), then use the [glossary](/knowledge/glossary/) for its terminology. For a practical investigation, choose a recurring decision or workflow and open the [practical toolkit](/tools/).

The organising transformations are **Orient, Coordinate and Improve**. The six operational capabilities are **Sense, Know, Choose, Mobilise, Learn and Adapt**. These are connected and can recur. Chapter 10 integrates the model rather than adding another operational stage.

## The ten chapters

| Chapter | What it examines |
| --- | --- |
| [1. Signals and Attention](/knowledge/signals-and-attention/) | How material events become visible, receive attention and enter organisational response. |
| [2. Organisational Context](/knowledge/organisational-context/) | The purpose, conditions and constraints that make signals, knowledge and decisions meaningful. |
| [3. Knowledge and Expertise](/knowledge/knowledge-and-expertise/) | How knowledge across people, practices, relationships and records becomes available when it matters. |
| [4. Decision and Authority](/knowledge/decision-and-authority/) | How organisations form legitimate commitments, establish decision rights and remain accountable. |
| [5. Coordination and Collective Action](/knowledge/coordination-and-collective-action/) | How authorised decisions become coherent work across teams, systems, partners and AI. |
| [6. Execution and Operational Reliability](/knowledge/execution-and-operational-reliability/) | How authorised work is performed reliably, including exceptions, recovery and operational control. |
| [7. Outcomes, Feedback and Evaluation](/knowledge/outcomes-feedback-and-evaluation/) | How to distinguish activity and outputs from outcomes and produce credible evaluative feedback. |
| [8. Organisational Memory, Reflection and Learning](/knowledge/organisational-memory-reflection-and-learning/) | How experience is preserved, reconstructed and interpreted to change future understanding and response. |
| [9. Capability Development and Adaptation](/knowledge/capability-development-and-adaptation/) | How learning becomes durable organisational capability and how that capability changes with conditions. |
| [10. Integrated and Responsible Organisational Intelligence](/knowledge/integrated-and-responsible-organisational-intelligence/) | The integrated definition, proposed mechanism, responsible exercise and limits of the whole model. |

## 1. Purpose

This chapter integrates the nine domain chapters into one bounded account of **Organisational Intelligence**.

Its governing question is:

> **What is Organisational Intelligence as a complete organisation-level construct, how does it operate, how can it be observed responsibly, and what distinguishes it from adjacent concepts and merely intelligent-looking organisational behaviour?**

The preceding chapters examined:

1. Signals and Attention;
2. Organisational Context;
3. Knowledge and Expertise;
4. Decision and Authority;
5. Coordination and Collective Action;
6. Execution and Operational Reliability;
7. Outcomes, Feedback and Evaluation;
8. Organisational Memory, Reflection and Learning;
9. Capability Development and Adaptation.

Each chapter developed a detailed local model.

This chapter does not create another operational stage.

It:

- establishes the final working definition for the first coherent BOK release;
- reconciles the three higher-order transformations;
- reconciles the six operational capabilities;
- defines the connective mechanisms and integrated states;
- states the unit and level of analysis;
- specifies the proposed feedback-coupled mechanism;
- decides the current status of Connection Quality;
- integrates purpose, epistemic quality, authority, accountability, ethics, power and contestability;
- integrates human and AI participation without creating a separate AI theory of the organisation;
- states a parsimonious higher-order proposition set;
- defines a bounded evidence and diagnostic architecture;
- distinguishes the construct from its enablers, outcomes and proxies;
- states counterexamples and falsification expectations;
- dispositions the principal open questions and working terms;
- preserves the limitations that synthesis cannot resolve;
- and defines the post-BOK empirical programme.

This chapter is the culmination of a research synthesis.

It is not evidence that Project OI has already established:

- a novel validated theory;
- an organisation-wide diagnostic instrument;
- a causal model proven across sectors;
- a universal governance prescription;
- or a stable account of AI capability independent of model, task, interface, provider and time.

## 3. Final Working Definition

### 3.1 Short Definition

For the first coherent BOK release, Project OI retains the existing short definition:

> **Organisational Intelligence is the persistent organisation-level capacity to form an orientation from relevant signals and experience, coordinate collective response, and improve future response through feedback.**

This definition is intentionally parsimonious.

It contains:

- an organisation-level unit;
- persistence across relevant time;
- signals and experience as inputs;
- orientation;
- collective response;
- feedback;
- and improvement of future response.

It does not attempt to include every mechanism, quality condition, enabling factor or outcome in the core sentence.

### 3.2 Extended Definition

> **Organisational Intelligence is the persistent capacity of an organisational system to convert signals and experience judged material under current purpose and context into a sufficiently shared or aligned orientation, convert that orientation into a deliberate and authorised organisationally enacted response, and use consequences and feedback to strengthen or preserve future response fitness under stated conditions. The capacity is instantiated through the organisation’s people, relationships, structures, practices, technologies and external ties rather than being reducible to the intelligence of any one participant, model, system or episode. Project OI hypothesises that the complete capacity depends materially on the integrity of the transitions among its capabilities.**

The extended definition adds:

- conversion rather than passive possession;
- deliberate and authorised response;
- consequences as the source of feedback;
- sociotechnical and relational instantiation;
- and a testable hypothesis concerning transition integrity.

It does not make:

- ethical purpose;
- favourable outcome;
- decentralisation;
- documentation;
- technology;
- or AI participation

part of the descriptive core.

### 3.3 Responsible Exercise Statement

> **The responsible exercise of Organisational Intelligence requires epistemic quality proportionate to risk, legitimate authority, accountability, contestable purpose, appropriate representation of affected interests, respect for applicable rights and law, and human and technical controls proportionate to autonomy, uncertainty, reversibility and consequence.**

This statement governs how OI should be exercised.

It is not a second definition.

An organisation may display substantial instrumental capability while:

- pursuing a harmful purpose;
- suppressing affected voices;
- using illegitimate authority;
- applying unreliable knowledge;
- or externalising unacceptable harm.

Project OI should describe such an organisation as:

> **instrumentally capable but irresponsibly governed**

rather than defining effective coordination or adaptation as inherently legitimate.

### 3.4 Why “Capacity” Remains in the Core Definition

The term **capacity** is retained for the integrated construct.

The six domains beneath it are called **operational capabilities**.

This preserves a useful distinction:

```text
Organisational Intelligence
= higher-order persistent organisation-level capacity

Sense, Know, Choose, Mobilise, Learn and Adapt
= operational capabilities contributing to that capacity
```

This avoids making Organisational Intelligence appear to be:

- one capability beside six others;
- one technology;
- or a single maturity level.


### 3.5 Interpretation Notes for the Short Definition

The short definition remains compact, but four terms require bounded interpretation.

#### Relevant Signals

Relevance is not inherent in an event or data point. A signal is judged provisionally material in relation to:

- organisational purpose;
- current context;
- affected stakeholders;
- timing;
- risk;
- and the consequence of missing it.

#### Collective Response

Collective response means an organisationally enacted and consequential response that uses, changes or depends on organisational arrangements. It does not require democratic decision-making or several people performing every episode. A founder or authorised individual may act on behalf of an organisation, but a strong OI claim requires the capacity to persist beyond that person’s unaided cognition and permanent availability.

#### Improve

Improve means strengthening or preserving future response fitness relative to an explicit and revisable purpose, affected stakeholders, a relevant time horizon and responsible-use conditions. It does not mean any change, higher activity or favourable result.

#### Persistent

Persistence is claim-specific. A bounded OI claim should state its time horizon and demonstrate at least two of the following where relevant:

- repeated enactment;
- survival beyond one participant;
- retained organisational memory;
- recovery after interruption;
- successful transfer;
- or evidence that prior feedback changed later response.

### 3.6 Current Construct Status

The definition is:

- coherent enough for a first integrated working release;
- grounded in a broad literature synthesis;
- operational enough to support field inquiry;
- and bounded enough to challenge.

It remains:

- provisional;
- unvalidated as a general organisation-level theory;
- and subject to OI-BOK-REVIEW-001.

## 4. The Core Construct

The minimum distinctive phenomenon remains:

```text
ORIENT
        ↓
COORDINATE
        ↓
IMPROVE
```

### 4.1 Orient

> **Orient is the organisation-level capacity to form a sufficiently shared or aligned understanding of what is happening, why it matters, what remains uncertain and what conditions should shape response.**

Orient combines:

```text
Sense
        +
Contextualise
        +
Know
        ↓
Organisational Orientation
```

### 4.2 Coordinate

> **Coordinate is the organisation-level capacity to convert orientation into a deliberate collective response.**

Coordinate combines:

```text
Choose
        +
Mobilise
        ↓
Decision, coordination and execution
```

The term **Coordinate** is broader than the Chapter 5 mechanism **coordination**.

Coordinate includes:

- commitment;
- authority;
- mobilisation;
- dependency management;
- and reliable execution.

### 4.3 Improve

> **Improve is the organisation-level capacity to use consequences and feedback to strengthen future orientation and response.**

Improve combines:

```text
Outcome–Feedback
        +
Learn
        +
Adapt
        ↓
Changed future response capacity
```

Improvement may mean:

- correcting;
- reinforcing;
- stabilising;
- extending;
- retiring;
- or reconfiguring.

It does not require continual visible change.

### 4.4 Why the Three Transformations Are Retained

The three-transformation model is retained because it provides a more parsimonious construct boundary than the complete operational cycle.

It can explain Organisational Intelligence without placing every mechanism inside the definition.

The six operational capabilities make the model actionable.

The three transformations keep it conceptually intelligible.

### 4.5 The Construct Is Not a Linear workflow

The transformations may be:

- iterative;
- recursive;
- concurrent;
- nested;
- distributed;
- partially automated;
- and enacted at different organisational levels.

A routine decision may draw on previously institutionalised orientation and require little explicit search.

A novel incident may move repeatedly among:

- contextualisation;
- knowledge;
- decision;
- and execution.

An emergency may compress deliberation while still relying on prior learning, authority and capability.

The model describes required organisational functions and conversions under bounded rationality rather than assuming complete information or optimisation.[^1]

It does not prescribe one universal temporal sequence for every episode.

## 5. The Operational Capability Model

The Project OI Operational Capability Model remains:

```text
SENSE
        ↓
KNOW
        ↓
CHOOSE
        ↓
MOBILISE
        ↓
LEARN
        ↓
ADAPT
```

### 5.1 Sense

> **The OI operational capability to notice, capture, recognise, qualify and route material signals and experience for organisational attention.**

Sense is shaped by organisational attention structures that influence which issues, actors and answers become salient.[^2]

Sense is not:

- data receipt;
- alert generation;
- or notification volume.

### 5.2 Know

> **The OI operational capability to create, validate, locate, combine and apply relevant knowledge and expertise in context.**

Know integrates knowledge creation, organisational interpretation and distributed expertise rather than reducing knowing to retrieval.[^5][^6][^7]

Know is not:

- document storage;
- search;
- or model recall.

### 5.3 Choose

> **The OI operational capability to frame a response, evaluate alternatives and make an attributable and authorised organisational commitment under uncertainty.**

Choose includes:

- acting;
- delaying;
- monitoring;
- escalating;
- experimenting;
- seeking evidence;
- and deliberate non-action.

### 5.4 Mobilise

> **The OI operational capability to translate an authorised commitment into coordinated, governed and reliable collective action and execution.**

Mobilise contains two distinct mechanism families:

```text
Coordination
        +
Execution
```

### 5.5 Learn

> **The OI operational capability to revise organisationally available understanding, memory, routines and future response through credible feedback, reflection and inquiry.**

Feedback is an input to Learn.

It is not learning by itself.

### 5.6 Adapt

> **The OI operational capability to develop, retire or reconfigure organisational capability, authority, structure and resources in response to credible learning and changed conditions.**

Adapt is not:

- initiative volume;
- transformation rhetoric;
- or technology adoption.

### 5.7 Status of the Six Capabilities

The six capabilities are retained as:

- diagnostic lenses;
- design domains;
- research categories;
- and implementation capabilities.

They are not yet proven to be:

- statistically independent;
- mutually exclusive;
- individually necessary in every episode;
- or sufficient when scored separately.

Their value is operational and explanatory.

## 6. Connective Mechanisms and Integrated States

Three important elements do not become additional operational capabilities.

### 6.1 Contextualise

> **Contextualise is the connective mechanism through which attended signals and available information are related to purpose, entities, relationships, current state, history, time, authority, evidence, permissions, uncertainty and relevant expertise so that they can contribute to orientation.**

Contextualise is not a seventh capability. Context changes the occurrence and meaning of organisational behaviour rather than merely surrounding it.[^4]

It connects Sense and Know.

It also recurs throughout the cycle when:

- a decision changes;
- execution encounters an exception;
- an outcome is evaluated;
- or prior learning is reused.

### 6.2 Organisational Orientation

> **Organisational Orientation is a sufficiently shared or aligned organisational account of what is happening, why it matters, what remains uncertain and what should shape response.**

Orientation is an integrated state.

A bounded orientation should contain enough evidence to identify:

1. **Situation account** — what is believed to be happening;
2. **Significance** — why it matters to the focal organisational system and affected stakeholders;
3. **Material uncertainty** — what remains unknown, disputed or conditional;
4. **Constraints** — which purpose, authority, resources, rights and operating conditions bound response;
5. **Response conditions** — what should shape the next decision, monitoring action or search for evidence.

Orientation is not established by:

- one document;
- unanimous belief;
- a context package alone;
- a model summary or answer;
- or complete knowledge.

It may preserve:

- role-specific interpretation;
- disagreement;
- uncertainty;
- and conditional response.

### 6.3 Outcome–Feedback Bridge

Outcomes are consequences.

Feedback is evidence about consequences made available for evaluation and future response.

The bridge includes:

```text
Output
→ Consequence
→ Observation
→ Evaluation
→ Feedback suitable for learning
```

Outcome is not a seventh capability.

Feedback and evaluation connect Mobilise to Learn.

### 6.4 Memory as Continuity Infrastructure

Organisational memory supplies prior:

- signals;
- context;
- decisions;
- relationships;
- routines;
- and lessons

to the current cycle.

It also retains what the current cycle produces.

Memory is therefore both:

- an input across the model;
- and an output of Learn.

It is not merely a stage after action.

## 7. Unit of Analysis, Emergence and Persistence

### 7.1 Focal Unit

The focal unit is an **organisational system**:

> **A bounded and persistent arrangement of people, relationships, structures, practices, technologies and external ties with sufficient identity and coherence to form orientation, coordinate response, receive feedback and alter future response.**

Possible focal units include:

- a team;
- a function;
- a business unit;
- an organisation;
- an inter-organisational network;
- an ecosystem;
- or a bounded human–AI operating system.

### 7.2 Organisational System Versus Sociotechnical System

The terminology decision is:

- **Organisational system** is the preferred construct-level term for the focal unit.
- **Sociotechnical system** is the preferred implementation perspective describing how the capability is instantiated through interacting human, technical, structural and relational elements.

The terms are related.

They are not identical.

### 7.3 Minimum Coherence Conditions

The selected unit should possess enough identity and continuity to:

1. recognise or receive material signals;
2. form an orientation;
3. make or accept an organisational commitment;
4. coordinate and execute a response;
5. experience or observe consequences;
6. retain or retrieve relevant memory;
7. and change future response.

A temporary collection of people may satisfy these conditions.

A legal organisation may fail to satisfy them in practice.


### 7.4 Claim-Boundary Protocol

Every material OI claim should state:

```text
Focal boundary
Purpose and class of response
Membership and participating actors
Decision and action authority
Organisational memory or continuity mechanism
Feedback receiver
Relevant time horizon
Persistence evidence
Level at which the claim is made
```

An ecosystem-level claim additionally requires an explicit governance and feedback boundary. Shared technology or market participation alone does not create one organisational system.

### 7.5 Multilevel Evidence Rule

Evidence about an individual, team or model supports an organisation-level OI claim only where the evidence is connected to organisational:

- roles;
- authority;
- routines;
- memory;
- transitions;
- and repeated system-level consequences.

Employee intelligence, one team case or model benchmark performance cannot be aggregated automatically into an organisation-level claim.

### 7.6 Emergence

OI is organisation-level because the complete capacity emerges from relationships among:

- individual cognition;
- group interaction;
- roles;
- routines;
- authority;
- technology;
- memory;
- and institutional arrangements.

It cannot be inferred by:

- averaging employee intelligence;
- counting experts;
- measuring one team;
- or reporting model performance.

### 7.7 Multilevel Structure

Nested units can display different forms and degrees of OI.

Examples:

- a project team may orient and learn effectively while the wider organisation prevents lessons from spreading;
- a business unit may act intelligently while creating harmful externalities for the enterprise;
- or an organisation may coordinate well internally while depending on an opaque external platform for critical capability.

Evidence must specify the level at which the claim is made.

### 7.8 Persistence

Persistence means that the capacity is:

- reproducible;
- retained;
- recoverable;
- or improvable

across the period relevant to the claim.

Persistence does not require:

- permanent independence from individuals;
- complete documentation;
- or unchanging routines.

It does require more than one isolated episode of heroic performance.

For a release-level bounded claim, persistence evidence should include at least two relevant forms identified in §3.5 and should state why the chosen time horizon is appropriate to the capability being claimed.

### 7.9 Human–AI Unit Claims

A human–AI operating system may be a focal unit where:

- organisational purpose is clear;
- human and AI roles are bounded;
- authority and accountability are represented;
- memory and state are attributable;
- and the system can receive feedback and alter future response.

The AI model alone is not the organisational unit.

## 8. Proposed Mechanism: Feedback-Coupled Organisational Transformation

Project OI retains the proposed mechanism:

> **Organisational Intelligence operates through feedback-coupled organisational transformation.**

The organisational system repeatedly:

1. converts signals and experience into orientation;
2. converts orientation into collective response;
3. converts execution and consequence into evaluative feedback;
4. converts feedback into learning;
5. and converts learning into changed future capability.

### 8.1 The Complete Candidate Cycle

```text
PURPOSE, GOVERNANCE AND RESPONSIBLE CONDITIONS
shape every part of the cycle

Signals and experience
        ↓
SENSE
Notice, qualify and route material signals
        ↓
CONTEXTUALISE
Connect signals to purpose, relationships, state, time,
history, evidence, authority and uncertainty
        ↓
KNOW
Create, validate, locate, combine and apply knowledge and expertise
        ↓
ORGANISATIONAL ORIENTATION
Form a sufficiently shared or aligned account
        ↓
CHOOSE
Frame alternatives and make an authorised commitment
        ↓
MOBILISE — COORDINATION
Translate commitment into roles, dependencies and shared action
        ↓
MOBILISE — EXECUTION
Perform authorised work under operational control
        ↓
OUTCOME AND FEEDBACK
Observe and evaluate intended, unintended and distributed consequences
        ↓
LEARN
Revise organisationally available understanding and future response
        ↓
ADAPT
Develop, retire or reconfigure capability
        ↓
Changes future sensing, knowing, choosing, mobilisation and improvement
```

### 8.2 Temporal Order

The model contains a plausible temporal order.

It does not claim:

- one rigid sequence;
- equal duration;
- one cycle at a time;
- or one central actor.

The expected dependencies are:

- response requires some orientation;
- learning requires some experience or acquired knowledge;
- and adaptation requires some basis for capability change.

Empirical research must still test:

- how often stages are explicit;
- where order reverses;
- and which transitions are necessary under different conditions.

### 8.3 Recursion

The cycle is recursive.

Examples:

- execution produces a new signal requiring reorientation;
- evaluation changes the framing of the original decision;
- adaptation alters which signals can be noticed;
- and new knowledge changes the organisation’s purpose.

### 8.4 Feedback Closure

The loop is closed only where consequence can influence future organisational capacity.

A report that is never used does not close the loop.

A one-off human insight that disappears does not close the loop.

A system change made without evaluating consequence may alter future response without constituting credible learning.

A claim of loop closure should trace:

```text
Evaluative feedback
        ↓
Retained organisational learning
        ↓
Changed decision, routine, authority or capability
        ↓
Observed use in a later relevant episode or readiness test
```

A post-incident action item or one-off correction is not sufficient evidence of closed-loop Organisational Intelligence.


### 8.5 Current Theory Status

Feedback-coupled transformation is:

- a proposed explanatory mechanism;
- supported by convergent research traditions;
- and made operational through the chapter sequence.

It is not yet a validated causal theory.

## 9. Connection Quality

### 9.1 Current Decision

Project OI retains **Connection Quality** as an explicit integrative research hypothesis and diagnostic working term.

It is not:

- a seventh capability;
- a general process score;
- or a replacement for coordination.

### 9.2 Definition

> **Connection Quality is the reliability and integrity with which one Organisational Intelligence capability or state is converted into, accepted by and made usable within the next relevant capability or state.**

### 9.3 Why It Is Retained

The nine chapters repeatedly show that local capability can coexist with system failure.

An organisation may:

- notice a signal that never receives context;
- possess knowledge that never reaches the decision;
- make a decision that never becomes shared commitment;
- coordinate work that fails in execution;
- execute successfully without observing the outcome;
- evaluate accurately without learning;
- or learn without changing capability.

The transitions are therefore not administrative detail.

Project OI hypothesises that transition integrity explains some organisation-level difference beyond local capability strength. That hypothesis remains unvalidated.

### 9.4 Candidate Dimensions

Connection Quality may be examined through:

1. **Semantic preservation**  
   Does the receiving stage understand what the source stage produced?

2. **Context and temporal integrity**  
   Are scope, state, history and validity preserved?

3. **Evidence and provenance**  
   Can source and transformation be inspected?

4. **Attribution and authority**  
   Are ownership, rights and accountability retained?

5. **Acceptance and readiness**  
   Does the receiving capability accept the output as usable?

6. **Timeliness and continuity**  
   Does the conversion occur within the useful window?

7. **Failure detectability and repair**  
   Can a broken transition be identified and corrected?

8. **Reconstructability**  
   Can the organisation explain how the conversion occurred?

9. **Feedback closure**  
   Can later consequence modify the earlier process or capability?

These dimensions are Chapter-10 synthesis.

They are not validated measurement dimensions.

### 9.5 Relationship to Coordination

Coordination manages dependencies among interdependent contributions during collective action.

Connection Quality applies across the complete OI cycle, including:

- signal to context;
- context to knowledge;
- feedback to learning;
- and learning to capability.

Coordination may be one mechanism supporting Connection Quality.

The terms are not synonyms.

### 9.6 No Universal Connection Score

Connection Quality should be assessed:

- per transition;
- for a defined episode;
- at a stated organisational level;
- and with evidence from both sides of the conversion.

A single score would conceal:

- which transition failed;
- whether the failure was epistemic, temporal, authority-based or operational;
- and how the failure should be repaired.

### 9.7 Empirical Status

The term remains a Project OI integrative extension.

Its discriminant and predictive validity remain open.

## 10. Orient

Orient is the first higher-order transformation.

### 10.1 Sense

The organisation:

- encounters events and experience;
- recognises cues and patterns;
- qualifies materiality;
- allocates attention;
- and routes signals.

Sense quality is not established by:

- data volume;
- alerts;
- dashboards;
- or notification speed.

### 10.2 Provisional Sense Quality Profile

OI-BOK-001 v0.1 did not contain an explicit source quality-dimension section. The adversarial correction pass added a clearly labelled Project OI synthesis section to OI-BOK-001 v0.2 using the following candidates:

1. materiality discrimination;
2. diversity and coverage of signal channels;
3. timeliness;
4. provenance and attribution;
5. routing clarity;
6. attention-capacity and saturation control;
7. contestability of suppression or dismissal;
8. and follow-through into contextualisation.

These remain synthesis candidates requiring empirical testing.

### 10.3 Contextualise

The attended signal is connected to:

- purpose;
- entities and relationships;
- current and historical state;
- authority;
- evidence;
- permissions;
- uncertainty;
- and relevant negative context.

Context should remain:

- purpose-bound;
- temporally explicit;
- evidence-bearing;
- and appropriately incomplete.

### 10.4 Know

Relevant knowledge and expertise are:

- identified;
- evaluated;
- accessed;
- translated;
- and integrated.

The organisation distinguishes:

- information from knowledge;
- expertise from title or tenure;
- knowledge authority from decision authority;
- and explicit representation from tacit practice.

### 10.5 Form Orientation

Orientation is not consensus. It is informed by organisational sensemaking through which circumstances become a situation capable of guiding action.[^3]

It is sufficient alignment concerning:

- the situation;
- its significance;
- uncertainty;
- constraints;
- and response conditions.

### 10.6 Orient Failure

Orient fails where the organisation:

- notices the wrong signals;
- suppresses material evidence;
- overloads attention;
- strips context;
- uses stale authority;
- cannot access expertise;
- manufactures false consensus;
- or presents AI synthesis as source truth.

## 11. Coordinate

Coordinate converts orientation into deliberate response.

### 11.1 Choose

The organisation:

- identifies the decision;
- frames the issue;
- establishes decision rights;
- assembles evidence and dissent;
- evaluates alternatives;
- and creates an attributable commitment.

A recommendation is not a decision.

An approval is not necessarily decision ownership.

Technical capacity is not authority. Formal decision rights, real influence and accountability may sit in different actors and require explicit governance.[^8][^9]

### 11.2 Shared Commitment

The decision becomes organisationally usable where relevant actors understand:

- that it is effective;
- which obligations follow;
- which conditions remain;
- and how dissent, review or appeal operate.

### 11.3 Mobilise Through Coordination

The organisation:

- maps dependencies;
- allocates roles and resources;
- establishes shared state;
- selects coordination mechanisms;
- manages handoffs;
- and repairs interdependent work.

Communication volume is not coordination. Coordination manages dependencies and creates accountability, predictability and common understanding among interdependent contributions.[^10][^11]

Task assignment is not collective action.

### 11.4 Mobilise Through Execution

The organisation:

- establishes operational readiness;
- translates the commitment into operational intent;
- grants action authority;
- performs work;
- observes state;
- handles exception and failure;
- recovers or compensates;
- and sets an accurate completion state.

Task completion is not outcome. Dependability research distinguishes reliability, availability, safety, integrity and maintainability, while high-reliability research emphasises active organising for weak signs and surprise.[^12][^13]

Availability is not correctness or safety.

### 11.5 Coordinate Failure

Coordinate fails where the organisation:

- cannot attribute the decision;
- hides real authority;
- ignores unique information;
- creates approval congestion;
- loses rationale in handoffs;
- optimises local tasks;
- lacks operational state;
- performs unauthorised action;
- or cannot contain or reconstruct failure.

## 12. Improve

Improve converts consequence into stronger future response capacity.

### 12.1 Observe Outcomes

The organisation distinguishes:

- input;
- activity;
- output;
- implementation;
- outcome;
- impact;
- and broader consequence.

It identifies:

- stakeholder;
- value frame;
- time horizon;
- baseline;
- and alternative explanation.

### 12.2 Evaluate Feedback

The organisation:

- assesses implementation;
- examines mechanism;
- tests attribution or contribution;
- evaluates measurement validity;
- searches for unintended and distributed effects;
- and makes a contestable judgement.

A metric is not an outcome. Causal inference, indicator corruption and feedback complexity require separate attention when interpreting consequences.[^14][^15][^16]

A target is not purpose.

Positive movement is not automatically improvement.

### 12.3 Learn

The organisation:

- reconstructs the episode;
- preserves decision-time context;
- reflects on expectation and consequence;
- forms scoped lessons;
- updates memory;
- and makes learning available for future use.

Feedback is not learning. Organisational memory distributes retention across several reservoirs, and learning must become available beyond one episode or participant.[^17][^18][^19]

A lesson recorded is not a lesson institutionalised.

### 12.4 Adapt

The organisation:

- translates learning into a capability implication;
- develops or modifies the capability configuration;
- practises and tests;
- institutionalises;
- evaluates;
- renews;
- scales;
- retrenches;
- or retires.

Change volume is not adaptation. Dynamic-capability and exploration–exploitation research distinguish reconfiguration from ordinary performance and show why current returns can crowd out future capability search.[^20][^21]

Technology adoption is not capability.

### 12.5 Improve Failure

Improve fails where the organisation:

- confuses output with outcome;
- selects targets retrospectively;
- relies on invalid proxies;
- suppresses negative feedback;
- creates a lessons graveyard;
- reconstructs official memory selectively;
- trains without changing work;
- scales before reliability;
- preserves obsolete capability;
- or becomes locked into an external provider it cannot govern or exit.

## 13. Purpose, Governance and Responsible Exercise

### 13.1 Purpose

Purpose determines:

- which signals matter;
- which context is relevant;
- which knowledge is needed;
- which decision is legitimate;
- which coordination is coherent;
- and which outcome counts as improvement.

Purpose remains outside the descriptive core because an organisation can exhibit strong instrumental OI in service of a harmful aim.

For responsible exercise, purpose should be:

- explicit;
- communicable;
- contestable;
- legitimate;
- and revisable.

### 13.2 Epistemic Quality

Evidence quality should be proportionate to:

- risk;
- uncertainty;
- urgency;
- reversibility;
- and consequence.

Relevant qualities include:

- source;
- provenance;
- currentness;
- expertise;
- transparent assumptions;
- confidence;
- counter-evidence;
- and reviewability.

### 13.3 Legitimacy

A response should use authority regarded as legitimate enough to support voluntary deference and implementation, while remaining open to review.[^24]

It should be:

- valid;
- scoped;
- attributable;
- and appropriate to the affected interests and governing environment.

### 13.4 Accountability

Material decisions and actions should remain attributable across:

- people;
- teams;
- leaders;
- systems;
- models;
- AI agents;
- workflows;
- evaluators;
- memory stewards;
- and capability owners.

### 13.5 Contestability

Affected or authorised actors should have appropriate ways to participate and speak up; psychological safety can support the interpersonal conditions for reporting error and challenging assumptions.[^25]

They should be able to:

- raise a signal;
- challenge evidence;
- question a decision;
- stop unsafe execution;
- contest a metric;
- correct memory;
- or appeal an automated determination.

Contestability does not imply that every challenge must prevail.

### 13.6 Proportionality

Controls should match:

- consequence;
- autonomy;
- uncertainty;
- scale;
- reversibility;
- and the organisation’s ability to detect and repair failure.

### 13.7 Rights, Law and Culture

The model does not resolve:

- jurisdiction-specific authority;
- privacy;
- employment power;
- consent;
- retention;
- discrimination;
- or sector regulation.

Application requires local legal, cultural and rights interpretation.

### 13.8 Responsible Exercise Is Not a Second Construct

Project OI retains one Organisational Intelligence construct.

It distinguishes three practical states:

| Capability | Governance | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Weak | Responsible intention | Responsible aspiration without sufficient OI capability |
| Strong | Irresponsible | Instrumentally capable but irresponsibly governed |
| Strong | Responsible | Capable and responsibly exercised under stated conditions |

The phrase **Responsible Organisational Intelligence** may be used descriptively.

It should not become a second competing construct or score. For consequential practical use, however, a capability finding should normally be reported together with responsible-exercise evidence rather than presented in isolation.

## 14. Power, Representation and Organisational Reality

Formal process models can conceal who shapes what the organisation is able to know and do.

### 14.1 Power Shapes Attention

Actors with greater power may determine:

- which signal is visible;
- which issue is urgent;
- and which concern is ignored.

### 14.2 Power Shapes Context

Official context may omit:

- marginalised experience;
- frontline conditions;
- or externalised harm.

### 14.3 Power Shapes Knowledge

Expertise can be discounted because of:

- status;
- role;
- identity;
- or organisational interest.

### 14.4 Power Shapes Decisions

Real authority may sit with:

- a founder;
- gatekeeper;
- interface default;
- model score;
- or approval bottleneck

rather than the formal decision-maker.

### 14.5 Power Shapes Evaluation

The owner of an intervention may control:

- the question;
- metric;
- population;
- evaluator;
- and publication of findings.

### 14.6 Power Shapes Memory

Official memory can become:

- selective;
- sanitised;
- or aligned with present identity.

### 14.7 Power Shapes Adaptation

Capabilities tied to:

- budgets;
- status;
- careers;
- and identity

may persist despite contrary evidence.

### 14.8 Representation Requirement

Responsible OI should therefore ask:

```text
Whose signal can enter?
Whose context is represented?
Whose expertise is recognised?
Who may decide?
Who bears the consequence?
Who can contest?
Who defines the lesson?
Who gains or loses capability?
```

Power is a cross-cutting condition. Cross-boundary knowledge may require transfer, translation or transformation rather than neutral information exchange.[^26]

It is not fully solved by transparency or participation alone.

The current source base is weighted toward mainstream organisation, strategy, information-processing and capability research. It underrepresents critical organisation theory, labour-process analysis, stakeholder theory, institutional power and rights-based perspectives. This is a material limitation and a priority post-BOK research stream.

## 15. Authority and Accountability Across the OI Cycle

Authority changes form across the cycle.

| Stage | Primary authority concern |
|---|---|
| Sense | Right and responsibility to raise, route, prioritise or suppress signals |
| Contextualise | Access, source authority, permission and purpose limitation |
| Know | Knowledge authority and expertise relevance |
| Choose | Decision rights, approval, veto and delegation |
| Mobilise — coordination | Role, dependency, resource and escalation authority |
| Mobilise — execution | Action, override, stop, recovery and compensation authority |
| Outcome and evaluation | Evaluative independence, access and judgement authority |
| Learn | Memory stewardship, lesson ownership, correction and supersession |
| Adapt | Capability ownership, resource reallocation, reconfiguration and retirement authority |

### 15.1 Authority Does Not Travel Automatically

Knowledge authority does not confer decision authority.

Decision authority does not confer technical expertise.

Action authority does not confer the right to redefine purpose.

Evaluation authority does not confer the right to rewrite historical evidence.

Model capability does not confer any organisational authority by default.

### 15.2 Accountability Relationship

For a material episode, the organisation should be able to identify:

- who or what acted;
- who authorised;
- which forum may question;
- what evidence is available;
- and what correction or consequence may follow.

### 15.3 Cross-Stage Accountability

The system should preserve accountability through transitions.

An organisation should not lose the decision owner when the work enters a workflow, or lose the acting principal when an AI agent calls a tool.

## 16. Human–AI Organisational Intelligence

AI is treated as a participant in the organisational system.

It is not a separate source of Organisational Intelligence.

The Organisational Intelligence construct applies fully to organisations that use no AI. AI is one contingent participant whose capabilities and risks must be integrated into the same organisational model.

### 16.1 AI Participation Across the Cycle

| OI position | Possible AI contribution |
|---|---|
| Sense | detect patterns, classify signals, route attention |
| Contextualise | retrieve and assemble purpose-bound context |
| Know | synthesise sources, identify expertise, generate hypotheses |
| Choose | generate options, predictions, recommendations and challenge |
| Mobilise — coordination | plan, assign, schedule and monitor dependencies |
| Mobilise — execution | use tools and perform bounded actions |
| Outcome and evaluation | analyse evidence, detect subgroup effects and test explanations |
| Learn | retrieve memory, draft lessons and identify recurrence |
| Adapt | propose capability changes and support experimentation |

### 16.2 Organisational Integration Requirements

AI contributes to OI only where its participation is governed as part of the organisation’s risk-management lifecycle rather than treated as an isolated model feature.[^28]

Its participation should be integrated with:

- an acting principal;
- a stable identity where consequential;
- explicit role;
- data and context boundaries;
- tool and action authority;
- source and provenance;
- human expertise;
- review and contestability;
- operational state;
- memory governance;
- outcome evaluation;
- and lifecycle ownership.

### 16.3 AI agent Execution

For consequential AI agent work, the organisation should define an **AI agent execution envelope** containing:

```text
Objective
Acting principal
AI agent identity
Permitted tools and data
Authority scope
Budget and time
Current state
Required evidence
Prohibited actions
Approval and stop conditions
Recovery
```

This remains an implementation heuristic in the extended glossary, not a core OI construct or product contract.

### 16.4 AI Memory

AI memory is not organisational memory by itself.

It should preserve:

- source;
- user or acting unit;
- time;
- transformation;
- confidence;
- permission;
- scope;
- and supersession.

Persistent recall does not create authority.

### 16.5 Human Oversight

“Human in the loop” is discouraged because it hides:

- which human;
- at which stage;
- with what expertise;
- with which evidence;
- under which authority;
- and with what ability to alter the result.

### 16.6 Capability Effects

Field evidence shows that generative AI can improve performance and distribute some expert-derived patterns in specific settings, while benefits and failure boundaries vary by task, worker and deployment design.[^29][^30][^31]

AI can:

- augment capability;
- distribute expert-derived patterns;
- substitute for routine work;
- recombine expertise;
- and increase capacity.

It can also:

- erode skill;
- narrow variation;
- increase opacity;
- create new oversight load;
- and shift capability to a provider.

### 16.7 Borrowed Capability

**Borrowed Capability** is retained as a Project OI diagnostic working term, not a core construct.

It identifies performance materially enabled by an external provider, model, expert or platform that the organisation cannot yet reproduce, govern or sustain independently.

The term should be compared continually with:

- outsourcing;
- ecosystem capability;
- resource dependence;
- externalised competence;
- and platform dependence.

It remains a diagnostic lens rather than a proven distinct construct.

### 16.8 Capability Opacity

**Capability Opacity** is retained as a diagnostic risk term.

It concerns inability to explain adequately:

- how the capability performs;
- where it depends;
- why it fails;
- or what would be required to reproduce or exit it.

Legitimate abstraction is not automatically opacity. The term overlaps with explainability, transparency, dependency visibility and technological opacity and remains a provisional diagnostic lens.

### 16.9 Capability Sovereignty

**Capability Sovereignty** is narrowed to a governance lens rather than a constitutive OI concept.

It asks whether the organisation retains sufficient control over critical:

- purpose;
- authority;
- continuity;
- evidence;
- portability;
- and exit.

It does not imply that every capability should be internal. It should be challenged against strategic autonomy, resilience, resource dependence, portability and exit rights rather than treated as an established independent construct.

### 16.10 AI Evidence Expiry

Every material AI claim should preserve:

```text
Task and domain
Model and version
Provider
Interface and tools
Context and data conditions
Human role
Authority mode
Study or deployment setting
Date
Known limitations
Review trigger
```

AI evidence should be marked:

- current;
- qualified;
- superseded;
- contradicted;
- or expired.

### 16.11 Responsible Human–AI Principle

> **AI contributes to Organisational Intelligence only where its outputs and actions remain organisationally situated, attributable, authorised, contestable, evaluable and connected to the organisation’s memory, capability and responsibility.**

## 17. Relationship to Adjacent Constructs

Project OI does not claim to have invented the term Organisational Intelligence or all of its components.


### 17.1 Explicit Organisational Intelligence Lineage

Project OI does not originate the idea that organisations can possess intelligence beyond the sum of individual intelligence.

The explicit Organisational Intelligence tradition includes several close predecessors.

- **Wilensky** treats intelligence as an institutional capacity for obtaining, analysing and using knowledge under conditions shaped by hierarchy, ideology, expert advice, secrecy and the treatment of warning voices.[^32]
- **Matsuda** distinguishes intelligence as both product and process and explicitly integrates human and machine intelligence, organisational memory, cognition, learning, communication, inference and coordination.[^33]
- **Halal** links organisational knowledge creation to strategic adaptation through a wider system of structure, culture, stakeholders and knowledge assets.[^34]
- **Albrecht** presents OI as mobilised collective capacity directed toward mission through shared purpose, alignment, knowledge deployment and willingness to change.[^35]
- **Yolles** connects OI to viable systems, self-diagnosis, learning, adaptation and organisational pathology.[^36]
- **Akgün, Byrne and Keskin** treat OI as recursively produced through actors, rules and resources rather than as a static organisational possession.[^37]
- **Choo** links sensemaking, knowledge creation and decision-making in the Knowing Organisation.[^5]
- **Kolbjørnsrud** explicitly includes human and digital actors in an AI-era organisational design account.[^38]

Project OI therefore does not claim originality for:

- organisation-level intelligence;
- distributed human and machine participation;
- knowledge creation and application;
- organisational memory and learning;
- mission-directed mobilisation;
- adaptation;
- or emergence through interacting organisational capabilities.

The bounded potential contribution of Project OI is the integration and operationalisation of:

1. a governed end-to-end orientation–response–feedback cycle;
2. source, evidence and provenance integrity;
3. explicit separation of knowledge, decision and action authority;
4. outcome-to-capability traceability;
5. persistent and governed human–AI organisational memory;
6. transition-centred research through Connection Quality;
7. and proportionate operationalisation for SMBs and other non-enterprise settings.

These are contribution hypotheses, not established novelty claims.

### 17.2 Adjacent Constructs

| Adjacent construct | Primary focus | Boundary from Project OI |
|---|---|---|
| Individual intelligence | Cognitive capacity of a person | OI is an emergent organisation-level capacity |
| Collective intelligence | Group performance across tasks | OI adds persistence, institutionalisation, authority, memory and feedback-coupled response |
| Organisational information processing | Fit between uncertainty and information-processing capacity | OI extends through knowledge, authorised response, learning and adaptation |
| Sensemaking | Construction of meaning under ambiguity | Sensemaking contributes to Orient but does not include the complete response and improvement loop |
| Knowing Organisation | Sensemaking, knowledge creation and decision | OI extends through coordination, execution, outcome feedback and capability change |
| Knowledge management | Management of knowledge assets and flows | OI requires knowledge to influence response and future capability |
| Organisational memory | Retention and retrieval across time | Memory supports the loop but can preserve obsolete or harmful patterns |
| Organisational learning | Change in organisationally available understanding or response | OI also includes forming orientation and coordinating present response |
| Dynamic capabilities | Modification and reconfiguration of capabilities | Dynamic capability contributes to Adapt; OI also includes ordinary sensing, knowing, choosing and mobilisation |
| Organisational resilience | Ability to withstand, recover or extend under disruption | Resilience is one quality or capability; OI also includes orientation, authority, evaluation and learning.[^27] |
| Organisational effectiveness | Achievement of valued outcomes | OI is a repeatable capacity and can produce poor outcomes under uncertainty |
| Business intelligence | Data analysis supporting decisions | OI extends into context, authority, action, feedback and adaptation |
| Digital maturity | Organisational use of digital technology | Technology maturity is an enabling condition, not proof of OI |
| AI capability | Model or system performance | OI depends on organisational integration, authority, outcome and learning |
| Culture | Shared meanings and norms | Culture conditions OI but does not demonstrate the complete construct |
| General adaptability | Ability to change | Adaptation is one operational capability and may be incoherent or irresponsible |

### 17.3 Distinctive Integrative Gap

The potential Project OI contribution is not one novel component.

It is the explicit connection of:

```text
Orientation
→ Authorised collective response
→ Outcome feedback
→ Persistent learning
→ Capability change
```

with traceability across the transitions.

### 17.4 Novelty Boundary

Project OI should describe the current release as a literature-grounded working synthesis, proposed construct and operational research programme. It should not claim a novel general theory until empirical work demonstrates:

- distinctiveness;
- predictive value;
- and useful explanatory power

beyond adjacent constructs.

## 18. Integrated Higher-Order Proposition Architecture

The 182 chapter propositions remain the traceability base.

Chapter 10 adopts eleven higher-order working propositions.

They are not validated organisational laws.

### OI-IP-001 — Persistent Organisation-Level Capacity

> **Organisational Intelligence exists where the capacity to orient, coordinate and improve is instantiated through an organisational system sufficiently persistent to operate beyond one participant, tool or isolated episode.**

**Type:** Constitutive proposition  
**Primary basis:** Organisation level, persistence, institutionalisation and multilevel emergence

### OI-IP-002 — Selective Materiality

> **Organisational Intelligence begins with selective organisational attention; information quantity, visibility or notification volume cannot substitute for the capability to recognise, qualify and route material signals.**

**Type:** Mechanism proposition  
**Primary basis:** Sense and attention structures

### OI-IP-003 — Evidence-Bearing Orientation

> **Useful organisational orientation requires context, knowledge and expertise combined in a form that preserves relevant source, time, authority, uncertainty, disagreement and representation limits.**

**Type:** Mechanism and epistemic proposition  
**Primary basis:** Contextualise and Know

### OI-IP-004 — Authorised Commitment

> **Orientation influences organisational response only where it is converted into an attributable commitment under explicit decision rights, relevant evidence and appropriate contestability.**

**Type:** Mechanism and governance proposition  
**Primary basis:** Choose

### OI-IP-005 — Mobilisation and Operational Fidelity

> **A decision contributes to Organisational Intelligence only where its purpose and authority survive translation into coordinated dependencies and reliable execution under actual operating conditions.**

**Type:** Mechanism proposition  
**Primary basis:** Mobilise, coordination and execution

### OI-IP-006 — Consequence and Evaluative Feedback

> **Activity, completion and output do not establish outcome; credible improvement requires observation and evaluation of material consequences, implementation, stakeholder distribution, alternative explanation and measurement integrity.**

**Type:** Mechanism and epistemic proposition  
**Primary basis:** Outcome–feedback bridge

### OI-IP-007 — Persistent Learning

> **Feedback becomes organisational learning only where reflection changes organisationally available understanding or future response and that change remains retrievable and influential beyond the immediate individual or episode.**

**Type:** Mechanism proposition  
**Primary basis:** Learn

### OI-IP-008 — Capability and Coherent Adaptation

> **Learning closes the Organisational Intelligence loop only where it is translated into durable capability or an evidence-linked decision to preserve, renew, combine, retire or reconfigure capability coherently.**

**Type:** Mechanism proposition  
**Primary basis:** Adapt

### OI-IP-009 — Connection Quality

> **Project OI hypothesises that the complete organisation-level capacity depends materially on the integrity of the conversions among its capabilities; strong local capability may not compensate reliably for broken, delayed, unauthorised or unreconstructable transitions.**

**Type:** Integrative working proposition  
**Primary basis:** Cross-chapter transition clusters

### OI-IP-010 — Responsible Exercise

> **Instrumental Organisational Intelligence can be exercised irresponsibly; responsible exercise requires proportionate epistemic quality, legitimate authority, accountability, contestable purpose, affected-interest representation and appropriate human and technical control.**

**Type:** Normative governance proposition  
**Primary basis:** Responsible exercise statement and V7 review set

### OI-IP-011 — Organisationally Integrated AI

> **AI contributes to Organisational Intelligence only where model capability is integrated with organisational identity, context, knowledge, authority, workflow, operational state, evidence, human capability, outcome evaluation, memory, continuity and exit.**

**Type:** Technology-sensitive integrative proposition  
**Primary basis:** AI-era proposition set

### 18.1 Status of the Integrated Set

The set is intended to be:

- parsimonious enough for the final theory;
- traceable to the 182 source propositions;
- and challengeable in OI-BOK-REVIEW-001.

The full register remains authoritative for:

- evidence;
- local scope;
- AI qualification;
- and unresolved validation.

## 19. Evidence Architecture

### 19.1 Bounded Claims

A material OI claim should state:

```text
Focal organisational unit
Purpose or class of response
Time period
Operating conditions
Relevant stakeholders
OI capability or transformation claimed
Evidence portfolio
Outcome and risk evidence
Known limitations
Human–AI involvement
Responsible-use conditions
```

Avoid:

> This company has high Organisational Intelligence.

Prefer:

> Over the previous twelve months, the customer-operations unit demonstrated a repeatable capacity to identify recurring billing anomalies, assemble cross-system context, authorise corrective response, coordinate refund and defect work, evaluate customer and operational outcomes, and revise future controls across three observed episodes.

### 19.2 Four Claim Types

#### Capability Claim

Evidence that one operational capability is enacted under stated conditions.

#### Transition Claim

Evidence that one capability’s output is accepted and made usable by the next.

#### Complete OI Claim

Evidence across:

- Orient;
- Coordinate;
- Improve;
- and feedback closure over time.

#### Responsible-Exercise Claim

Evidence concerning:

- purpose;
- authority;
- accountability;
- rights;
- contestability;
- control;
- and distribution of consequence.

### 19.3 Eight Evidence Roles

A candidate evidence portfolio contains:

1. **Enabling-condition evidence**  
   Relevant structures, resources, roles and access exist.

2. **Process or control evidence**  
   The capability mechanism is enacted.

3. **Transition evidence**  
   Inputs are converted and accepted by the next capability.

4. **Governance evidence**  
   Purpose, authority, accountability and contestability are present.

5. **Direct capability-behaviour evidence**  
   The organisation performs the relevant behaviour under stated conditions.

6. **Outcome evidence**  
   Material consequences are observed across stakeholders and time.

7. **Risk-signal evidence**  
   Weakness, debt, drift and failure remain visible.

8. **Learning and adaptation evidence**  
   Feedback changes future understanding or capability and persists.

### 19.4 Minimum Portfolio Principle

No one evidence role is sufficient.

A stage-specific claim should normally include:

- direct behaviour;
- process or control;
- governance;
- and risk evidence.

A transition claim requires:

- evidence from the sending stage;
- explicit transition evidence;
- and evidence of receiving acceptance or use.

A complete OI claim requires:

- evidence from all three transformations;
- transition evidence;
- outcome evidence;
- learning or adaptation persistence;
- and governance evidence.

### 19.5 Direct Evidence Versus Enablers

Examples of enabling conditions:

- psychological safety;
- data access;
- technology;
- clear roles;
- slack;
- and leadership support.

They may strengthen OI.

They do not prove that the capability operates.

### 19.6 Outcome Evidence Versus Capability

Positive outcome is relevant but insufficient.

The organisation may succeed because of:

- favourable conditions;
- luck;
- market power;
- or unsustainable effort.

### 19.7 No Single Composite Score

Project OI does not endorse one OI score at this stage.

A score would require:

- validated constructs;
- reliable measures;
- known weighting;
- discriminant validity;
- and evidence that aggregation does not conceal a critical transition failure.

A profile, evidence narrative or episode trace is more defensible for the current research stage.

### 19.8 Indicator Reactivity

Any assessment system may change the organisation it measures.

Project OI indicators should therefore be reviewed for:

- gaming;
- goal displacement;
- proxy collapse;
- and burden.

## 23. Positive Integrated Example

A small manufacturer receives several unusual customer complaints about a product batch.

### 23.1 Sense

The organisation detects that the complaints:

- share a pattern;
- exceed ordinary variation;
- and warrant attention.

The signal is not lost because complaints arrive through different channels.

### 23.2 Contextualise

The complaints are connected to:

- batch;
- supplier;
- production date;
- customer type;
- prior exceptions;
- and current inventory.

The organisation preserves:

- source;
- time;
- uncertainty;
- and affected scope.

### 23.3 Know

Frontline customer knowledge, production expertise, supplier history and quality data are brought together.

The organisation identifies:

- what is known;
- what is inferred;
- and which expertise is still required.

### 23.4 Orientation

A sufficiently aligned view forms:

> A supplier-material variation may have created a product defect affecting one batch, but root cause and full customer scope remain uncertain.

### 23.5 Choose

An authorised decision is made to:

- isolate remaining stock;
- contact potentially affected customers;
- pause use of the material;
- and begin supplier and production investigation.

Decision rights, rationale and review conditions are recorded.

### 23.6 Mobilise — Coordination

The organisation establishes:

- customer-contact ownership;
- inventory isolation;
- production tasks;
- supplier engagement;
- and escalation.

Dependencies and handoffs remain visible.

### 23.7 Mobilise — Execution

Stock is isolated.

Customers are contacted.

Tests are performed.

External effects are confirmed.

Exceptions and recovery are recorded.

### 23.8 Outcome and Evaluation

The organisation observes:

- customer impact;
- defect rate;
- response timeliness;
- cost;
- supplier findings;
- and unintended production delay.

It distinguishes output from customer and quality outcomes.

### 23.9 Learn

The review identifies:

- a material-inspection gap;
- a misleading quality proxy;
- and an undocumented supplier exception.

The lesson preserves evidence and applicability.

### 23.10 Adapt

The organisation:

- revises supplier controls;
- changes inspection capability;
- trains relevant staff in the new routine;
- updates authority;
- and retires the old exception path.

### 23.11 Why This Demonstrates the Complete Construct

The example contains:

- orientation;
- authorised collective response;
- consequence;
- learning;
- and changed future capability.

It also preserves:

- authority;
- evidence;
- affected stakeholders;
- and feedback closure.

The organisation produces excellent analysis but cannot:

- decide;
- authorise;

The complete construct is weak.

The organisation automates an obsolete process and performs it reliably.

Coordinate is effective relative to a weak orientation and purpose.

A poorly reasoned response succeeds because of favourable external conditions.

Outcome is positive.

OI evidence is weak.

The organisation appears intelligent while capability remains concentrated and non-persistent.

Lessons are documented and searchable.

- authority;
- acting principal;
- memory;

are unclear.

The system is instrumentally capable and irresponsibly governed.

The organisation coordinates efficiently and adapts quickly while:

- externalising harm;
- suppressing dissent;

The organisation possesses advanced:

- analytics;

## 25. Construct-Validity and Falsification Expectations

A definition alone cannot establish a construct. Construct validity requires evidence concerning convergence, discrimination, prediction and the interpretations attached to measures.[^22]

### 25.1 Convergent Relationships

OI should correlate positively, but not perfectly, with:

- collective intelligence;[^23]
- organisational learning;
- absorptive capacity;
- knowledge integration;
- decision quality;
- coordination quality;
- dynamic capabilities;
- resilience;
- and adaptive performance.

### 25.2 Discriminant Relationships

OI should remain distinguishable from:

- organisational size;
- profitability;
- employee engagement;
- technology maturity;
- data volume;
- leadership charisma;
- innovation output;
- and general organisational effectiveness.

### 25.3 Predictive Expectations

Higher OI should predict over time, under comparable conditions:

- fewer repeated preventable failures;
- faster appropriate response to material change;
- more reliable cross-boundary coordination;
- stronger retention and transfer of learning;
- better revision of failed assumptions;
- and improved future response under changed conditions.

These are hypotheses.

### 25.4 Connection-Quality Expectation

Connection Quality should explain some system-level difference beyond local capability evidence.

For example:

- two organisations may possess similar expertise;
- but differ in whether that expertise reaches decisions and future learning.

This requires empirical testing.

### 25.5 Evidence Against the Construct

Project OI should be revised materially if research shows that:

- the three transformations do not provide useful discriminant structure;
- the six operational capabilities add no explanatory or practical value beyond adjacent models;
- transition evidence does not predict or explain organisational response;
- the construct cannot be distinguished from general organisational effectiveness;
- bounded OI claims cannot be assessed more reliably than subjective global impressions;
- or the model performs poorly across SMB and non-enterprise settings.

### 25.6 Evidence Against Necessity

The model should also be challenged by organisations that:

- repeatedly form appropriate response and improve without one or more proposed capabilities;
- or possess all proposed capabilities without demonstrating the integrated capacity.

### 25.7 No Confirmation by Illustration

A plausible case example supports understanding.

It does not validate the theory.

**Disposition:** Retain chapter-scoped distinctions:

- Context Decay concerns loss of current usefulness;
- Context Drift concerns changed surrounding conditions;
- Context Laundering concerns authority inflation through reuse;
- Memory Decay concerns reduced retrievability or influence;
- Memory Drift concerns divergence of retained representation;
- Memory Contamination concerns compromised retained content.

### 27.4 Borrowed Capability

**Disposition:** Retain as a working diagnostic term, not a core construct.

### 27.5 Capability Sovereignty

**Disposition:** Retain as a narrow governance lens concerning critical control, continuity, evidence, portability and exit.

### 27.6 Capability Opacity

**Disposition:** Retain as a diagnostic risk term.

### 27.7 Capability Maturity

**Disposition:** Retain only as a cautious descriptive term.

No universal linear maturity model is implied.

### 27.8 Unlearning

**Disposition:** Retain as a working term for deliberate release or replacement of governing assumptions or response patterns.

It remains distinct from:

- forgetting;
- supersession;
- deletion;
- and capability retirement.

### 27.9 Learning About Learning

**Disposition:** Retain as a learning-process qualifier.

Do not introduce a separate seventh capability or rely on the ambiguous term triple-loop learning.

### 27.10 Collective Response and Collective Action

**Disposition:** Retain both.

- **Collective Response** is the higher-order deliberate organisational response shaped by orientation and authority.
- **Collective Action** is the connected and mutually adjusted contribution through which response may be enacted.

### 27.11 Organisational System and Sociotechnical System

**Disposition:** Use Organisational System for the focal construct-level unit and Sociotechnical System for the implementation perspective.

### 27.12 Responsible and Instrumental OI

**Disposition:** Retain one OI construct.

Use:

- instrumentally capable;
- responsibly exercised;
- or irresponsibly governed

as descriptors.

Do not create separate Responsible OI and Instrumental OI scores.

## 28. Limitations of the Current BOK Synthesis

### 28.1 Literature-Grounded, Not Field-Validated

All 182 source propositions still require field and SMB validation.

### 28.2 Construct Breadth

OI spans several established disciplines.

The integrated construct may still be too broad, and its bounded contribution relative to explicit OI predecessors remains to be demonstrated empirically.

### 28.3 Level of Analysis

The source literature moves among:

- individuals;
- teams;
- organisations;
- technical systems;
- and ecosystems.

The multilevel model remains provisional.

### 28.4 Causal Mechanism

The feedback-coupled mechanism is plausible.

Its temporal and causal structure is not proven.

### 28.5 Generalisability

Much of the evidence comes from:

- large firms;
- software teams;
- healthcare;
- high-reliability operations;
- and Western organisational research.

### 28.6 Measurement

The evidence portfolio is not a validated assessment instrument.

### 28.7 Sense Quality

The Sense quality profile is Chapter-10 synthesis because Chapter 1 lacked an explicit source quality section.

### 28.8 Normative Boundary

Responsible exercise is defined normatively.

Its detailed application varies across:

- culture;
- law;
- sector;
- and affected rights.

### 28.9 Power

The capability model may underrepresent:

- coercion;
- structural inequality;
- identity;
- and political control.

### 28.10 Tacit Knowledge

No representation can capture all:

- situated;
- embodied;
- and relational knowledge.

### 28.11 Memory Reconstruction

Organisational memory cannot be historically neutral.

### 28.12 Capability Boundary

Capability remains a contested construct, and maturity language risks circularity.

### 28.13 HRO and Normal-Accident Tension

The BOK retains unresolved tension between organising for high reliability and the limits imposed by interactive complexity and tight coupling.

### 28.14 Outcomes and Causality

Organisational outcomes are multi-causal and difficult to attribute.

### 28.15 AI Currency

AI evidence can expire rapidly.

The chapter’s AI conclusions require continuous review.

### 28.16 Human–AI Dependence

Current evidence does not yet establish when AI reliably augments, substitutes or erodes organisation-level capability over time.

### 28.17 Product Independence

The BOK was developed alongside a product programme.

The deferred architecture reconciliation reduces but does not eliminate product bias.

### 28.18 Chapter Asymmetry

Later chapters contain more granular definitions and failure modes partly because the research process became more detailed over time.

### 28.19 No Single Universal Purpose

The construct can describe instrumentally capable organisations pursuing different and potentially conflicting purposes.

### 28.20 No Final Novelty Claim

Project OI currently claims:

- a coherent working synthesis;
- an operational capability model;
- an explicit transition architecture;
- and a responsible human–AI interpretation.

It does not yet claim a novel general theory.

## 29. Minimum Post-BOK Empirical Programme

### 29.1 Comparative Organisational Case Studies

Study contrasting organisations or units with:

- similar environments;
- different response patterns;
- and traceable episodes.

Objectives:

- test construct distinctiveness;
- identify missing mechanisms;
- and examine contextual variation.

### 29.2 Longitudinal Episode Tracing

Trace complete episodes from:

```text
signal
→ orientation
→ decision
→ mobilisation
→ outcome
→ learning
→ capability change
```

This is essential for testing:

- loop closure;
- persistence;
- and transition integrity.

### 29.3 Transition and Handoff Studies

Study where locally capable units fail to convert:

- attention into context;
- knowledge into decision;
- decision into execution;
- and feedback into capability.

This is the primary empirical test of Connection Quality.

### 29.4 Measurement Research

Develop and test:

- bounded evidence portfolios;
- indicator validity;
- multi-source corroboration;
- inter-rater reliability;
- and resistance to gaming.

Do not begin with one composite score.

### 29.5 SMB Field Tests

Use episodes such as:

- customer complaint;
- supplier failure;
- pricing change;
- employee departure;
- operational incident;
- AI-assisted workflow;
- and repeated service defect.

Test whether the model is:

- understandable;
- proportionate;
- discriminating;
- and practically useful

without enterprise bureaucracy.

### 29.6 Human–AI Comparative Studies

Compare:

- human-only;
- AI-assisted;
- AI agent-mediated;
- and mixed arrangements

on:

- outcome;
- authority;
- learning;
- capability;
- recovery;
- and dependence.

### 29.7 Counterexample and Falsification Studies

Actively search for:

- effective organisations not explained by the model;
- high-scoring local capabilities with weak OI;
- and cases where transitions add no explanatory value.

### 29.8 Responsible-Use Studies

Examine:

- power;
- voice;
- rights;
- legitimacy;
- distributional outcome;
- and contestability

in materially different cultural and legal settings.

## 31. Current Project OI Position

Project OI’s current position is:

> **Organisational Intelligence is best understood as a persistent organisation-level capacity for forming orientation, coordinating collective response and improving future response through feedback.**

This capacity is:

- organisational rather than merely individual;
- persistent rather than episodic;
- collective rather than private;
- feedback-coupled rather than static;
- instantiated through sociotechnical and relational arrangements;
- dependent on connection integrity;
- and not reducible to one person, model, system, repository or outcome.

The complete current architecture is:

```text
ORIENT
        ↓
COORDINATE
        ↓
IMPROVE
```

supported operationally by:

```text
SENSE
        ↓
CONTEXTUALISE
        ↓
KNOW
        ↓
ORIENTATION
        ↓
CHOOSE
        ↓
MOBILISE
        ↓
OUTCOME–FEEDBACK
        ↓
LEARN
        ↓
ADAPT
```

The responsible exercise of this capacity requires:

- proportionate epistemic quality;
- legitimate authority;
- accountability;
- contestable purpose;
- representation of affected interests;
- applicable rights and law;
- and human and technical controls proportionate to risk.

The plausible Project OI contribution is:

1. a parsimonious organisation-level construct definition;
2. a six-capability operational model;
3. an explicit account of contextualisation and outcome feedback;
4. a transition-centred account of Connection Quality;
5. a traceable proposition, diagnostic and evidence architecture;
6. and a responsible human–AI interpretation that treats model capability as one organisational element rather than the intelligence of the organisation.

This is a coherent working synthesis.

It is not yet a validated general theory.

## 34. Editorial Guardrail

> **Project OI must not confuse information, analysis, intelligence-looking technology, coordinated activity, favourable outcome, learning rhetoric or change volume with Organisational Intelligence. The construct is demonstrated only where a persistent organisational system can form orientation, coordinate deliberate collective response and improve future response through credible feedback, while the responsible exercise of that capacity preserves epistemic integrity, legitimate authority, accountability, contestability, affected interests and proportionate control.**

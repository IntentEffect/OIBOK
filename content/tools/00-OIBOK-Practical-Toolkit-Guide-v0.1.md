## Using the practical toolkit

The Organisation Intelligence Body of Knowledge (OIBOK) examines how organisations orient, coordinate and improve. Use this toolkit to investigate a problem, design an improvement and examine what changes. Start with one recurring decision or workflow. The intended output is an informed action and a record of its effects.

Version 0.1 provides methods for piloting in practice. The worksheets are grounded in OIBOK's working synthesis and relevant evaluation guidance. They do not provide a validated intelligence score or a guarantee of improvement.

| Resource | Use it to | Suggested session |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organisational Intelligence Practice Review | Examine evidence and choose a problem to investigate. Includes the Context and Knowledge Map and Decision and Coordination Canvas. | 90 minutes |
| AI Opportunity and Work Design Canvas | Compare options and specify a bounded intervention when AI is being considered. | 90 minutes |
| Outcome and Learning Record | Plan the evaluation before the change, review results and assign changes to working practice. | 30 minutes to plan, 45 to review and 15 for a later check |

### Prepare and complete

- Choose a workflow owner, a facilitator and people who do and depend on the work. Bring contrasting cases, current guidance and available outcome data.
- Copy the blank worksheet pages into a working document. Replace bracketed prompts, add rows or extend response spaces as needed. Keep the worked examples separate.
- Carry one case ID through the tools. Link source records in your existing authorised workspace. Record observations, interpretations, unknowns and disagreements distinctly.
- Complete the evaluation plan before starting the change. If the intervention contains no AI, move directly from the Practice Review to the Outcome and Learning Record.
- Use the findings to continue, adapt, stop or defer the intervention. Check later whether the changes to knowledge and working practice persist.

Orient, Coordinate and Improve are connected organising ideas. You can revisit them as understanding changes. They do not require every organisation to follow a fixed linear sequence.

## Evidence and chapter guide

An observation records what a source shows. An interpretation explains what it might mean. An unknown identifies what still needs checking. Give each important statement a source reference, scope, date and limitation. Use Not applicable with a reason when a prompt does not fit.

Work with contrasting and failed cases as well as successful ones. Retain dissent and alternative explanations. A small convenience sample can identify a question to investigate; it cannot establish an organisation-wide rate or causal effect.

### The shared worked example

Harbour Services is fictional. Its case identifiers, records, counts and results are invented for instruction. The example follows one complaint workflow across all three tools. Its numerical targets are local example decisions, not benchmarks. The later persistence review remains pending.

### Improving the tools

Record the tool version, workflow type, what was useful or confusing, missing prompts, changes made and observed consequences. Share an authorised, de-identified account through OIBOK's established contribution channel when available. Field feedback can improve the methods; a successful example alone does not validate the whole model.

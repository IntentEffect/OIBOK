## Using the outcome and learning record

Use this record to decide whether a change helped, what else may explain the result, and what the organisation should retain or change. Complete the evaluation plan before introducing the change. Return after the agreed observation window and again to check whether learning persists.

### People and preparation

Include the person accountable for the service or workflow, someone doing the work, the evidence owner and a facilitator. Bring the Practice Review, the proposed intervention, case records, existing measures and relevant complaints or exceptions. Where possible, use a facilitator who is independent of the proposal sponsor.

### Suggested sessions

- Before the intervention, allow 30 minutes to complete the evaluation plan and measures. Agree the eligible cases, comparison, collection responsibilities and decision rules.
- After the observation window, allow 45 minutes to review outcomes and explanations, make a decision, and assign changes to knowledge and working practices.
- Set a later 15 minute check to examine whether the changes are still used, including when a different person handles the work.

### How to record evidence

Separate observed results, interpretation and unknowns. Record source locations, dates, units, denominators and missing observations. Label estimates as estimates. Keep the original evaluation plan and explain later changes. A comparison can strengthen interpretation, but differences in case mix, staffing or other changes may remain.

### Output and next step

Produce a recorded continue, adapt, stop or gather more evidence decision, with an accountable owner and dated learning actions. Link it back to the Practice Review and, where relevant, the AI Opportunity and Work Design Canvas. Reopen either tool when the evidence changes the problem or intervention.

## Harbour Services plan and results

Fictional worked example. Every organisation, record and result is illustrative. Case HS-01 links PR-HS-01, AI-HS-01 and this evaluation OL-HS-01. The Service Operations Lead owns the decision.

### Plan agreed before the pilot

The package combines a current policy source, explicit decision authority, minimum handoff fields and AI-assisted retrieval and drafting. An adviser checks the source and approves every reply. AI has no authority to send messages, approve refunds or take external action.

Baseline sources B1 case audit, B2 timestamps and B3 staff walkthroughs cover 100 standard visit complaints over four weeks. All cases were followed through closure and seven calendar days. Legal disputes, specialist vulnerable-customer cases and unusual compensation are excluded.

Forty new eligible complaints enter a two-week pilot: 20 receive the package and 20 receive the existing process. Allocation alternates within issue and severity categories, with exceptions recorded. It is neither randomised nor blinded. Follow-up is complete for all 40 cases.

Example targets are median resolution at or below 3.5 business days and repeat contact at or below 20%. In intervention cases, final policy errors, unauthorised actions and confidentiality incidents must be zero. Any such incident pauses that use for owner review. These thresholds are local example choices.

### Illustrative observations

| Measure | Baseline 100 | Comparison 20 | Package 20 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Median resolution in business days | 4.8 | 4.4 | 3.1 |
| Repeat contact within seven days | 28/100 | 6/20 | 4/20 |
| Median human handling in minutes | 38 | 36 | 30 |
| Final policy errors | 8/100 | 1/20 | 0/20 |
| Unauthorised actions | 0/100 | 0/20 | 0/20 |

Repeat contact is measured within seven calendar days of closure. Handling includes checking and correction. No confidentiality incidents were observed in either pilot group. Pilot case audits and timestamps are fictional example evidence.

## Harbour Services decision and learning

Fictional worked example. OL-HS-01 records the following interpretation and decision after the complete pilot observation window.

### Interpretation and limits

The package meets the example time and repeat-contact targets: 3.1 business days and 4/20 cases, or 20%. Its observed outcomes are better than the concurrent comparison, but the small, unblinded sample, non-random allocation, case mix and staff spillover limit attribution. The pilot does not isolate AI from the policy, authority and handoff changes.

AI drafts contained unsupported policy claims in 3/20 package cases. Review caught all before sending: one used a superseded policy and two misapplied exception wording. These are draft defects, separate from final customer-facing policy errors. Zero final errors in 20 cases does not establish reliability.

### Decision and conditions

ADAPT AND EXTEND a limited pilot after correcting version filtering and exception handling, retesting and confirming named reviewers. The Service Operations Lead retains the decision. AI remains unable to send or approve actions. The comparison policy error also requires normal service correction and owner review.

| Action | Owner | Due | Completion evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Correct current-version selection | Policy owner | Before restart | Approved source and version rules |
| Retest retrieval and exceptions | Systems lead | Before restart | Recorded typical and exception tests |
| Update review and handoff guidance | Team lead | Before restart | Revised guidance and adviser walkthrough |
| Review next 20 package cases | Analyst | Next pilot review | Counts, outcomes and draft-defect log |
| Examine causes of cancelled visits | Service owner | Next pilot review | Cause review and assigned service actions |

At week 8, the Service Operations Lead will check later cases with another adviser, confirm that the revised policy and authority are usable, and examine whether outcomes persist. This check is pending; no sustained improvement is claimed.

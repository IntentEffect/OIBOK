## Facilitator guide

Use this review to understand one recurring decision or workflow. The output is a documented problem, evidence and uncertainty, and an improvement with an owner and review date. This is a proposed practice resource for field testing; it does not produce a validated intelligence or maturity score.

### Prepare and run the review

Invite someone who performs the work, the decision owner, a knowledge or policy owner, and affected teams. Include systems and analysis support when relevant. Where possible, use a facilitator independent of the sponsor. Bring three recent cases, including a successful case and an exception, with source references and dates. Remove unnecessary personal or confidential details.

Allow 15 minutes for scope and evidence, 20 for context and knowledge, 25 for decisions and coordination, 20 for competing explanations and priorities, and 10 to agree ownership and follow-up. Capture disagreement. Ask what evidence could change each conclusion.

### Record evidence clearly

Separate observed evidence, interpretation and unknowns. Identify each source, date, sample and limitation. A reported experience is evidence of that person's account; it does not by itself establish frequency or cause. Mark irrelevant fields not applicable and explain why. Add rows or response space as needed.

## Harbour Services review findings

Fictional worked example. Harbour Services, its records and all figures are invented for illustration. They are not evidence that OIBOK has been validated.

Case HS-01; review PR-HS-01. Decision owner: Service Operations Lead. Participants: front-line adviser, team leader, policy owner, systems lead and analyst. A team member independent of the sponsor facilitates.

Scope: standard complaints about delayed or cancelled visits, from receipt to closure and seven-day follow-up. Exclusions: legal disputes, specialist vulnerable-customer cases and unusual compensation. Intended outcome: resolve complaints accurately with fewer delays and repeat contacts.

### Evidence and uncertainty

| Source | Observed evidence | Limit or interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B1 Case audit | 100 eligible baseline cases over four weeks: repeat contacts 28/100 within seven days of closure; incomplete handoffs 32/100; customer-facing policy errors 8/100; unauthorised actions 0/100. | All cases followed through closure and seven days. Counts describe this period; they do not identify the causes. |
| B2 Timestamps | Median resolution 4.8 business days. Median total human handling, including review, 38 minutes. | Delays may reflect approvals, workload or case mix. |
| B3 Walkthroughs | Staff describe inconsistent policy access, unclear refund authority and context lost between teams. | Reported experience. Frequency and relative contribution need investigation. |

## Harbour Services improvement decision

Fictional worked example. Case HS-01; review PR-HS-01. This page records the proposed improvement before pilot results are available.

### Decision and coordination canvas

| Decision or handoff | Who decides and acts | Required context | Exception |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Interpret policy | Policy owner maintains source; adviser checks version. | Current policy and relevant case facts. | Conflicting or missing policy goes to supervisor. |
| Authorise response | Named authorised person decides; adviser records rationale. | Explicit authority and compensation limits. | Specialist cases leave the standard workflow. |
| Send and close | Adviser approves each reply and records outcome. | Case history, source reference and minimum handoff fields. | AI cannot send, approve refunds or act externally. |

### Competing explanations

Outdated sources could explain policy errors; unclear approval authority could explain waiting. Inadequate handoffs, workload, case mix and skills could also contribute. Walkthroughs support investigation, but do not establish which explanation dominates. Compare current practice, process and knowledge changes without AI, and the combined package.

### Priorities and responsibility

| Action | Owner | Due |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Establish one current policy source with ownership and version selection. | Policy owner | Before pilot |
| Clarify authority and minimum handoff context. | Team leader | Before pilot |
| Test retrieval and draft assistance, human review, escalation and fallback. | Systems lead | Before pilot |

Decision: prepare a limited combined pilot of policy access, authority and handoff improvements with AI retrieval and draft assistance. Confirm company requirements with their owners. Use de-identified typical and exception cases before live use. Missing or conflicting policy requires abstention and escalation.

Handoff: AI-HS-01 records the intervention and alternatives. OL-HS-01 records baseline, comparison, quality limits and evaluation. The Service Operations Lead owns the decision. The analyst owns the pilot review; a week 8 retention review remains pending and will check use by another adviser.

The review identifies a testable intervention. It makes no AI-only effectiveness claim or organisation-wide intelligence assessment.

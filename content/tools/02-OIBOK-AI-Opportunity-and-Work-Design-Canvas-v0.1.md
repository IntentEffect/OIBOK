## Using the canvas

Use this canvas to decide whether AI is a useful part of an improvement to one workflow or recurring decision. Define the work, compare alternatives, agree responsibilities and set an evaluation plan before introducing the change.

### Who should participate

Bring the person accountable for the outcome, people who do the work, the relevant knowledge or policy owner, a systems representative and someone who can assess outcomes. Invite affected users where practical. Use a facilitator who can question the sponsor's preferred solution.

### Prepare

- Bring the Practice Review, two or three recent cases, current process and policy sources, known constraints and baseline measures.
- Use approved records with only the information needed. Link to controlled sources instead of copying confidential records into this worksheet.
- Record sources and dates. Separate observed evidence, interpretation, assumptions and unknowns. Mark a field not applicable with a reason when appropriate.

### Run the session

- Spend 20 minutes defining the outcome and comparing the current approach, a change without AI and an AI-assisted option.
- Spend 30 minutes describing the work, required context, human authority and exception handling.
- Spend 25 to 40 minutes agreeing a bounded pilot, measures, stop rules and the decision owner. Assign each unresolved dependency an owner and date.

### Leave with a decision

Choose a limited AI pilot, a change without AI, further investigation or no change. Record the reasons, conditions and next review. Carry the proposal ID, baseline, measures and decision rules into the Outcome and Learning Record.

### Research connection and limits

The canvas connects Chapters 2 and 3 on context and knowledge, 4 on authority, 5 and 6 on coordination and reliable execution, 7 on evaluation, and 10 on responsible integration. It is a proposed practice aid. Completion does not establish model validity, compliance or permission to deploy.

## Example scope and work design

Fictional worked example. Harbour Services and all records, counts and decisions in this example are invented for illustration.

Case HS-01 | Review PR-HS-01 | Proposal AI-HS-01 | Evaluation OL-HS-01. Decision owner: Service Operations Lead. Participants: front-line adviser, team leader, policy owner, systems lead and analyst.

### Problem and evidence

Standard complaints about delayed or cancelled visits take too long to resolve. The scope runs from receipt to closure and seven-day follow-up. Legal disputes, specialist vulnerable-customer cases and unusual compensation are excluded.

In 100 fully followed baseline cases over four weeks, median resolution was 4.8 business days, repeat contacts were 28/100 and customer-facing policy errors were 8/100. Median human handling, including review, was 38 minutes. B1 case audit, B2 timestamps and B3 staff walkthroughs indicate inconsistent policy access, unclear refund authority and incomplete handoffs. Workload, case mix and training remain alternative explanations.

| Option | Benefit to test | Effort | Main dependency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Current approach | Reference for comparison | Existing handling and rework | Consistent case records |
| Knowledge and process changes | Fewer policy and handoff errors | Policy ownership, authority and training | Adoption by advisers |
| Full package including AI | Faster retrieval and drafting with sound decisions | Above plus integration, review and correction | Current sources and human checks |

### Selected design

Test one current policy source with an owner and version, explicit decision authority, minimum handoff fields and AI-assisted retrieval and drafting. A named adviser checks the cited source, version and proposed response before every external reply. AI cannot send, approve refunds, change policy or take external action.

Missing or conflicting policy triggers abstention and supervisor escalation. Handoffs preserve the issue, relevant evidence, policy source, decision rationale and responsible owner. Use de-identified records for preparation and the company's permitted access and record-handling arrangements during the pilot.

## Example pilot conditions and decision

Fictional worked example. These are proposed pilot conditions, not completed test evidence or demonstrated benefits.

### Trial design

Use 40 eligible new complaints received in the same two-week window: 20 with the full intervention package and 20 with the existing process. Allocate alternately within issue and severity categories and record exceptions. Follow every case to closure plus seven days. Allocation is neither randomised nor blinded. Small numbers and possible staff learning across groups limit conclusions.

| Measure | Baseline | Agreed target | Evidence owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Median resolution | 4.8 business days | At most 3.5 business days | Analyst using timestamps |
| Repeat contact within seven days of closure | 28/100 | At most 20 percent | Analyst using case audit |
| Final customer-facing policy errors | 8/100 | Zero in intervention cases | Policy owner using reply audit |
| Total human handling including review | 38 minutes median | Track benefit and correction effort | Analyst using work records |

Record unsupported draft claims separately from errors reaching customers. Record unauthorised actions and confidentiality incidents, with zero allowed in the intervention. The targets are chosen for this fictional case, not benchmarks.

### Conditions before use

The systems lead and policy owner test typical and exception cases, source version filtering, permissions, abstention and supervisor fallback. The team leader confirms named reviewers and records training. Company data and security requirements remain with their accountable owners.

Any final customer-facing policy error, unauthorised action or confidentiality incident pauses the affected pilot use. The Service Operations Lead reviews the incident and authorises restart only after remediation and retesting. The established manual process provides fallback.

### Decision and next record

Proceed to a limited pilot only when the listed conditions have evidence and owners. Record the decision date before the first live case. Transfer the measures and criteria to OL-HS-01. The pilot assesses the whole package; it cannot isolate AI's contribution from the accompanying organisational changes.

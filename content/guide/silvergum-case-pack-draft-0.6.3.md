## How to use this case pack

Follow one inquiry through completed records, then use the separate blank worksheets for your own work. This pack accompanies the [OIBOK Guide (draft 0.6.3)](https://oibok.org/guide/read/) and [Working Companion (draft 0.6.3)](https://oibok.org/guide/companion/). The Guide tells the story in Appendix A4 and examines the cross-team decision in Chapter 8. The records below make the reasoning, handoffs and unresolved questions inspectable.

**Every organisation, person, source, observation and decision in this pack is fictional.** Evidence IDs name invented teaching records; they are not links to a real audit or supporting dataset. The pack expands the Silvergum case introduced in Guide 0.4. Added administrative details show how the tools can be completed. No participant has approved these records in real life, and no field validation is claimed.

The completed Practice Review and Outcome and Learning Record follow Practical Toolkit 0.1. Supplementary diagnostic, exception and cross-team records follow the Working Companion. These are readable completed examples, not replacements for the five blank fillable A3 canvases. Timings and thresholds are choices made for this fictional situation, not OIBOK benchmarks.

## 1 Case index and sequence

**Case SG-01: routine Coast installation jobs from customer agreement to booking, followed for seven calendar days for scope-related rebooking.** The Coast service owner is accountable. Sales, scheduling and the customer coordinator contribute. The coordinator retains the customer booking promise while a job is returned or held. Emergency call-outs and specialist engineering jobs are outside the trial.

| Record | Purpose and relationship |
| --- | --- |
| SG-01 | Practice Review and common case identifier. Contains the Context and Knowledge Map and Decision and Coordination Canvas. |
| DG-SG-01 | Diagnostic reasoning from the baseline, linked to SG-01. |
| OL-SG-01 | Evaluation plan, first decision, amended plan and later review. |
| EX-SG-01 | Late-amendment exception discovered during the first trial. |
| EX-SG-02 | Week-eight ownership transfer and access correction. |
| CT-SG-01 | Director's decision about what Coast and Ranges should share. |

The baseline covers the twenty consecutive eligible jobs immediately before the change. Revision 1 of SG-01 and OL-SG-01 is recorded before the first trial. The first trial covers the next twenty eligible jobs; its review waits until every job completes seven-day follow-up. EX-SG-01 occurs within that trial. Revision 2 records the adaptation before another twenty jobs enter the later-use window. EX-SG-02 occurs at the start of week eight, before the relief lead handles those later cases independently. The later review follows completion of all twenty follow-ups. CT-SG-01 uses the Coast reviews and a separate fifteen-job Ranges inquiry. A further Coast review is scheduled one month after the later review.

These are relative teaching dates. Intake and review dates would be actual calendar dates in a live record. The example does not imply a particular weekly arrival rate.

## 2 Completed Practice Review SG-01

Use the [blank Practice Review](https://oibok.org/tools/practice-review/) for a real inquiry. The following entries cover each of its substantive worksheet fields.

### Scope and evidence worksheet

**Review identity.** SG-01 revision 1, recorded before the first trial. Organisation: fictional Silvergum Field Services, Coast. Decision owner: Coast service owner. Facilitator: an improvement colleague who does not own the proposed AI purchase. Participants: sales lead, scheduling lead and customer coordinator. The sales lead owns quote-source checks; the scheduling lead owns queue evidence. Role titles stand in for named people in this teaching case.

**Work and intended outcome.** Help routine installation customers receive a booking based on their current agreed scope, without repeated clarification or avoidable scope rebooking. Start at customer agreement; end at booking, with a seven-calendar-day outcome check. Customers, sales, schedulers and crews are affected. Existing pricing and specialist-approval limits stay in force. A scope check cannot create crew capacity.

**Evidence and uncertainty.** SG-E01 exposes the difference between an approved sale and a packet the receiver can act on. SG-E02 establishes eight returns in twenty jobs and two crew waits among first-time acceptances. SG-E03 shows that the current authorised revision is not consistently available to scheduling. This supports investigating the handoff. It does not establish how much elapsed delay is caused by missing scope, or rule out workload and crew constraints. The baseline and trial counts are preserved in OL-SG-01.

### Context and Knowledge Map

**Signals and shared understanding.** Repeat customer calls draw attention to the delay. Sales treats “approved” as customer agreement. Scheduling needs a current scope, authority to use it and enough detail to assign work. The team separates customer agreement, scheduling acceptance and booking. A packet being sent does not mean it has been accepted. A receiver's acceptance applies to the identified scope revision; an amendment reopens that acceptance.

| Knowledge needed | Source, owner and action |
| --- | --- |
| Current authorised scope | Customer agreement and identified quote revision, checked by sales. Link the original authorised record before sending to scheduling; a copied summary cannot settle which revision was agreed. |
| Information needed to schedule | Queue instructions owned by the scheduling lead. Make the required detail and return reasons visible before trial intake. Do not add fields without establishing how the receiver uses them. |
| Customer booking promise | Customer-contact history owned by the coordinator. Check the promise and update the customer while the job is held. A returned packet does not transfer this responsibility to an unassigned queue. |
| Approval and reopen authority | Service-owner delegation and role permissions. Verify the current named holder and fallback before independent use; test both during handover. |
| Crew availability | Scheduling queue and roster, owned by scheduling. Keep capacity waiting separate from scope returns; take unresolved capacity questions to the service owner. |

**Knowledge actions.** Before trial intake, sales links the authorised source and scheduling publishes the acceptance instruction in the queue. The coordinator verifies access to the booking-promise history. Before the later-use window, scheduling replaces the duplicate-entry instruction with revision-linked acceptance and a reopen step. At handover, the duty manager corrects access and records delegation. At the later review, the source owner retires the old folder copy and verifies the queue link. Each action has an owner and an observable completion check in SG-E05 to SG-E08.

### Decision and Coordination Canvas

| Decision or handoff | Authority, completion and recovery |
| --- | --- |
| Identify agreed scope | Sales links the current customer-authorised revision. Completion requires an identifiable source, not a statement that the sale is approved. An unresolved amendment remains with a named salesperson for clarification. |
| Accept for scheduling | The scheduler accepts the identified revision or returns it with a reason and named sales recipient. Sales acknowledges the return. The coordinator continues to own the booking promise. |
| Confirm a booking | Scheduling checks accepted scope and available crew. A specialist requirement is held for the relevant approval. A booking against an unapproved revision triggers the agreed pause and service-owner review. |
| Handle an amendment | The case is held and reopened; sales obtains the current authorisation; the receiver accepts again before booking. During the week-eight access gap, the relief lead uses the duty manager's delegated reopen route. |
| Authorise a local change | The service owner decides trial, adaptation and local continuation using OL-SG-01. A change to pricing, the advertised service promise or another branch's process goes to the relevant higher authority. |

**Rules and rationale.** This fictional service owner's delegation permits a reversible local acceptance trial within existing approval limits. Acceptance is an event attached to a revision and a receiver. Changes to the underlying scope invalidate reliance on the old acceptance. Queue history records who held, reopened, authorised and accepted the job. People can challenge an interpretation by bringing the source revision to the service owner; disagreement does not authorise booking a disputed scope.

**Coordination gap to investigate.** SG-E02 links five returns to missing current authorised scope. The other three returns need their own reasons; crew waits need a separate account. Sales and scheduling jointly inspect the returned histories before trial intake. The service owner retains unresolved capacity questions instead of silently attributing them to the scope process.

### Improvement and handoff worksheet

**Competing explanations.** Missing usable scope may cause returns; crew constraints may cause waiting after acceptance; copying and later amendment may create a new mismatch. Staff skill or workload could also contribute, but the available record does not quantify their contribution. DG-SG-01 records what would distinguish these explanations.

**Prioritised improvement.** Trial explicit scope identification, receiver acceptance or a reasoned return, and continuing ownership of the customer promise. Sales and scheduling prepare the change before the next twenty eligible jobs. An AI email summary is deferred: the immediate need is to establish which revision was authorised, and a plausible summary would not establish that authority.

**Outcomes and continuing learning.** Use OL-SG-01 for baseline, first trial and later use. Count jobs returned at least once and scope rebookings within seven days; retain sales preparation time and unapproved-revision bookings. Inspect the exception and handover records, not just the totals. The service owner schedules the later review and a further monthly check.

**Decision and next records.** Proceed with the bounded first trial after the evaluation plan and source/acceptance checks are in place. The service owner owns the decision; sales and scheduling own implementation. Link OL-SG-01. An AI canvas is not applicable because no AI design or pilot is being taken forward. Reopen SG-01 if the trial exposes a different problem, authority boundary or affected group.

## 3 Completed diagnostic record DG-SG-01

**Episode.** A customer agrees a revised quote, but the receiving scheduler sees the first version and cannot safely book the revised work. Compare the timely Monday job and the twenty-job baseline before classifying the failure.

| Explanation | Supporting evidence and next discriminating check |
| --- | --- |
| Handoff failure | Five baseline returns lack current authorised scope. Inspect whether explicit source identification and receiver acceptance reduce these returns. A complete packet still waiting for a crew challenges a scope-only explanation. |
| Evidence failure | Sales and scheduling cannot consistently establish which revision was authorised. Trace agreement to the original revision; do not treat a generated summary as an authority record. |
| Capacity or coordination failure | Two first-time acceptances wait for a crew. Inspect roster and queue timing separately. A complete scope check would not remove a genuine capacity constraint. |
| Persistence failure | At week eight, the relief lead can view the queue but cannot reopen an accepted job. A demonstration after the access correction tests operability; subsequent live cases test use beyond the rehearsal. |

**Decision.** The service owner initially tests the handoff and evidence explanation, while retaining capacity as an unresolved contributor. The later amendment and handover add recovery and persistence checks. These classifications organise inquiry; they are neither scores nor proof that every failure class is present.

## 4 Completed Outcome and Learning Record OL-SG-01

Use the [blank Outcome and Learning Record](https://oibok.org/tools/outcome-and-learning/) for your own evaluation. Preserve the plan made before results are known.

### Evaluation plan

**Record and responsibility.** Case SG-01, evaluation OL-SG-01 revision 1, before first-trial intake. Decision owner: Coast service owner. Facilitator: improvement colleague. Evidence owners: scheduler for queue events, sales lead for preparation log and coordinator for rebooking follow-up. Link the Practice Review SG-01; AI canvas not applicable because AI is deferred.

**Change and expected effect.** Sales identifies the authorised scope, scheduling explicitly accepts or returns it, and the coordinator retains the customer promise. This should reduce avoidable returns and scope rebooking. The original trial also copies scope into a second field. That implementation choice creates extra work and amendment risk and is removed in revision 2. No observed effect is attributed to AI.

**Eligible cases and window.** The unit is one routine Coast installation job. Take twenty consecutive eligible jobs for baseline and each trial window. Follow each job for seven calendar days after booking. Exclude emergency and specialist jobs under the same rules throughout; if a specialist need is discovered, hold for approval and record the exclusion and reason. Record every included job and any missing follow-up. Do not replace a missing observation with a favourable one. All three fictional groups complete follow-up; no missing observation is imputed.

**Comparison and other changes.** Compare successive groups in the same service, without random allocation or a concurrent control. The scheduling lead logs demand, staffing and scope complexity. First-trial duplication changes effort; the later period uses the revised process and a relief lead. Fewer complex amendments in the later period are a rival explanation. The small groups cannot isolate a causal effect or establish an expected rate for another branch.

### Measures and decision rules

| Measure and collection | Rule recorded before the first trial |
| --- | --- |
| Primary outcome: jobs rebooked for inaccurate scope within seven calendar days of booking. Coordinator checks all eligible jobs against booking history. | Compare with baseline 4/20. Fewer returns alone do not establish improved customer outcomes. Unchanged rebooking requires investigation and a reasoned adaptation or further-evidence decision. |
| Process measure: jobs returned at least once for clarification. Scheduler counts each job once and retains each reason. | Compare with baseline 8/20. Do not treat repeated returns on one job as extra jobs or combine this measure with Ranges elapsed-delay counts. |
| Effort guardrail: median sales preparation minutes per eligible job, including scope-entry work. Sales logs each job. | Adapt if the median exceeds six minutes, even if returns fall. Baseline is four minutes. Scheduling, coordination and crew effort are not quantified here; total savings cannot be claimed. |
| Authority guardrail: bookings made against an unapproved revision. Scheduler inspects all eligible bookings; coordinator reports discovered discrepancies. | Pause on any such booking; service owner reviews correction and restart conditions. A caught mismatch is still logged, but does not satisfy the booking-based pause trigger. |

**Missing or ambiguous evidence.** Keep the eligible-job denominator, identify missing fields and postpone an outcome decision when follow-up is incomplete. A disputed scope-rebooking classification is reviewed by the service owner against the record and kept visible. These are procedural examples; this fictional pack contains complete follow-up and no asserted statistical significance.

### Results and alternative explanations

| Measure | Baseline | First trial | Later use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Eligible jobs with seven-day follow-up | 20/20 | 20/20 | 20/20 |
| Jobs returned at least once | 8 | 3 | 2 |
| Jobs rebooked for inaccurate scope | 4 | 4 | 1 |
| Median sales preparation time | 4 min | 8 min | 6 min |
| Bookings against unapproved revisions | 0 | 0 | 0 |

First trial: five fewer jobs returned, no change in scope rebookings and four additional median preparation minutes. These descriptive differences do not establish causation. The copied field requires transcription and becomes stale after a late amendment. SG-E04 and EX-SG-01 preserve the caught mismatch. No booking crosses the pause trigger. A zero observed count does not establish absence of risk.

Later use: two returns, one scope rebooking and six median preparation minutes, with all follow-up complete. SG-E07 records the relief lead handling two amendments through reopening and second acceptance. The later group also contains fewer complex amendments. The original lead's absence and the live cases support a limited persistence judgement; they do not separate the effects of the revised form, staff and job mix.

### Decisions and rationale

**First review: adapt.** The service owner removes duplicate transcription, links the original authorised scope and requires reopening plus receiver reacceptance after an amendment. This responds to the effort rule and exception evidence. Scheduling updates the instruction, sales removes the duplicate field, and both rehearse the amendment. SG-E05 records the inspected history and permission for twenty more routine jobs. The first-trial results remain visible; revision 2 does not rewrite the original plan.

**Later review: continue within Coast's routine installation work.** The service owner accepts continued local use with the same authority guardrail and a monthly review. This is a bounded operational decision under uncertainty. It does not approve specialist work, a company-wide rollout or a claim of total labour savings. Any unapproved-revision booking pauses use immediately; a rise in seven-day rebooking at the next monthly review reopens the inquiry.

### Knowledge and working practices to update

The scheduling lead owns the revised queue instruction and acceptance/reopen sequence. The sales lead owns removal of duplicate scope entry. The duty manager records delegated reopening and corrects the relief role's access. The source owner retires the obsolete shared-folder instruction and verifies the current queue link. The coordinator keeps the customer-promise responsibility visible while jobs are held. SG-E05 to SG-E08 record completion checks.

### Check that learning persists

At week eight, use EX-SG-02 to test access, authority, recovery and receiver acceptance before independent work. At the later review, inspect all twenty job histories and the two live amendments, with the original lead absent. One month later, the service owner will review the next completed seven-day cohort, state its actual size, check scope rebooking and inspect an amendment handled by a different available colleague. That future check is planned, not represented as completed. Reopen on a guardrail breach, increased rebooking, lost access or inability to apply the current instruction.

### Record history and feedback

Revision 1: baseline, first plan and limits, recorded before intake. Revision 2: first results, adaptation and retest conditions, recorded after full first-window follow-up and before later intake. Revision 3: later results, bounded continuation and next review, recorded after later follow-up. The service owner records each decision; evidence owners preserve earlier entries. All sources in this published example are invented. In a live record, store customer material only in its authorised workspace and share de-identified learning only with permission.

## 5 Completed exception and ownership records

### EX-SG-01 Late amendment

**Trigger and evidence.** During the first trial a customer amendment leaves copied scope text out of date. The scheduler catches the mismatch before booking; SG-E04 links the competing revisions. This is a near miss under the booking-based pause rule, not a completed unapproved booking.

**Holding owner and customer responsibility.** Scheduling holds the job. A named salesperson obtains the current authorisation. The coordinator retains the booking promise and keeps the customer informed. The handoff back to sales records the reason and acknowledgement.

**Decision and authority.** The service owner approves adaptation after the first review. The revised process links the original authorised scope and reopens an accepted job when scope changes. Scheduling must accept the new revision before booking. Specialist approval remains a separate requirement.

**Recovery evidence.** Sales and scheduling rehearse the late-amendment path. The service owner inspects the amended job history and approves the later twenty-job window. OL-SG-01 retains both the exception and first-trial costs. A real unapproved booking would instead trigger the pause and a specific restart decision.

### EX-SG-02 Ownership transfer at week eight

**Trigger and outgoing record.** The original scheduling lead hands over to the relief lead. SG-E05 identifies the current instruction, the acceptance/reopen sequence and unresolved limits. The handover includes a late-amendment rehearsal, not just receipt of the document.

**Gap found.** The relief lead can view the queue but lacks reopen permission. They hold the rehearsal job and contact the duty manager with delegated reopen authority. Viewing a record has not proved ability to carry out the decision.

**Correction and acceptance.** The duty manager corrects role-based access and records the delegation. The relief lead demonstrates reopening and reacceptance before independent handling of the next affected job. SG-E06 records the demonstration and acknowledged ownership. The coordinator continues to hold the customer promise during real exceptions.

**Later evidence.** With the original lead absent, the relief lead handles two live amendments in SG-E07. Histories show authorised revisions, second acceptances and bookings. The relief lead also identifies an obsolete instruction; the source owner retires it and checks the queue link in SG-E08. This is evidence of use and correction in the later window. The next monthly review remains necessary.

## 6 Completed cross-team decision CT-SG-01

**Question and authority.** Should the operations director mandate one scope form across Coast and Ranges? The director owns common definitions, the service offer and resource decisions. Each service owner retains authority for bounded local practice within that delegation.

**Evidence brought.** Coast brings SG-E02, OL-SG-01 and its later limits. Ranges brings RG-E01, a delayed and a timely job, route calendar and customer promise. Coast counts clarification returns; Ranges counts waits over two working days. Coast says “accepted” at customer agreement, while Ranges uses the word for scheduling acceptance. The director does not combine 8/20 returns and 6/15 delays into an overall failure rate.

| Decision | Owner, condition and next evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Share event definitions | Director, after both receivers check actual histories. Record customer agreement, scheduling acceptance and booking separately, with return or planned-wait reasons. Ask an unfamiliar colleague to interpret one history from each team at the next review. |
| Continue Coast's local response | Coast service owner. Retain scope-linked acceptance, reopening and OL-SG-01 guardrails. Review the next completed cohort one month after the later review. |
| Investigate Ranges' service promise | Ranges service manager. Check whether route windows were accurately explained and whether the advertised promise fits the remote-service pattern. Preserve the four planned waits and two clarification waits as separate reasons. |
| Escalate service or capacity changes | Operations director. A changed promise or fleet requirement needs a decision on service and resources; a form cannot resolve it. Bring customer consequences and resource options before approval. |

**Recorded decision.** Adopt common event definitions and authorise two local responses. Do not mandate Coast's form for Ranges on these data. The first cross-team review occurs after both teams have completed their local evidence review; the director confirms the actual date with the owners. A local outcome window must not be shortened merely to meet that meeting.

**Evidence that would change the decision.** Comparable scope failures recurring in both teams could justify a shared acceptance requirement, subject to local receiver tests. Evidence that route-window expectations drive Ranges dissatisfaction would support a service-promise decision. Increased effort, unapproved scope or inability to use the current instruction would reopen the relevant local design. Coast's counts cannot establish the economics of a remote-service run.

## 7 What to transfer into your own review

Carry the case ID, source references, decision boundary and authority into each next record. Preserve the original plan, contrary evidence, corrections and unresolved questions. Replace every fictional owner, date and threshold with a justified entry for your work. Mark an unknown as unknown and assign a way to resolve it. The purpose is a usable decision record, not completion of every box for its own sake.

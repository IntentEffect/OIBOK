# The OIBOK Guide

**DRAFT · For practitioner review**  
**Guide 0.4 · 24 November 2026**

The OIBOK Guide helps leaders, practitioners and teams apply Organisational Intelligence to real work. Use it to investigate a recurring problem, examine AI proposals, clarify responsibilities, evaluate a change and retain useful learning.

This is the complete illustrated draft Guide, accompanied by a Working Companion with preparation prompts, connected records and a practitioner feedback record. Nine draft diagrams connect the concepts to practical work. The Guide, Companion and diagrams are published for review and improvement by the OIBOK community.

**New in 0.4.** Use Chapter 2A to distinguish seven kinds of failure. Follow a delayed quote through a trial, an exception and a change of owner in Appendix A4. Chapter 8 uses the same fictional company to show what two teams should share and what each should decide locally.

## What the Guide covers

The Guide begins with a practical question: which recurring decision or workflow would benefit from a more coherent organisational response? It then connects the OIBOK research to the work of investigation, design, evaluation and learning.

| Guide chapter | Practical focus |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Understand organisational intelligence | Use Orient, Coordinate and Improve, and the six capabilities, to examine organisational work. |
| 2 Start with a worthwhile problem | Define a useful boundary, bring together relevant perspectives and examine evidence. |
| 2A Diagnose what is breaking down | Trace the episode, compare competing explanations and choose evidence that can distinguish them. |
| 3 Understand the work and its knowledge | Identify the signals, context, sources and expertise that people need. |
| 4 Clarify decisions coordination and execution | Make authority, commitments, dependencies, handoffs and exceptions explicit. |
| 5 Design how people and AI work together | Compare options and define any AI contribution, its limits and people’s responsibilities. |
| 6 Evaluate the change | Plan the comparison, assess consequences and examine competing explanations. |
| 7 Turn experience into lasting capability | Retain learning and return to later work to examine whether the change lasts. |
| 8 Tailor and extend the approach | Apply the approach proportionately and identify what needs further inquiry. |

The appendices provide four clearly labelled fictional examples, facilitation guidance, chapter and tool mappings, and the source record. The Working Companion includes editable sheets for diagnosis, ownership transfer and decisions across teams, alongside the practitioner feedback record.

## Start with one piece of work

Choose one recurring decision or workflow, bring evidence from contrasting cases and use the [Practice Review](/tools/practice-review/) to understand what happens. Reuse existing records where they serve the purpose. For a selected change, connect the review to an [Outcome and Learning Record](/tools/outcome-and-learning/).

Use the [AI Opportunity and Work Design Canvas](/tools/ai-opportunity-and-work-design/) when taking an AI option forward into work design or a pilot. The Guide also supports improvements that do not involve AI. The [Practical Toolkit](/tools/) includes five fillable A3 canvases.

## Draft status and evidence boundaries

Guide 0.4 and Working Companion 0.4 are **drafts for practitioner review**. External practitioner review, walkthroughs, field application and independent validation remain pending. Their publication invites feedback; it does not indicate that these activities have been completed.

The Guide is based on **OIBOK release 1.0** and **Practical Toolkit 0.1**. It is a practice companion to that literature-grounded working synthesis. Publishing this draft does not create a new research release or change the status of the existing chapters or canvases.

The examples are fictional. The Guide provides no certification, validated Organisational Intelligence score or assurance of compliance with another standard. Its preparation and material AI assistance are disclosed in the Guide’s publication status section.

## Help improve the draft

Feedback is welcome from people who read the Guide, facilitate a walkthrough or try the methods in a suitable piece of organisational work. Tell us which version and section you used, what was clear or difficult, what you observed and what change you suggest. Distinguish observations from interpretations and include relevant sources where possible.

Use the feedback record in the Working Companion, email [info@oibok.org](mailto:info@oibok.org), or [open a GitHub issue](https://github.com/IntentEffect/OIBOK/issues). Follow the [contribution guidance](/contribute/), and keep confidential organisational information in its authorised workspace.

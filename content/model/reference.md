# OIBOK model reference

**DRAFT 0.2 · For practitioner review · 23 November 2026.**

This is the complete readable companion to the [interactive OIBOK model](/model/). It includes every selectable area, practical question and resource link. It is published for feedback and remains subject to revision.

The visual is based on OIBOK research release 1.0, a literature-grounded working synthesis. It does not amend the research release or establish independent field validation of the complete model. The examples below are generic illustrations, and the suggested evidence is not a validated assessment checklist.

## How the model is organised

| Transformation | Operational capabilities | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Orient | Sense and Know | Form an organisational orientation from relevant signals, experience, context and knowledge. |
| Coordinate | Choose and Mobilise | Convert orientation into authorised commitment and reliable collective response. |
| Improve | Learn and Adapt | Use evaluated consequences to strengthen or preserve future understanding and response. |

These transformations can overlap, recur and operate across organisational boundaries. Feedback from Improve returns to both Orient and Coordinate. Contextualise connects Sense and Know and is revisited throughout. Organisational orientation is an integrated state; the Outcome–Feedback bridge connects action to learning. Organisational memory supplies prior context and retains current learning across all three transformations.

Purpose, governance and responsible exercise apply across the model. People and AI contributions are considered within the same organisational authority and accountability arrangements; AI participation is optional. The six capabilities have equal status. [Chapter 10](/knowledge/integrated-and-responsible-organisational-intelligence/) provides the governing definitions, research argument and limitations.

## Sense

*Operational capability · Orient*

Notice, capture, recognise, qualify and route material signals and experience for organisational attention.

**Core work**

- Notice relevant changes, weak signals, complaints and experience.
- Qualify source, timing, uncertainty and possible significance.
- Route the signal to people or roles able to investigate or respond.

**What to look for**

- A traceable signal, its context and a reason it matters.
- A recipient, an acknowledgement and a route for exceptions.
- Evidence that missing or underrepresented signals can be raised.

**People.** Judge materiality, contribute lived experience and challenge what receives attention.

**Possible AI contribution.** Detect patterns, classify incoming information and assist routing within an agreed scope.

**Boundary.** Alert volume does not establish effective sensing. Automated prioritisation needs a route for challenge.

**Illustrative breakdown.** Alerts increase, but an important problem still fails to reach anyone able to act.

**Practical question.** Which signals are being missed, suppressed or routed to the wrong place?

## Know

*Operational capability · Orient*

Create, validate, locate, combine and apply relevant knowledge and expertise in context.

**Core work**

- Find relevant sources and people with applicable expertise.
- Validate currency, provenance, scope and disagreements.
- Combine explicit information with experience to support this situation.

**What to look for**

- Sources and expertise connected to the actual work.
- Current versions, applicability checks and unresolved uncertainty.
- A route to correct an answer or seek additional expertise.

**People.** Interpret circumstances, supply expertise and distinguish evidence from inference.

**Possible AI contribution.** Retrieve and synthesise sources, suggest expertise and generate hypotheses for checking.

**Boundary.** A fluent answer or successful retrieval does not establish truth, applicable expertise or decision authority.

**Illustrative breakdown.** A plausible answer is used without checking whether its policy or source applies to this case.

**Practical question.** What do we need to know, and how do we know it is applicable here?

## Choose

*Operational capability · Coordinate*

Frame a response, evaluate alternatives and make an attributable, authorised commitment under uncertainty.

**Core work**

- Frame the problem, purpose, constraints and credible alternatives.
- Consider trade-offs, uncertainty, dissent and who bears consequences.
- Commit, delay, monitor, experiment, escalate or deliberately take no action.

**What to look for**

- An identifiable decision owner and legitimate authority.
- The decision, rationale, conditions and unresolved uncertainty.
- A review trigger and a route for challenge or escalation.

**People.** Exercise decision rights, assess alternatives and own the organisational commitment.

**Possible AI contribution.** Generate options, predictions, recommendations and challenges to the reasoning.

**Boundary.** Model capability confers no organisational authority by default. Any delegation needs an explicit scope.

**Illustrative breakdown.** A recommendation is treated as approval, with no accountable decision owner.

**Practical question.** Who may commit the organisation, to what, under which conditions?

## Mobilise

*Operational capability · Coordinate*

Translate an authorised commitment into coordinated, governed and reliable collective action and execution.

**Core work**

- Agree responsibilities, dependencies, resources and accepted handoffs.
- Execute permitted actions using the current operational state.
- Detect exceptions, escalate, stop, recover and verify what happened.

**What to look for**

- Accepted responsibilities and handoffs that recipients can use.
- Action records, dependencies, state and reliable completion evidence.
- Defined exception, override, stop and recovery arrangements.

**People.** Accept responsibility, coordinate across boundaries and exercise action and recovery authority.

**Possible AI contribution.** Support planning and dependency monitoring; perform tool actions inside a defined execution envelope.

**Boundary.** Specify the acting principal, tools, data, authority, limits, evidence, approval and stop conditions, and recovery.

**Illustrative breakdown.** Work is marked complete while the receiving team cannot act or an exception remains unresolved.

**Practical question.** Can the commitment be carried through reliably, including when something goes wrong?

## Learn

*Operational capability · Improve*

Revise organisationally available understanding, memory, routines and future response through credible feedback, reflection and inquiry.

**Core work**

- Interpret evaluated consequences and compare them with expectations.
- Examine rival explanations, exceptions and decision-time context.
- Make warranted lessons available for subsequent decisions and work.

**What to look for**

- A scoped lesson linked to observations and its limits.
- Named stewardship, correction and supersession arrangements.
- Later evidence that relevant work can access and use the learning.

**People.** Interpret consequences, contest the account, examine alternatives and steward organisational memory.

**Possible AI contribution.** Analyse records, detect recurrence, retrieve previous experience and draft lessons for review.

**Boundary.** Feedback, a retrospective document or stored conversation history does not establish organisational learning by itself.

**Illustrative breakdown.** A lesson is recorded, but the next relevant case repeats the same unsupported assumptions.

**Practical question.** What have we learned, why do we believe it, and where will it change future work?

## Adapt

*Operational capability · Improve*

Develop, retire or reconfigure capability, authority, structure and resources in response to credible learning and changed conditions.

**Core work**

- Translate learning into implications for capability and its dependencies.
- Change, stabilise or deliberately retain arrangements as evidence warrants.
- Assign ownership and examine later use, reliability and unintended effects.

**What to look for**

- An explicit capability decision and accountable owner.
- Changes to work, authority, resources or routines, where warranted.
- Evidence from relevant later work over a stated review horizon.

**People.** Own resource and capability choices, institutionalisation, retirement and continued review.

**Possible AI contribution.** Propose changes, examine scenarios and support bounded experiments.

**Boundary.** A successful pilot or scheduled review does not demonstrate persistent capability. Consider skill loss and dependency as well as speed.

**Illustrative breakdown.** New technology or training is introduced, but work and ownership remain unchanged.

**Practical question.** What should be changed or retained, and what later evidence would show that it lasts?

## Contextualise

*Connective mechanism · Revisited throughout*

Relate attended signals and information to the organisational circumstances that give them meaning.

**Core work**

- Connect purpose, entities, relationships and the current state.
- Check history, time, evidence, relevant expertise and uncertainty.
- Respect authority, access, permissions and purpose limitations.

**What to look for**

- Relevant context with source, time, scope and permission.
- Material missing or disputed context made explicit.
- Reconsideration when a decision, exception or condition changes.

**People.** Explain local meaning, tacit knowledge, stakeholder experience and disputed interpretations.

**Possible AI contribution.** Retrieve and assemble purpose-bound context with source and provenance.

**Boundary.** Contextualise connects Sense and Know and recurs across the model. It is not a seventh capability or a requirement to install a knowledge graph.

**Illustrative breakdown.** An accurate fact is applied to the wrong customer, policy version, time period or authority boundary.

**Practical question.** What would we need to know about this situation to interpret the information correctly?

## Organisational orientation

*Integrated state · Sufficiently shared or aligned*

An account of what is happening, why it matters, what remains uncertain and what should shape response.

**Core work**

- Situation and significance: what is believed to be happening, why it matters and to whom.
- Uncertainty and constraints: unknowns, disagreements, purpose, authority, resources and rights.
- Response conditions: what should shape action, monitoring or further inquiry.

**What to look for**

- Relevant roles can explain the situation and their response conditions.
- Material disagreement and uncertainty remain visible.
- The account connects to the next decision and can be revised.

**People.** Bring perspectives into sufficient alignment for coordinated response while preserving legitimate disagreement.

**Possible AI contribution.** Assemble evidence, compare interpretations and identify gaps for people to resolve or retain explicitly.

**Boundary.** A document, an AI summary or unanimous agreement alone does not establish organisational orientation.

**Illustrative breakdown.** Teams share a summary but interpret its implications differently and make incompatible commitments.

**Practical question.** Do the relevant people understand what matters, what is uncertain and what should shape the response?

## Outcomes and feedback

*Connective bridge · Mobilise to Learn*

Turn evidence about consequences into feedback suitable for learning: output → consequence → observation → evaluation → feedback.

**Core work**

- Observe intended, adverse, delayed and redistributed consequences.
- Compare with a baseline or credible alternative and check implementation.
- Evaluate competing explanations and state the warranted conclusion.

**What to look for**

- Whose outcomes, which work and what time horizon are covered.
- Traceable observations, comparison logic and missing evidence.
- Uncertainty, rival explanations and a proportionate follow-up decision.

**People.** Bring domain and evaluation expertise, represent affected groups and challenge favourable interpretations.

**Possible AI contribution.** Analyse records, detect differences across groups and suggest alternative explanations for testing.

**Boundary.** A completed output or a favourable metric does not establish benefit or causality. Observation and evaluation are distinct.

**Illustrative breakdown.** Faster handling is attributed to AI even though easier cases, staffing or policy changes may explain it.

**Practical question.** What changed for whom, compared with what, and what else could explain the result?

## Organisational memory

*Continuity infrastructure · Across all three transformations*

Supply prior signals, context, decisions, relationships, routines and lessons, and retain what current learning produces.

**Core work**

- Preserve provenance, decision-time context and applicability.
- Assign stewardship, permissions, correction and supersession.
- Make relevant memory available when subsequent work needs it.

**What to look for**

- Records and routines with ownership, source, time and scope.
- Current and superseded knowledge clearly distinguished.
- Evidence of appropriate use, correction and transfer into later work.

**People.** Steward memory, explain historical conditions and judge when an earlier lesson applies.

**Possible AI contribution.** Retrieve and organise permitted material, identify recurrence and surface superseded information.

**Boundary.** A repository or an AI agent’s recall is not organisational memory by itself. Persistent recall creates no authority.

**Illustrative breakdown.** An old decision is reused without its assumptions, context or record of supersession.

**Practical question.** Can the organisation recover the right lesson, with its limits, when the next relevant situation arises?

## Purpose, governance and responsible exercise

*Spanning conditions · Every capability and transition*

Make the purpose and exercise of organisational capacity legitimate, attributable, proportionate and open to challenge.

**Core work**

- Make purpose, rights, representation and consequences explicit.
- Define authority, evidence expectations and accountability through handoffs.
- Provide challenge, appeal, correction, stop and recovery routes.

**What to look for**

- Who may decide, act, question and correct, with what authority.
- Whose signals and expertise enter, and who bears consequences.
- Evidence and review proportionate to the decision and its stakes.

**People.** Exercise accountable authority, represent affected interests and maintain legitimate challenge.

**Possible AI contribution.** Assist traceability, identify missing evidence and flag possible exceptions for accountable review.

**Boundary.** Effective coordination alone does not establish a legitimate purpose or acceptable consequences. Accountability must survive delegation.

**Illustrative breakdown.** A fast, coordinated process shifts harm to a group that cannot question the decision.

**Practical question.** Who can question the purpose, the decision and its consequences, and secure a correction?

## People and AI in organisational work

*Participation across the model · AI is optional*

Design the contribution of people and AI around the actual work, expertise, authority and consequences.

**Core work**

- Define the problem, eligible cases and a credible non-AI alternative.
- Specify people’s roles and any AI objective, principal, data, tools and action limits.
- Set review, challenge, stop, recovery and lifecycle ownership.

**What to look for**

- Which person reviews what, with which expertise and evidence.
- Explicit authority, permitted actions and attributable records.
- Outcome evaluation that includes review effort, failure and dependency.

**People.** Provide expertise and accountable decisions, redesign the work and retain the ability to intervene effectively.

**Possible AI contribution.** May support any capability through retrieval, analysis, recommendations or explicitly permitted actions.

**Boundary.** AI use is not a prerequisite for organisational intelligence. “Human oversight” needs a named role, evidence, authority and ability to alter the result.

**Illustrative breakdown.** A nominal reviewer approves recommendations without the time, expertise or authority to challenge them.

**Practical question.** Where does AI help this work, what remains with people, and how do we respond when it is wrong?

## Connection quality

*Provisional diagnostic lens · No universal score*

Examine whether meaning, authority, state, evidence and accountability survive the transitions between capabilities.

**Core work**

- Trace a real episode across signals, knowledge, commitments and action.
- Inspect handoffs and the return of evaluated feedback to later work.
- Compare connection problems with workload, incentives, resources and other explanations.

**What to look for**

- Source and recipient accounts of what was transferred and understood.
- Usable context, accepted responsibility and traceable action.
- Exceptions, lost information, rework and unresolved accountability.

**People.** Reconcile meanings, accept handoffs and resolve mismatches in expertise, incentives or authority.

**Possible AI contribution.** Help trace dependencies, compare records and flag inconsistencies for investigation.

**Boundary.** This is a research and diagnostic lens, not a validated universal score or the sole explanation for organisational performance.

**Illustrative breakdown.** Each team meets its own target, while authority or context disappears between teams.

**Practical question.** Where does the next person, team or system lose what it needs to respond well?

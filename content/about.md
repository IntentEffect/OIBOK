# About OIBOK

The Organisation Intelligence Body of Knowledge is an open research publication and practical toolkit for understanding how organisations notice important signals, make sense of their circumstances, decide, coordinate work and learn from what happens.

**Founded by Intent & Effect, developed by the OIBOK community.**

## Why this work exists

Adding a capable AI system leaves organisations with questions about the work around it. What problem is worth solving? Which knowledge can be trusted? Who has authority to decide? How do people and systems coordinate? What evidence shows whether the change helped, and how does that learning become part of future practice?

OIBOK brings these questions into one connected account. It gives organisations a shared language for examining the relationships between knowledge, decisions, collective action and learning, alongside practical tools for investigating and improving a real workflow.

Its research draws on organisational studies, management and decision science, systems thinking, knowledge management, organisational learning, information systems and relevant work on artificial intelligence. AI is one potential participant in this organisational system. The scope also includes work that uses no AI.

## The working definition

> Organisational Intelligence is the persistent organisation-level capacity to form an orientation from relevant signals and experience, coordinate collective response, and improve future response through feedback.

This is the working definition in OIBOK 1.0. It describes a proposed organisation-level capacity whose empirical boundaries remain open to investigation.

## Three connected transformations

**Orient.** Develop a sufficiently informed organisational orientation from relevant signals, experience and context.

**Coordinate.** Translate that orientation into deliberate, authorised collective response.

**Improve.** Use outcomes and feedback to strengthen or preserve future response capability.

The six operational capabilities are **Sense, Know, Choose, Mobilise, Learn and Adapt**. The model is recursive and sensitive to context; organisations can revisit a question as their understanding changes.

## What you can use

The [body of knowledge](/knowledge/) contains ten connected chapters and supporting research registers. Read a chapter to investigate a particular problem, or follow the connections to understand its wider organisational context.

The [practical toolkit](/tools/) helps a team review evidence, map context and knowledge, examine decision rights and coordination, design a bounded AI intervention, and plan how to evaluate and retain learning. Start with one recurring decision or workflow and involve people who do and depend on the work.

The [research resources](/research/) expose propositions, definitions, references and open questions so that the work can be checked, challenged and improved.

## What the evidence currently supports

OIBOK 1.0 was released on 15 August 2026 as a literature-grounded working synthesis. Its ten chapters and supporting registers underwent documented review, corrections and consistency checks. Independent field validation of the complete construct remains future work.

OIBOK does not currently provide a validated intelligence scale, maturity score, certification or guarantee of organisational improvement. The practical toolkit, version 0.1, provides methods for piloting and learning in practice. The proposed 1.1 research amendments remain under consideration and are separate from the released 1.0 baseline.

## Origins and stewardship

Intent & Effect initiated the research programme and developed the first edition. It serves as the founding steward and initial publisher, responsible for the official release, editorial infrastructure and publication integrity.

OIBOK maintains its own publication identity and welcomes external contributors. Its [governance](/governance/) explains how proposals are reviewed, decisions are recorded and the work can evolve.

## Source appraisals

The EW identifiers belong to this supplement and do not renumber the released source register. Access dates are **28 November 2026**. Inspection means reading the primary publication and the relevant methods, results and limitations; it does not mean reproducing the analysis or independently auditing the underlying data. EW-04 has the narrower access stated in its entry.

### EW-01 Coordination mechanisms

Katharina G. Kugler, Julia A. M. Reif, Agnes B. Hoechtl and Felix C. Brodbeck. [*Temporal Coordination in Teams: The Interaction Between Explicit and Implicit Mechanisms*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10726-026-10029-6). *Group Decision and Negotiation* 35, article 77. Published 20 November 2026. **Access:** full text.

**Design and finding.** A survey of 154 employees and studies of 37 and 57 student teams report that explicit and implicit temporal coordination can compensate for one another. The evidence concerns coordination of time within teams.

**Limits and alternatives.** Shared temporal cognition is a proxy for implicit coordination. Small or partial team samples and differing effectiveness measures limit transfer. Familiarity, task characteristics and leadership could help explain performance. The evidence does not justify generally reducing coordination or removing required controls.

**Open question.** When do the mechanisms substitute, complement one another or cease to work across teams and changing conditions?

### EW-02 Useful knowledge

Minye Shao, Chenghao Xiao, Xingyue Zhao and Yang Long. [*AutoOntologyBench: A Task-Grounded Benchmark for Agentic Knowledge Engineering*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27734v1). arXiv:2611.27734v1, submitted 20 November 2026. **Access:** full paper. **Status:** preprint.

**Design and finding.** The benchmark compares nine models using 19,947 tasks from 18 sources across seven domains. Different representations perform differently across domains; task success, representation accuracy, semantic repair and resource use reveal different trade-offs.

**Limits and alternatives.** Benchmark performance does not demonstrate organisational knowledge, sustained capability or long-term maintenance value. Model choice, retrieval, representation construction and task selection can affect results. An ontology's presence alone does not identify the cause of a gain.

**Open question.** Which representation supports the organisation's actual decisions and remains useful after source changes, exceptions and maintenance costs are included?

### EW-03 Compatible understanding

Cornelia Reindl and Maja Störmer. [*Spanning boundaries in socio-technological transformation: practical approaches for developing and iterating shared mental models*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11612-026-00924-6). *Gruppe. Interaktion. Organisation.* Published 25 November 2026. **Access:** full text.

**Design and contribution.** A structured narrative review selects 41 publications from 220 records. It develops a conceptual account of how shared understanding can be built and revised across organisational boundaries, including through material and digital artefacts.

**Limits and alternatives.** The paper supplies no new causal test of organisation-wide effectiveness. Review selection and the move from team research to wider networks limit inference. Improved results could also reflect clearer authority, resources or changed incentives.

**Open question.** Does the proposed representation help different roles interpret consequential cases compatibly while retaining useful disagreement and specialist distinctions?

### EW-04 Cognitive ambidexterity candidate

Tahir Abbas Syed. [*Cognitive Ambidexterity: Balancing Algorithmic Efficiency and Contextual Judgement in AI-Enabled Decision Systems*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/01492063261501111). *Journal of Management*. Published 21 November 2026. **Access:** publication metadata and abstract only; full text unavailable in this check.

**Disposition: defer substantive use.** The title and abstract identify a potentially relevant account of AI-enabled decisions. The full argument, methods, evidence, limitations and competing explanations were not inspected sufficiently to support an amendment or a new practice requirement.

**Next check.** Obtain the complete paper and assess its contribution against existing work before deciding whether it adds anything to OIBOK. Existing guidance on authority, challenge and contextual judgement continues under its established sources; this candidate does not strengthen its evidential status.

### EW-05 Recovery and learning

Nils-Ole Hohenstein and David M. Herold. [*Pandemic crisis and the race for supply chain resilience through organizational learning and agility*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43621-026-05370-4). *Discover Sustainability* 7, article 1992. Published 18 November 2026. **Access:** full text. **Window:** supplementary, outside 20–26 November.

**Design and finding.** Seven European firm cases draw on 17 retrospective interviews collected during 2022–2024. Participants describe learning, coordination and agility in pandemic supply-chain responses.

**Limits and alternatives.** These are context-specific qualitative accounts, not causal effect estimates or AI evidence. Retrospective selection and recall matter; resources, supplier relationships and changing demand are competing explanations. Recovery does not by itself establish enduring adaptability.

**Open question.** Which changes persist, transfer to another person or setting, and improve the response to a later disruption?

## How to use the refinements

For a coordination problem, ask the people involved how the work stays aligned in an ordinary case and an exception. Trace accepted completion and the effort required. The [decision and handoff guidance](/guide/decisions-and-handoffs/) and [evaluation guidance](/guide/evaluate/) help turn the explanation into a comparison that could prove it wrong. Preserve applicable decision rights and required records throughout the comparison.

For a knowledge problem, separate **information availability**, **semantic representation**, **usable organisational knowledge** and **organisational capability**. These distinctions organise the inquiry; each claim needs its own evidence. A readable representation should help someone perform the intended work, and a capability claim requires evidence across relevant episodes. The [work and knowledge chapter](/guide/work-and-knowledge/) applies the distinction to a small vocabulary and the people who use it.

For an incident or disruption, record when service returned, what was learned, who subsequently used that learning and what happened under a later change of circumstances. The [retained-learning guidance](/guide/retain-learning/) keeps these observations separate. A completed recovery record does not answer the later questions.

These are OIBOK's practical applications to test, not interventions validated by the new sources. None supplies a universal connection score, a prescribed ontology architecture or evidence of the whole model's superior effectiveness.

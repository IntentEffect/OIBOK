# Try Chapter 1 on a real decision

This draft pilot asks whether using [Signals and attention](/knowledge/signals-and-attention/reader/) changes how a small-business team explains a recurring problem or what it decides to do about it. We are seeking one team and one independent subject reviewer. No pilot outcomes or external reviews have been recorded for this edition.

## Join the pilot

**Small-business team.** Does a piece of work regularly come back for another pass? Bring one example that went smoothly and one that stalled. We’d like two colleagues to try the worksheet together, keep their own case records, and tell us whether a prompt changed their decision. [Volunteer as a team](mailto:info@oibok.org?subject=OIBOK%20small-business%20pilot).

**Independent reviewer.** If you work in organisational research, attention or evaluation and had no role in drafting Chapter 1, we invite you to check one claim against its source. A specific counterexample or a narrower wording is welcome. [Volunteer to review a claim](mailto:info@oibok.org?subject=OIBOK%20independent%20review).

## Finding the tool unaided

Before showing the links, ask a colleague to start on the Knowledge page with the prompt “Work keeps coming back. Find something you could use with your team.” Let them choose the route. Ask them to save a worksheet with invented entries and reopen it. Record the device, where they hesitated, whether they needed a hint, and whether their entries survived. Use a phone for one attempt and keyboard navigation for another. Record any facilitator assistance alongside the result.

## For the team

Choose a real, bounded decision you can revisit. An accepted quote that repeatedly stalls before booking is one possibility. Choose something the team already understands well enough to have an opinion about.

1. **Make your starting position visible.** Before introducing the chapter, ask what the team thinks happens, what it would ordinarily do next, and why. Record the answer in the [A4 worksheet](/tools/two-case-review/). Record when you start.
2. **Follow two actual cases.** Bring the person who did the work and the person who received it. Compare a case that went through with one that stalled. Read the reader chapter and check which explanation survives the records. Note what a team member disputed or what evidence was missing.
3. **Make a decision.** Keep, change or defer the original plan. Point to the exact question or passage that influenced the decision, if any. Record a decision owner, a review date, elapsed time, and any effort shifted to another person.
4. **Return after the next eligible cases.** Record the number examined, how they were selected, what happened and any unwanted effects. If possible, check a case handled by someone who missed the first discussion. If no suitable case occurs, record that and leave the outcome open.

The before-and-after comparison tells us what this team found useful. A fresh pair of eyes, a second look at the files or the facilitator’s questions might explain a change. Ask which of these could have produced the same decision without the chapter. Report a result of “no added value” as readily as an improvement.

## Independent subject review

Choose a reviewer with experience in organisational attention, organisational research or a related evidence discipline. They should have had no part in drafting this chapter. Record their relevant background, any relationship with the steward and what they actually checked.

| Review question | What to return |
| --- | --- |
| Does the chapter fairly represent its strongest theoretical claim? | Choose a sentence about how rules and routines direct attention. Give the source passage and page, your verdict and any narrower wording you would support. |
| Can you tell where OIBOK’s interpretation begins? | Check the eight qualities against the Chapter 1 attribution and Joseph et al. Point out language that implies stronger evidence than the source provides. |
| What observation could overturn the diagnosis? | Give a counterexample or rival explanation. Say whether the worksheet would lead the team to look for it. |
| Would you retain this chapter for readers? | Identify one passage to keep, one to change or remove, and why. Record any sources you could only check in abstract. |

## Returning feedback

Send the resource title, draft 0.1, role, time spent and a short account of what to keep or change to [info@oibok.org](mailto:info@oibok.org). Keep customer names, case files and identifiable staff details in the organisation. The team can return a de-identified summary. The steward will ask separately before publishing any case details, reviewer comments or names.

After both reviews, record what changed in the team’s decision, where the chapter overreached, and what remains uncertain. Use those findings to choose whether to revise this chapter, test it with a second team or extend the reader format. The decision about the wider Guide 0.7 revision follows that review. The focused [Guide 0.6.3 practice update](/guide/) is available meanwhile. A separate [workforce-development pilot](/research/workforce-development-pilot/) examines learning arrangements and workflow outcomes; it does not replace this Chapter 1 review. Record the exact edition used and keep it stable within a comparison; no pilot result is claimed by this editorial update.

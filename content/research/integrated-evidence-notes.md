# Evidence behind OIBOK’s eleven propositions

Editorial evidence notes, version 0.5, 28 November 2026. Each quoted claim comes from research release 1.0. All eleven propositions now have a targeted appraisal. This edition adds notes 01, 03, 04, 05, 06, 08 and 10, distinguishing empirical findings from conceptual definitions, proposed mechanisms and governance commitments.

Each note states what the inspected sources support, where OIBOK extends them and what could challenge a practical judgement. Source access varies and is recorded beside each claim. These appraisals do not complete the whole bibliography audit or independently validate the propositions. Released wording and historical source bytes are preserved; independent subject review remains pending.

The [25 November AI appraisal](/research/working-with-ai/) and [27 November evidence watch](/research/evidence-watch-2026-11-27/) remain part of notes 02, 07, 09 and 11. The [Guide evaluation chapter](/guide/evaluate/#worked-decision-a-booking-handoff) shows how the questions can inform a fictional decision.

## 01 · Persistent organisational capacity

**Released claim · OI-IP-001**

> Organisational Intelligence exists where the capacity to orient, coordinate and improve is instantiated through an organisational system sufficiently persistent to operate beyond one participant, tool or isolated episode.

**Kind of claim.** This is a constitutive proposition: it sets the boundary of what OIBOK calls organisational intelligence. It is not an established finding that these conditions are necessary or sufficient for good organisational performance.

**What the evidence supports.** Crossan, Lane and White develop a conceptual framework linking learning by individuals and groups with its incorporation into organisational arrangements. They explain how learning can remain in routines, structures and systems after particular members leave. They also discuss how established arrangements can obstruct new learning. This supplies a theoretical basis for examining persistence, rather than treating an individual's achievement as an organisational capability. [Crossan, Lane and White (1999), framework and institutionalising sections, pp. 523–525, 529–530; discussion, pp. 534–535](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.1999.2202135).

**Where OIBOK extends it.** The paper concerns organisational learning and strategic renewal. It does not test OIBOK's three transformations, define how long a capacity must persist, or establish a threshold for independence from a person or tool. Those are choices within OIBOK's proposed account. Persistence alone can also preserve an ineffective routine, so its presence does not establish useful adaptation or responsible conduct.

**What could change the judgement?** Follow comparable cases handled by the original expert and by a colleague, including a change of tool or circumstances where relevant. Identify what lets the second person proceed, then inspect the outcome and effort. A shared verbal routine may provide continuity; a document that nobody can use may not. If the result depends on the expert's unrecorded intervention, narrow the claim to that person's contribution. To challenge the usefulness of the definition itself, ask whether different reviewers can apply its boundary consistently and whether it explains anything beyond existing accounts of organisational learning and capability.

**Source check, 28 November 2026.** Selected full-text passages inspected. This is conceptual support and an OIBOK application to test; it does not amount to independent validation.

## 02 · Selective attention

**Released claim · OI-IP-002**

> Organisational Intelligence begins with selective organisational attention; information quantity, visibility or notification volume cannot substitute for the capability to recognise, qualify and route material signals.

**What the evidence supports.** Ocasio’s attention-based theory explains how the situation, rules and channels of a firm influence which issues get decision-makers’ attention. Joseph and colleagues describe later work on attention through interactions and relationships. These sources support investigating who could see a clue and where it could be raised. [Ocasio (1997), theoretical principles, pp. 187–188](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/towards-an-attention-based-view-of-the-firm/) · [Joseph et al. (2024), introduction, pp. 6–7](https://doi.org/10.1177/14761270231223397).

**Where the eight qualities come from.** Chapter 1 says the editorial review added a set of qualities to its existing indicators. It asks whether the team can distinguish a material clue, hear from different sources, notice it in time, trace its origin, route it, handle the volume, challenge a dismissal and carry the clue into interpretation. OIBOK selected these eight prompts. The cited research gives them a theoretical context. Their completeness, relative importance and value in small firms remain open. [Chapter 1, section 11A](/knowledge/signals-and-attention/#11a-signals-and-attention-quality).

**What could change the judgement?** Use one customer acceptance and the decision it should inform. An inbox timestamp establishes delivery. A scheduler’s contemporaneous note explaining the request provides stronger evidence of attention. If delayed and timely bookings were noticed and understood equally promptly, inspect authority and staffing before changing the notification rules. To test the prompts themselves, record whether they reveal anything a team’s usual review missed, including occasions when they add no value.

## 03 · Evidence-bearing orientation

**Released claim · OI-IP-003**

> Useful organisational orientation requires context, knowledge and expertise combined in a form that preserves relevant source, time, authority, uncertainty, disagreement and representation limits.

**Kind of claim.** This combines a proposed mechanism with an epistemic standard for judging how well an organisation understands a situation. The complete list of information qualities is OIBOK's synthesis.

**What the evidence supports.** Johns argues that situational conditions affect both the meaning of organisational behaviour and relationships between variables. His conceptual treatment gives a reason to preserve context when interpreting a finding. [Johns (2006), publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.2006.20208687).

Faraj and Sproull report a cross-sectional study of 69 software development teams. Coordinating expertise was associated with performance beyond the contribution of expertise being present and other measured team characteristics. The useful distinction is between possessing relevant knowledge and being able to bring it into the work. [Faraj and Sproull (2000), publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.46.12.1554.12072).

**Where OIBOK extends it.** Neither source tests the proposition's entire bundle of source, time, authority, uncertainty, disagreement and representation limits. Johns supplies a conceptual argument; the team study reports an association in a particular work setting. Its abstract does not permit a complete assessment of measurement quality, sampling or alternative causal explanations. Treat the bundle as questions for an evidence review, with depth proportionate to the decision, rather than an empirically validated checklist.

**What could change the judgement?** Ask two people who must act on the same case to explain what is known, where it came from, when it was true, who can resolve a disputed point and what remains uncertain. Compare their explanations with the original records and with a later case. If an apparently complete brief still leads to incompatible decisions, examine its meaning and usability. If a shorter account supports equally sound decisions with less effort, reduce the information requirement. More metadata is useful only when it helps the particular judgement. The [Guide's knowledge checks](/guide/work-and-knowledge/) and [recent representation appraisal](/research/evidence-watch-2026-11-27/#ew-02-useful-knowledge) develop this application.

**Source check, 28 November 2026.** Both publisher abstracts were inspected; the full methods and results of the team study were not available in this check. The scope of this note is correspondingly limited.

## 04 · Authorised commitment

**Released claim · OI-IP-004**

> Orientation influences organisational response only where it is converted into an attributable commitment under explicit decision rights, relevant evidence and appropriate contestability.

**Kind of claim.** This combines a proposed account of how decisions lead to action with a governance standard about attribution, authority and challenge. The governance component should be assessed openly as a normative choice.

**What the evidence supports.** Aghion and Tirole's formal model separates the right to decide from effective influence over a decision. Information and delegation interact: giving a subordinate more influence can encourage initiative while reducing the principal's control. This challenges an assumption that the person named in an approval process necessarily determines what happens. The result is theoretical, conditional on the model's assumptions. [Aghion and Tirole (1997), introduction and model, pp. 1–8; summary, pp. 27–28](https://doi.org/10.1086/262063).

Bovens defines accountability through an actor's obligation to explain conduct to a forum that can question and judge it, with possible consequences. The framework distinguishes accountable conduct from simply making information public. It is a conceptual analysis of public governance. [Bovens (2007), pp. 450–452](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0386.2007.00378.x).

**Where OIBOK extends it.** These sources do not establish that orientation can influence action only through explicit decision rights. Informal influence, implicit delegation and emergent action require attention as well. The released wording is best treated as OIBOK's standard for an attributable, challengeable commitment, not a universal causal law. Whether a particular approval or review arrangement improves decisions requires separate evidence; adding approvals can introduce delay without improving judgement.

**What could change the judgement?** Trace one proposal to the actual commitment of people, money or work. Identify who could authorise it, who shaped it in practice, what evidence they used and how a challenge could change it. A signed approval with no feasible means of acting points to a different problem from an unresolved decision right. Compare similar cases before and after clarifying authority, keeping decision quality, waiting and effort visible. If the change adds delay without resolving the contested decision, revise the arrangement rather than assuming that more formalisation is better. The [Guide's decision and handoff guidance](/guide/decisions-and-handoffs/) supplies the working prompts.

**Source check, 28 November 2026.** The cited full-text passages were inspected. Neither paper is an empirical test of the complete OIBOK proposition or a prescription for a universal decision hierarchy.

## 05 · Mobilisation and execution

**Released claim · OI-IP-005**

> A decision contributes to Organisational Intelligence only where its purpose and authority survive translation into coordinated dependencies and reliable execution under actual operating conditions.

**Kind of claim.** This proposes a mechanism connecting a decision to completed work. The requirement to preserve purpose and authority is part of OIBOK's interpretation of a useful organisational response.

**What the evidence supports.** Okhuysen and Bechky's integrative review explains how coordination arrangements can establish responsibility for tasks, anticipation of others' actions and a shared understanding of the work. Their framework includes both formal arrangements and coordination that develops through interaction. It does not prescribe one mechanism for every setting. [Okhuysen and Bechky (2009), integrative conditions and discussion, pp. 483–492](https://doi.org/10.5465/19416520903047533).

Bechky and Okhuysen compare qualitative material from one SWAT team and four film production crews. They describe people changing roles, routines or the order of work when surprises arise. The comparison combines separately collected studies; mission accounts relied on interviews, with observation of briefings and training, while film work was observed directly. It illustrates situated responses rather than estimating a causal effect across organisations. [Bechky and Okhuysen (2011), methods, pp. 241–244; findings and discussion, pp. 249, 256–259](https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2011.60263060).

**Where OIBOK extends it.** The sources support inspecting how interdependent work is accomplished, but they do not validate the full decision-to-execution chain in this proposition. Reliable execution can require an authorised adjustment to the plan. Fidelity should therefore concern the intended purpose and applicable constraints, rather than literal compliance with instructions that no longer fit. A completed task still requires separate evaluation of its consequences.

**What could change the judgement?** Follow a decision through an ordinary case and an exception. Ask the recipient what they accepted, what they needed from others and what changed while doing the work. Compare that account with the intended result, available resources and actual outcome. If a handoff was accepted and usable but the necessary equipment was unavailable, another handoff checklist may not address the delay. If the result depended on an improvised workaround, record whether the workaround preserved authority and purpose and whether another person could use it. Compare total effort and failures as well as completions. [Guide practice checks](/guide/decisions-and-handoffs/#choose-coordination-that-serves-the-work).

**Source check, 28 November 2026.** The cited full-text sections were inspected in author-uploaded copies. The review and situated qualitative comparison support bounded investigation; they do not validate a universal execution measure.

## 06 · Consequence and evaluative feedback

**Released claim · OI-IP-006**

> Activity, completion and output do not establish outcome; credible improvement requires observation and evaluation of material consequences, implementation, stakeholder distribution, alternative explanation and measurement integrity.

**Claim type.** A mechanism and epistemic proposition. It concerns the grounds for judging improvement, including whose consequences enter that judgement.

**What the evidence supports.** Carroll and colleagues critically review implementation fidelity and propose a framework for examining what was delivered and how. This helps distinguish a weak intervention from weak implementation. Their framework itself required further empirical testing. [Carroll et al. (2007), Background, conceptual framework and Summary](https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-2-40).

March and Sutton's methodological essay identifies causal complexity and the limits of retrospective accounts as problems when explaining organisational performance. Its argument supports caution about attributing an observed improvement to a selected management practice. [March and Sutton (1997), abstract, p. 698](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.8.6.698).

**Where OIBOK extends it.** OIBOK combines these concerns with its own requirements to examine consequences, affected interests and measurement integrity. Neither source tests that complete combination or establishes that every listed check is necessary in every case. Implementation evidence helps interpret an outcome; it does not establish that the intervention is beneficial. Decisions about whose interests count also require an explicit value judgement.

**What could change the judgement?** For a booking change, preserve the eligibility rule, intake cutoff, outcome window and original measures. Compare similar jobs and record staffing, customer wait, quality and effort across the people involved. More completed bookings alongside extra staff or easier cases leaves the proposed explanation unresolved. If the apparent benefit disappears after those differences are examined, narrow the claim. If customers benefit while another group carries substantial uncounted work, reopen the judgement about overall improvement. Report a credible finding of no benefit as clearly as a favourable one. The [Guide evaluation chapter](/guide/evaluate/) provides the working record and examples.

**Access check, 28 November 2026.** Carroll et al.: full text, including search approach, framework, measurement discussion and limitations. March and Sutton: publisher abstract only. This is a targeted methodological appraisal, not independent validation of OI-IP-006.

## 07 · Persistent learning

**Released claim · OI-IP-007**

> Feedback becomes organisational learning only where reflection changes organisationally available understanding or future response and that change remains retrievable and influential beyond the immediate individual or episode.

**What the evidence supports.** Levitt and March describe a theoretical mechanism through which organisations encode experience in routines that guide later behaviour. Darr, Argote and Epple examined weekly production data from 36 pizza stores belonging to ten franchisees. Unit costs fell with experience, the gains depreciated, and transfer followed ownership boundaries in this sample. Persistence and transfer are therefore questions worth measuring separately. [Levitt and March (1988), abstract, p. 319](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.so.14.080188.001535) · [Darr, Argote and Epple (1995), abstract, p. 1750](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.41.11.1750).

**Where OIBOK extends it.** A revised procedure shows that someone recorded a lesson. Its author recalling the fix shows individual retention. A colleague finding and applying the fix on a later case gives stronger evidence that the team can draw on it. This sequence is OIBOK’s proposed field check. The franchise study measured production costs, so it cannot tell us which individual retrieved a particular lesson. It offers relevant service-setting evidence, with uncertain transfer to a standalone small firm.

**What could change the judgement?** Follow the same workflow across shifts. If the cover worker needs the author to intervene, qualify the claim of team learning. If the worker succeeds, ask how they knew what to do. Prior experience, an easier case or a supervisor’s private prompt could also explain success. A colleague independently applying a shared verbal routine is relevant evidence even where no written procedure exists.

**Workforce-development application, 26 November 2026.** The [prepared pilot](/research/workforce-development-pilot/) asks how changed entry roles and practice opportunities affect the development of required judgement. It records supervision and assistance, later performance on a different case and reuse by another person separately. A short pilot can inform a local learning arrangement; it cannot establish the long-term supply of experienced practitioners. The [review records](/tools/workforce-development-review/) also separate OIBOK's contribution to the diagnosis from the effects of the chosen intervention.

**Recovery and adaptation application, 28 November 2026.** The supplementary [EW-05 appraisal](/research/evidence-watch-2026-11-27/#ew-05-recovery-and-learning) informs a further distinction in [Guide Chapter 7](/guide/retain-learning/): restored service, a lesson that remains usable, and evidence of adaptation in a later episode are separate observations. Record the later case and who used the learning, including failed reuse. Keep resources, supplier support and changed demand visible as possible explanations. The study does not establish an AI effect or validate OIBOK's persistence proposition.

## 08 · Capability and coherent adaptation

**Released claim · OI-IP-008**

> Learning closes the Organisational Intelligence loop only where it is translated into durable capability or an evidence-linked decision to preserve, renew, combine, retire or reconfigure capability coherently.

**Claim type.** A proposed mechanism within OIBOK's account of adaptation. The requirement for a completed OI loop is part of the synthesis to be examined.

**What the evidence supports.** Winter distinguishes capabilities used in current work, patterned capabilities for changing that work, and ad hoc problem solving. His conceptual argument also considers the costs of maintaining a change capability; investing in one is not always advantageous. It supports examining both persistence and the value of the arrangement. [Winter (2003), pp. 991–994](https://doi.org/10.1002/smj.318).

Repenning and Sterman report an inductive study of two processes within one firm and develop a dynamic model. Their abstract proposes interactions between delayed improvement benefits and managers' interpretations of poor performance as a mechanism that can obstruct change. This supplies a bounded alternative to explaining failed improvement as a simple lack of willingness to learn. [Repenning and Sterman (2002), author-hosted abstract](https://mitmgmtfaculty.mit.edu/jsterman/capabilitytraps/).

**Where OIBOK extends it.** Neither paper tests the OIBOK loop or specifies a universal duration for durable capability. Winter offers conceptual distinctions, while the second source combines a single firm's experience with modelling. Transfer to another organisation requires examination. A useful lesson can justify keeping an arrangement or retiring an expensive one; the proposition should not become a requirement to create another process whenever someone learns something.

**What could change the judgement?** Follow a lesson to a specific choice about the work and inspect what happens on later cases, including a change of responsible person or operating conditions. Record what remains usable, the resources it depends on and the effort of maintaining it. If the result depends on the original expert returning each time, narrow the capability claim to that dependency. If the revised arrangement costs more than the benefit it preserves, reconsider it. A single successful response leaves persistence open. The [retained-learning chapter](/guide/retain-learning/) separates these observations from recovery alone.

**Access check, 28 November 2026.** Winter: full text, pp. 991–994, in a university-hosted publisher PDF. Repenning and Sterman: the author's published abstract; detailed case methods and model assumptions were not inspected. No general causal effect or validation of OI-IP-008 is claimed.

## 09 · Quality of the connections

**Released claim · OI-IP-009**

> Project OI hypothesises that the complete organisation-level capacity depends materially on the integrity of the conversions among its capabilities; strong local capability may not compensate reliably for broken, delayed, unauthorised or unreconstructable transitions.

**What the evidence supports.** Gittell studied coordination among patient-care providers. The abstract reports that the relationship between performance and three arrangements (boundary-spanning roles, team meetings and routines) operated through the way participants communicated and worked together. The reported effects varied with uncertainty. That gives a reason to inspect what happens between roles when a process repeatedly stalls. [Gittell (2002), abstract, p. 1408](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.48.11.1408.268).

**Where OIBOK extends it.** A finding about coordination in patient care supplies indirect support for a much broader claim about connections across sensing, deciding, acting and learning. The study does not establish how often a damaged connection defeats strong local performance, or how much one team can compensate for another. For a small business, treat this as a plausible explanation to test.

**Contemporary evidence check.** The [new source register](/research/working-with-ai/#source-register) pairs Anthropic's exploratory interviews (WAI-01) with Chen and Stratton's software-production study (WAI-02). Read their methods and limits before using them. OIBOK's inference is to follow an improvement through the next stage and the eventual outcome, with particular attention to acceptance, waiting, reviewing capacity and rework. Neither source tests the complete OIBOK construct.

**What could change the judgement?** Take a delayed booking as the unit of inquiry. Trace when sales sent sufficient information, when scheduling accepted it, who could allocate a slot and what the customer experienced. Compare with a similar booking on a shift with similar capacity. Timely acceptance followed by a documented wait for the only qualified technician weakens a handoff diagnosis. Across many comparable cases, reliable outcomes despite repeatedly broken handoffs would challenge the broader proposition. Look for a compensating route, such as an experienced dispatcher making calls.

For an AI-assisted workflow, compare accepted completions, queue age, quality and total effort before and after the change, allowing for case mix and demand. If review capacity explains the wait while the handoff is usable and authorised, narrow the diagnosis to capacity. If additional output has no recipient or demand, examine the purpose of producing it. If outcomes improve because of extra expert support, do not attribute the improvement to connection quality alone. The [Guide's evaluation chapter](/guide/evaluate/) develops a worked comparison.

**Coordination qualification, 28 November 2026.** The [EW-01 appraisal](/research/evidence-watch-2026-11-27/#ew-01-coordination-mechanisms) gives bounded reasons to examine which function explicit and implicit coordination perform in the case. OIBOK's proposed check is to trace the actual route to usable, timely action before diagnosing a missing connection. Compare ordinary and unfamiliar cases, changes of personnel and the effort of keeping the arrangement reliable. An absent meeting or formal handoff record is insufficient by itself to establish failure. Preserve required authority, approvals and records. Connection Quality remains a provisional hypothesis; these checks do not turn coordination style into a validated score.

## 10 · Responsible exercise

**Released claim · OI-IP-010**

> Instrumental Organisational Intelligence can be exercised irresponsibly; responsible exercise requires proportionate epistemic quality, legitimate authority, accountability, contestable purpose, affected-interest representation and appropriate human and technical control.

**Claim type.** A normative governance proposition. It states commitments about how capability should be exercised. A performance improvement cannot, by itself, establish that those commitments are justified or fulfilled.

**What the sources support.** Bovens distinguishes an actor's obligation to explain conduct to a forum that can question and judge, with possible consequences for the actor, from simply making information visible. His conceptual framework concerns public accountability; its evaluative perspectives can favour different arrangements. [Bovens (2007), pp. 450–453 and 462–468](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0386.2007.00378.x).

The NIST AI RMF 1.0 provides a related governance reference for AI: clear responsibilities, defined human oversight and consideration of feedback from people outside the development or deployment team. It is voluntary guidance, not a causal study of these controls. [NIST (2023), Executive Summary and GOVERN 2.1, 3.2 and 5.1–5.2, pp. 2 and 23–24](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1).

**Where OIBOK extends it.** OIBOK applies its responsible-exercise commitments across organisational work, including work without AI. These sources help articulate selected responsibilities; they do not prove the completeness of OIBOK's list or settle disagreements about legitimate purpose, rights and the distribution of benefits and burdens. The decision needs a stated rationale and appropriate scrutiny from affected perspectives.

**What could challenge the judgement?** Choose a consequential decision and ask an affected person to explain how they could question its purpose, correct a material error or obtain review. Inspect a real challenge where one exists. Check whether the decision owner can explain the grounds, whether the reviewer can act, and whether correction reaches the work. An unusable appeal route, missing authority or unaddressed exclusion weakens a claim that the arrangement meets its stated commitments. Disproportionate controls can also warrant revision. These observations assess the arrangement against declared values; they do not turn responsibility into a score derived from speed or output. [Governance and contribution process](/governance/).

**Access check, 28 November 2026.** Bovens: the stated sections of the full publisher paper, reproduced in a public audit-library collection. NIST: the stated sections of the official 2023 framework. Independent subject review of this OIBOK application remains pending.

## 11 · AI within organisational work

**Released claim · OI-IP-011**

> AI contributes to Organisational Intelligence only where model capability is integrated with organisational identity, context, knowledge, authority, workflow, operational state, evidence, human capability, outcome evaluation, memory, continuity and exit.

**What the evidence supports.** The three records in the [dated evidence supplement](/research/working-with-ai/#source-register) provide different ways to investigate a bounded work arrangement. WAI-01 supplies candidate mechanisms; WAI-02 supplies operational observations; WAI-03 separates assisted performance from subsequent judgement. Their methods and limitations constrain the claims that can be made from them.

**Where OIBOK extends it.** The released proposition brings these concerns into a much wider account of persistent organisational capacity. That synthesis requires its own tests. The wording "only where" should not be taken as an empirically established checklist of conditions that are all necessary for every useful AI contribution. Specify the contribution and boundary before deciding which conditions need evidence.

| Claim being examined | Evidence to seek | Limit on the conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A bounded contribution | The intended beneficiary's outcome, quality, total effort, implementation and a credible comparison within the workflow. | A useful task or workflow result does not establish organisation-wide intelligence. |
| A sustained organisational capability | The contribution continues across relevant episodes, with usable ownership, current knowledge, required judgement and appropriate recovery. Examine a change of responsible person or conditions where relevant. | Continuity is demonstrated only over the conditions and horizon examined. |
| A complete OI claim | Connected evidence across Orient, Coordinate and Improve, transitions, consequences, feedback closure, persistence and governance. Assess responsible exercise separately. | These studies do not validate the whole model or establish the necessity of all its elements. |

**What could change the judgement?** Compare the same improved arrangement with and without AI where feasible. If clearer policy or a simpler automation gives equivalent outcomes with less burden, qualify AI's proposed contribution. If the benefit disappears when its creator is absent, narrow the continuity claim. If a team repeatedly achieves and sustains the claimed capability without a proposed integration element, investigate whether that element is necessary or whether another arrangement performs its function. An undocumented but shared routine may matter; absence of a document is not evidence that the function is absent.

**Practical implication to test.** Give the work an accountable owner, identify who may change it, ask who receives the benefits and burdens, and plan how required judgement and source material will remain usable. The depth should match the case. Role broadening, shared data and particular technical architectures remain options to assess. An ontology, knowledge graph or centralised system is not established as a necessary solution by these sources.

**Context and knowledge application, 28 November 2026.** The [EW-02](/research/evidence-watch-2026-11-27/#ew-02-useful-knowledge) and [EW-03](/research/evidence-watch-2026-11-27/#ew-03-compatible-understanding) appraisals inform further checks in [Guide Chapter 3](/guide/work-and-knowledge/). Keep information availability, semantic representation, usable organisational knowledge and organisational capability distinct. Ask the people using a representation to reconstruct the same consequential case, explain its meaning and identify unresolved differences. Evaluate whether it helps the work, what it gets wrong and what it takes to repair and maintain. This is an application to test, not new validation of OI-IP-011. The [EW-04 candidate](/research/evidence-watch-2026-11-27/#ew-04-cognitive-ambidexterity-candidate) is deferred and contributes no stronger evidence claim.

## Scope of the check

The Gittell, Levitt–March and Darr comparisons use the published abstracts. The attention check uses the opening theoretical argument in Ocasio and the introduction in Joseph et al. The [25 November source register](/research/working-with-ai/#source-register) states the inspected sections and access limits for the new AI studies. These notes distinguish a local explanation that can be contradicted by case records from the broader organisation-level claim, which needs comparisons across cases and settings. Definitions and governance principles elsewhere in the register call for different kinds of scrutiny.

The [27 November watch supplement](/research/evidence-watch-2026-11-27/#source-appraisals), assessed on 28 November, records the full-text scope for EW-01, EW-02, EW-03 and EW-05, the abstract-only limit and deferred disposition for EW-04, and the out-of-period status of EW-05. Its applications extend the questions asked here without changing the research 1.0 quotations or claiming a new research release.

# Working with AI: evidence and implications for OIBOK

**Editorial evidence supplement 0.1 · 25 November 2026**

This update examines evidence relevant to the gap between improving a task and improving the work around it. It adds source appraisals and practical tests to the [integrated evidence notes](/research/integrated-evidence-notes/). The research baseline remains OIBOK 1.0; its released propositions and source registers retain their original wording and bytes. Independent review of this supplement is pending.

## Editorial decision

**Accept for bounded use in evidence notes and Guide 0.6.1.** The three sources below inform questions about workflow constraints, human judgement and sustained capability. They do not validate the complete OIBOK model, establish a universal need to reorganise, or justify an organisational intelligence score.

The practical response is to make existing guidance easier to test: follow work through its limiting stage, identify who can change the arrangement, examine benefits and burdens, and observe whether the required judgement develops and remains available. These are OIBOK's proposed applications of the evidence.

## Source register

The identifiers below belong to this dated supplement. They do not renumber the 1.0 source register. Access dates are 25 November 2026.

### WAI-01 · Working with AI

Nathan Wilmers, Maxim Massenkoff and Peter McCrory. Anthropic Institute technical report, 23 November 2026. [Article](https://www.anthropic.com/research/working-with-ai) · [Report PDF](https://assets.claude.com/0cb09418f04d09baf72922e53a3f17383ef838c9.pdf).

**Inspected:** article and appendix. **Design:** 66 one-off interviews across 58 selected US customers; self-reported value and automated coding. **Finding:** greater attributed value accompanied organisational change. **Limits:** voluntary, non-representative, supplier-affiliated research; no causal estimate. Interview fieldwork dates and coding-validation details were not identified in the inspected methods.

**OIBOK appraisal:** use this source to formulate mechanisms worth testing. Selection, prior management capability and successful applications prompting later changes are competing explanations. Several applications within one company do not provide independent organisation-level replications. The appropriate next step is to observe the proposed mechanism in bounded work and compare plausible alternatives.

### WAI-02 · Artificial Intelligence in the Firm: Bottlenecks in Software Production

Fiona Chen and James Stratton. Harvard job market paper, version dated 19 November 2026; first version dated 7 January 2026. [Primary paper](https://fion.ac/jellyfish.pdf).

**Inspected:** the accessible paper's abstract and introduction, printed pages 1 to 5, including the design, principal findings and funding statement. This is a targeted reading, not an audit of the full appendix or underlying data.

**Design and reported finding:** staggered difference-in-differences using 445 million work events for 720,542 workers at 655 firms, January 2021 to August 2026. Increased code output did not translate proportionally into final software output; review queues and review work became constraints.

**Limits and interests:** adoption was not random, identification depends on comparable untreated trends, and final software output uses Jira measures. The working paper uses proprietary Jellyfish data and reports research funding, including Stripe and Schmidt Sciences support. Its long-run simulations are projections, not observed outcomes.

**OIBOK appraisal:** this provides a different evidence type from WAI-01 for inspecting complementary stages. The judgement should remain specific to the measured process. Distinguish an unusable handoff from insufficient capacity, more difficult cases, poor inputs or limited downstream demand.

### WAI-03 · Does AI Assistance Enhance or Erode Expertise? Evidence from a Three-Month Field Experiment in Patent Drafting

David Autor, Tanya Rodchenko, Joshua Martin, Zanna Iscenko, Scott Strand, David Pearl and Melissa Ferere. NBER working paper, 2026. [Google Research record and abstract](https://research.google/pubs/does-ai-assistance-enhance-or-erode-expertise-evidence-from-a-three-month-field-experiment-in-patent-drafting/) · [NBER record](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35720).

**Inspected:** the official Google Research abstract. The linked NBER full text was unavailable in this check; detailed implementation, funding disclosures and analyses were not independently examined.

**Design and reported finding:** a preregistered three-month randomised trial with 133 patent lawyers at 11 US firms and blinded expert assessment. Assisted drafting improved, with larger gains among juniors. Subsequent unaided judgement gains were concentrated among seniors; juniors showed no average gain. That is not an average decline.

**Limits and interests:** a specialised task, short follow-up and custom assistant limit transferability. The institutional source is Google Research; this abstract-level check does not establish independent review of its funding or interests.

**OIBOK appraisal:** distinguish current assisted performance from developing judgement. Test the human contribution required by the actual work arrangement. Reproducing every task without a tool is not a general requirement for organisational capability, and this source does not establish inevitable deskilling.

## What OIBOK should test next

### Follow the limiting stage

Record comparable cases from arrival to accepted completion. Separate production, waiting, review, correction and final outcome. After a change, examine which stage limits completion and whether its capacity, inputs or authority changed. If a different team acquires additional work, include that team's time and experience.

A useful local hypothesis is that a specified change to reviewing capacity or decision rights will improve accepted completion while meeting the agreed quality constraint. Record an alternative explanation and an observation that would weaken the hypothesis before examining the results. The worked example in [Guide Chapter 6](/guide/evaluate/) shows how to make this comparison concrete.

### Examine participation and continued judgement

Ask the people doing the work how the proposal changes their responsibilities, recognition, workload and opportunities to learn. A concern about quality, limited time, fear of loss and a disagreement about purpose call for different responses. Avoid assigning a motive without evidence.

Where experienced judgement remains necessary, specify how someone will acquire it. A suitable exercise might involve an initial judgement, comparison with the evidence and AI output, discussion with a supervisor, and a later unfamiliar case. Evaluate the required judgement separately from the quality of assisted output. Treat this as a proposed work design to test, not an intervention validated by WAI-03.

### Test OIBOK's additional value

Compare the questions raised through OIBOK with the team's usual review, using comparable cases and a similar time allowance. Record whether the OIBOK prompts reveal a consequential issue, distinguish explanations or change a decision. Also record occasions when they add burden without useful insight. This addresses the [construct-validity expectations](/knowledge/integrated-and-responsible-organisational-intelligence/#25-construct-validity-and-falsification-expectations) already present in Chapter 10.

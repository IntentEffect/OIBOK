## 1. Purpose

This register consolidates every working proposition from the nine completed Project OI domain chapters into one traceable evidence and disposition system.

It is intended to:

- assign a stable global identifier to every proposition;
- preserve the exact chapter wording and source location;
- map each proposition to the OI capability model and preferred glossary terms;
- distinguish direct literature anchors, source-grounded synthesis, emerging AI-era evidence and Project OI integrative extensions;
- make confidence, evidence limits and required validation visible;
- identify duplicate, overlapping and transition propositions for the first BOK release;
- and prevent Chapter 10 from presenting 182 locally useful propositions as 182 independent validated constructs.

The register does not validate the propositions. A direct literature anchor means that the proposition closely reflects a cited source or established research line; it does not mean that the proposition’s complete Project OI wording or cross-domain generalisation has been tested independently.

## 4. Evidence and Confidence Model

### 4.1 Direct Literature Anchor — D

Use where the proposition closely reflects a source finding, established distinction or mature research line cited in the owning chapter. “Direct” describes the route from source to proposition, not validation of the complete Project OI construct.

### 4.2 Source-Grounded Synthesis — S

Use where the proposition combines several sources, applies a source mechanism to the organisational level or creates a chapter-level implication that is not stated verbatim in one source.

### 4.3 Emerging AI-Era Evidence — A

Use where the proposition depends materially on recent evidence about AI, Agents, automation or human–AI work. These propositions require explicit qualification by task, model, organisation, interface, study design and date.

### 4.4 Project OI Integrative Extension — O

Use where the proposition primarily connects capabilities, formalises a transition, introduces a Project OI working construct or makes a normative design claim not yet established as an empirical generalisation.

### 4.5 Confidence

Confidence reflects the present evidence basis of the proposition, not confidence that the proposition is universally true. All propositions remain subject to construct-validity, cross-chapter and field review.

## 9. Consolidation Findings

### 9.1 The Register Contains Four Different Claim Types

The 182 propositions should not be treated as one homogeneous body of evidence. Directly anchored propositions, chapter synthesis, recent AI evidence and Project OI integrative extensions require different treatment in Chapter 10 and the adversarial review.

### 9.2 Connection and Transition Propositions Need One Integrated Form

Nine chapter-ending propositions state that the local capability must connect forward. They should remain in their source chapters but become one higher-order Chapter 10 proposition on **Connection Quality** and loop closure.

### 9.3 One Exact Duplicate Requires Merge

OI-PROP-EVL-003 and OI-PROP-LRN-007 are both titled **Feedback Is Not Learning**. The final proposition set should use OI-PROP-LRN-007 as semantic owner while preserving Chapter 7 as the transition and evidence source.

### 9.4 Transfer Propositions Need Scope Rather Than Collapse

Knowledge transfer, vicarious learning and capability transfer all require recontextualisation. They should remain distinct because the transferred object and receiving conditions differ.

### 9.5 AI Propositions Need Expiry-Aware Evidence

The 26 AI-era propositions should carry explicit metadata for:

- task and domain;
- model and version;
- interface and tool conditions;
- organisational setting;
- human role and authority;
- study design;
- date and update trigger.

They should not be generalised from one field experiment, benchmark or provider implementation to “AI” as a stable class.

### 9.6 Direct Source Anchoring Does Not Remove Level-of-Analysis Risk

Several direct anchors originate in individual, team, technical-system or large-enterprise research. Chapter 10 must not infer an organisation-level OI capability without testing the aggregation and persistence mechanism.

### 9.7 The Final Proposition Set Should Be Smaller

The 182 chapter propositions are a traceability set, not the recommended final theory. OI-BOK-010 should select a parsimonious higher-order proposition set while preserving the full register as supporting evidence and diagnostic detail.

## 10. High-Priority Review Sets

### 10.1 Project OI Integrative Extensions

All O-class propositions require the strongest construct-boundary and field-testing discipline because they are the principal candidates for Project OI’s distinctive theoretical contribution.

### 10.2 Emerging AI-Era Propositions

All A-class propositions require temporal maintenance and should be reviewed before each BOK release for changed model capability, research evidence and governance conditions.

### 10.3 Working-Term Propositions

Propositions depending materially on glossary working terms such as attentional debt, context drift, shadow authority, handoff integrity, operational debt, borrowed capability, capability opacity and capability sovereignty require terminology validation before empirical operationalisation.

### 10.4 Normative Propositions

Propositions using “should”, “must”, “responsible” or equivalent normative language require explicit separation of:

- empirical claim;
- design recommendation;
- ethical commitment;
- and governance requirement.

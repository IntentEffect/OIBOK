# From AI tools to organisational capability

An organisation's investment in AI becomes useful when it improves the work people need to accomplish. That requires a clear outcome, usable knowledge, legitimate decisions, coordinated action and a way to learn from the consequences. OIBOK offers a connected set of questions for designing and examining those conditions.

Begin with the capability the organisation wants to develop. It might be resolving a class of service requests accurately, making a recurring decision with better evidence or detecting an operational problem early enough to respond. The AI task then sits within a wider piece of work whose performance can be assessed.

## Design the work around the task

Suppose an AI assistant can prepare a useful draft response. The organisation still needs to identify the current source of guidance, provide the relevant case context, decide who checks the draft and establish who can approve the resulting action. It also needs a way to handle an exception or conflicting information.

OIBOK's [Knowledge and Expertise chapter](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/knowledge/knowledge-and-expertise/) examines how relevant understanding becomes available and credible in practice. The [Decision and Authority chapter](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/knowledge/decision-and-authority/) distinguishes a recommendation from an authorised organisational commitment. Together, they encourage a concrete question: what must happen between receiving a plausible answer and taking a legitimate action?

This is where work design matters. Name the task, permitted inputs, expected output, decision owner and next recipient. Make the evidence needed for review available to the reviewer. Include the time and expertise required to check and correct the work when estimating its total effort.

## Coordinate beyond the individual user

An individual's useful interaction with an AI system is one part of organisational work. Other people may need to understand the decision, supply missing information, manage dependencies or continue the case later.

The [Coordination and Collective Action chapter](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/knowledge/coordination-and-collective-action/) focuses on those dependencies and the context that must travel across handoffs. The [Execution and Operational Reliability chapter](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/knowledge/execution-and-operational-reliability/) examines authorised execution, exceptions, recovery and operational control.

Applied to an AI-assisted workflow, this means specifying what completion looks like, who owns an unresolved case and how work continues when the system or its output is unsuitable. These arrangements should be visible to the people responsible for the outcome.

A repeatable capability also needs continuity. Relevant knowledge, authority and working practices must remain usable when the person handling the work changes or when a new situation tests the original assumptions.

## Give people a shared reference

Project management offers an example of the role a body of knowledge can play. PMI's [PMBOK Guide](https://www.pmi.org/standards/pmbok) organises guidance around principles and areas of practice, with an emphasis on adapting approaches to context. A shared reference gives practitioners a structure for discussing work and improving how it is approached.

OIBOK applies that organising ambition to a different question: how organisations orient, coordinate and improve over time. Its current [research baseline](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/research/release-record/) is a developing, literature-grounded synthesis, with empirical questions still open. The practical value to test is whether its connections help a team design and evaluate a better way of working.

That inquiry can lead to several choices. A team might improve its source material, clarify decision authority, redesign a handoff, introduce AI assistance or combine these changes. The [AI Opportunity and Work Design Canvas](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/tools/ai-opportunity-and-work-design/) makes the comparison explicit before a pilot is selected.

## Evaluate the whole intervention

Measure the outcome that matters alongside errors, exceptions and total human effort. Record the baseline, eligible cases, observation period and comparison before interpreting results. A quicker draft is useful evidence about one task; the effect on the complete workflow needs its own examination.

If an intervention combines AI with training, clearer policy and redesigned handoffs, an improvement cannot automatically be attributed to AI alone. Workload, case mix, staffing and measurement differences may also contribute. The [Outcome and Learning Record](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/tools/outcome-and-learning/) keeps these alternatives visible and connects the decision to subsequent changes in practice.

The next question is what the organisation retains. Which source, routine, responsibility or training changes? Who maintains it? When will someone check whether it still helps? OIBOK's [account of organisational learning](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/knowledge/organisational-memory-reflection-and-learning/) makes that continuing use of experience central to the inquiry.

# Start with one workflow

A useful first step with OIBOK is to choose one recurring workflow and investigate it with the people responsible for the work. This gives the team a defined problem, evidence it can inspect and an improvement it can evaluate. The [practical toolkit](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/tools/) is organised around that approach.

Choose something bounded enough to follow from beginning to end: a service request, an approval, an operational exception or a recurring planning decision. Name the outcome that matters and the person accountable for it. Include people who do the work, maintain its knowledge and depend on its handoffs.

## Follow a few real cases

Start with the [Organisational Intelligence Practice Review](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/tools/practice-review/). Define where the workflow begins and ends, the relevant timeframe, exclusions and constraints. Bring recent cases, including a successful case and an exception, with source references and dates.

A small set of cases can expose a question worth investigating. It cannot establish an organisation-wide rate or prove a cause. Separate what the records show from what participants believe explains it. Preserve disagreements and identify the evidence that could change each interpretation.

Consider a hypothetical team handling requests to change a booking. The initial question might be why some requests require repeated contact. The review could examine how a request arrives, which policy applies, who can approve a change and what the next person needs to continue the work. This is an illustration of the inquiry, with no assumed findings or results.

## Map knowledge and decisions together

The Practice Review includes a **Context and Knowledge Map**. Use it to identify the information and expertise needed at important points in the workflow. Record the source, current version, accountable owner and consequence of a gap.

In the hypothetical booking workflow, relevant questions could include whether staff can identify the current policy, whether exceptions are documented and whether the history of the request reaches the next person. Mark unavailable evidence as unknown and assign a way to investigate it.

The accompanying **Decision and Coordination Canvas** follows authority and handoffs. Record who decides, who acts, what context must travel with the work and how an exception is escalated. A named owner is useful only if the role has the relevant authority and can act on the information provided. These maps connect the review to OIBOK's chapters on [context](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/knowledge/organisational-context/), [knowledge](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/knowledge/knowledge-and-expertise/), [authority](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/knowledge/decision-and-authority/) and [coordination](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/knowledge/coordination-and-collective-action/).

## Compare credible changes

Choose one improvement or investigation, with a reason, owner and review date. Keep competing explanations visible. A delay might reflect unclear authority, missing context, workload or a problem in the underlying service. The appropriate next step depends on the evidence.

If AI is being considered, move into the [AI Opportunity and Work Design Canvas](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/tools/ai-opportunity-and-work-design/). Compare the current approach, a credible change without AI and an AI-assisted option. Specify the proposed task, allowed inputs, human responsibilities, exceptions and fallback. Include checking, correction, training and support in the effort estimate.

For the hypothetical team, a possible comparison might include clearer policy ownership and handoff fields, with AI assistance considered for a defined retrieval or drafting task. The canvas records what would need to be established before testing that option. If AI is irrelevant to the selected change, proceed directly to evaluation planning.

## Plan the learning before the change

Use the [Outcome and Learning Record](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/tools/outcome-and-learning/) to agree the baseline or comparison, eligible cases, follow-up period and measures. Include outcome quality and errors alongside time or effort. Define who collects the evidence, how missing observations are recorded and what conditions would pause the intervention.

Keep one case identifier across the review, proposed change and evaluation. After the observation period, compare the results with the original plan. Consider workload, case mix and other changes before deciding to continue, adapt, stop or gather more evidence.

Then assign the changes to knowledge and practice. Identify what should be updated, who owns it and when a later check will establish whether people still use it. Practical Toolkit 0.1 provides methods for piloting; its worksheets do not produce a validated intelligence or maturity score.

The [A3 canvases](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/tools/#download-the-a3-canvases) provide concise working records. An [AI agent can help organise the inquiry](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/for-agents/), provided its sources, assumptions and action authority remain clear. The team retains responsibility for the evidence and decisions.

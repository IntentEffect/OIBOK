# Start with one workflow

A useful first step with OIBOK is to choose one recurring workflow and investigate it with the people responsible for the work. This gives the team a defined problem, evidence it can inspect and an improvement it can evaluate. The [practical toolkit](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/tools/) is organised around that approach.

Choose something bounded enough to follow from beginning to end: a service request, an approval, an operational exception or a recurring planning decision. Look for work that recurs, matters to someone and has records the team can inspect. The owner should be able to act on at least some of the findings. A broad ambition such as improving customer service needs a more specific starting point.

Name the outcome and its accountable owner. Include people who perform the work, maintain the relevant knowledge and receive its handoffs. Someone who can assess the evidence should participate, with systems support where needed. A facilitator able to question the sponsor's preferred solution can help preserve disagreement. If an affected role is absent, record whose perspective still needs to be heard.

## Follow a few real cases

Start with the [Organisational Intelligence Practice Review](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/tools/practice-review/). Define where the workflow begins and ends, the relevant timeframe, exclusions and constraints. Bring recent cases, including a successful case and an exception, with source references and dates. Keep sensitive records in their authorised location and link the evidence needed for the review.

A small set of cases can expose a question worth investigating. It cannot establish an organisation-wide rate or prove a cause. Separate what the records show from what participants believe explains it. An account of difficulty is evidence of that person's experience; its frequency and cause remain questions to investigate.

Consider a hypothetical team handling requests to change a booking. Its question is why some requests require repeated contact. The scope could run from receipt of a standard change request to confirmation and follow-up, with specialist disputes handled separately. This example illustrates the inquiry and assumes no findings or results.

For each selected request, reconstruct what arrived, what staff checked, where work waited and what the customer received. A timestamp can establish an interval without explaining the wait. A missing approval might reflect unclear authority, an unavailable decision-maker or an incomplete request. Preserve those alternatives and look for a contrasting case that could help distinguish them. Record missing evidence as unknown, with someone responsible for finding it.

## Map knowledge and decisions together

The Practice Review includes a **Context and Knowledge Map**. Use it to identify the information and expertise needed at important points in the workflow. Record the source, applicable version, accountable owner and consequence of a gap.

In the booking workflow, staff may need the original booking terms, the requested change, previous commitments and the policy governing exceptions. Ask how they establish which policy version applies to this booking and who resolves conflicting guidance. A link to a document is useful only if the person doing the work can access and interpret it. Record where expert judgement remains necessary.

The accompanying **Decision and Coordination Canvas** follows authority and handoffs. Record who decides, who acts and how exceptions are escalated. For a booking change, the receiving person may need the customer's request, checks already completed, policy reference and unresolved decision. Agree what makes that handoff complete and who owns it while approval is pending. A named owner needs authority and usable information. These maps connect to OIBOK's chapters on [context](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/knowledge/organisational-context/), [knowledge](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/knowledge/knowledge-and-expertise/), [authority](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/knowledge/decision-and-authority/) and [coordination](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/knowledge/coordination-and-collective-action/).

## Compare credible changes

Choose one improvement or investigation, with a reason, owner and review date. If the evidence cannot yet distinguish missing context from insufficient capacity, assign that investigation before committing to a solution.

If AI is being considered, use the [AI Opportunity and Work Design Canvas](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/tools/ai-opportunity-and-work-design/). Compare current practice, a credible change without AI and an AI-assisted option. In the booking example, the non-AI option could combine clearer policy ownership, a short handoff form and explicit approval responsibilities. It needs enough support and training to be a fair alternative.

An AI-assisted option could add retrieval of relevant policy passages and preparation of a draft response. Specify the allowed inputs, required source references and what the adviser must check. In this proposed design, the adviser retains responsibility for the response and an authorised person approves any booking change. Missing or conflicting policy would trigger escalation, with the established manual process available as fallback.

Include setup, training, checking, correction and ongoing support in the effort estimate. Comparing the AI-assisted option with the same organisational improvements without AI would help examine its additional contribution. Comparing the complete package only with current practice would leave that contribution unresolved. If AI is irrelevant, proceed directly to evaluation planning.

## Plan the learning before the change

Use the [Outcome and Learning Record](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/tools/outcome-and-learning/) to agree the baseline or comparison, eligible cases, follow-up period and measures before introducing the change. Keep one case identifier across the review, proposal and evaluation so the eventual decision can be traced to its evidence.

For the booking team, repeat contact needs a definition: which contacts concern the same request, during what period, and out of how many eligible cases? Time to resolution needs agreed start and end points. Include accuracy of the final change and total human handling, including review and correction. Track defects found in drafts separately from errors reaching customers. Assign collection owners and retain incomplete follow-up as missing evidence.

Choose which outcome will lead the decision and what quality must be preserved. If reducing repeat contact is the priority, read response speed alongside whether the request was resolved and stayed resolved. Record proposed targets and their reasoning before observing pilot results, together with the effort the team can sustain. These are local decisions to justify and review; the toolkit supplies no universal threshold for success.

Use historical cases with comparable definitions where possible, or collect a baseline if existing records cannot support one. Record differences in workload, staffing and case complexity when comparing periods or groups. Agree what would justify continuing, adapting, stopping or gathering more evidence, and which incidents would pause use. An unauthorised booking change, for example, could require immediate suspension of the affected task and owner review.

At the review date, examine the results against the original plan, including contrary cases and deviations. Better averages alongside increased correction work may require a different decision from the one initially expected. A small pilot can inform the next step while leaving important uncertainty unresolved.

## Make the learning usable

Translate the decision into owned changes. In the booking example, the policy owner might clarify exception guidance, the team leader revise the handoff record and the systems owner update retrieval settings. Each action needs a location, owner, due date and evidence of completion. Preserve the reasoning and retire superseded guidance where appropriate.

Set a later check to see whether another person can use the revised knowledge and authority in subsequent cases. This tests whether the lesson has entered working practice. Practical Toolkit 0.1 provides methods for piloting; its worksheets do not produce a validated intelligence or maturity score.

The [A3 canvases](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/tools/#download-the-a3-canvases) provide concise working records. An [AI agent can help organise the inquiry](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/for-agents/), provided its sources, assumptions and action authority remain clear. The team retains responsibility for the evidence and decisions.

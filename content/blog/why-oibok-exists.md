# Why OIBOK exists

Organisations can have capable people, extensive knowledge and powerful technology while still struggling to turn what they know into coordinated action and lasting improvement. OIBOK helps them examine and strengthen those connections.

That is the gap the Organisation Intelligence Body of Knowledge was created to explore. A useful answer must connect the information people can access, the decisions they are authorised to make, the work they need to coordinate and the learning they carry into the next situation. [OIBOK's purpose](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/about/) brings those questions into a shared research and practice agenda.

## Understanding the connections

An organisation may record a recurring problem without giving it the attention needed for action. It may have relevant expertise that a decision-maker cannot locate. A sound decision may lose its rationale as work passes between teams. A review may identify an important lesson without changing the conditions of the next decision.

These are different problems, but they are connected. Improving one part of the work can leave an important dependency unresolved. A better knowledge base still needs an owner, a way to identify current guidance and a route into the decisions where that knowledge matters.

OIBOK's working definition therefore focuses on an organisation's persistent capacity to form an orientation, coordinate collective response and improve through feedback. Its three organising transformations are **Orient, Coordinate and Improve**. The [integrating chapter](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/knowledge/integrated-and-responsible-organisational-intelligence/) examines how these relate, including authority, responsibility, continuity and the limits of the proposed model.

The practical question is whether useful understanding can survive the movement from one person, team or episode to another, and remain available when it is needed again.

## Useful to a person, usable by an organisation

A person may use an AI assistant to find information, prepare a brief or generate options. Whether that help becomes organisational capability depends on what happens around it. Can another person locate the same authoritative source? Can a reviewer distinguish the source from generated interpretation? Is there someone with authority to act on the recommendation? The [Knowledge and Expertise chapter](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/knowledge/knowledge-and-expertise/) makes these distinctions explicit when examining human and AI participation.

OIBOK treats persistence as a further question. A useful result today may depend on one person's relationships, judgement or private working notes. To make an organisation-level claim, the inquiry needs evidence about how relevant knowledge and practices are retained, recovered and used across the period being examined. Chapter 10's account of [persistence and levels of analysis](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/knowledge/integrated-and-responsible-organisational-intelligence/) provides that boundary.

This allows for changing people, tools and routines. The interest is in how the organisation maintains and improves its ability to respond as those conditions change.

## A shared reference across disciplines

Knowledge management, decision science, organisational learning, operations and systems thinking already offer substantial ways to examine this work. OIBOK draws on these disciplines and makes their relationships an explicit subject of inquiry. Its [ten chapters](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/knowledge/) provide entry points into signals, context, knowledge, decisions, coordination, execution, outcomes, memory, adaptation and responsible integration.

The proposed contribution is a way to follow a problem across those perspectives. Knowledge management can help examine whether guidance is available and maintained. Decision and coordination questions then address how that guidance enters an authorised commitment and travels with the work. Evaluation and learning examine what follows and how experience changes future practice. The connections help define where an investigation needs to go next, while leaving room for the specialist methods appropriate to each question.

A shared language helps make a discussion more precise. Is the issue missing information, conflicting interpretation, unavailable expertise or unclear authority? Does a handoff transfer the evidence and conditions behind a decision, or only the instruction? What would show that a lesson has changed future practice?

The purpose of these distinctions is to help people choose what to investigate. A term earns its place when it clarifies a problem, exposes an assumption or connects useful knowledge. The glossary and research registers make those choices visible so others can challenge them.

## A hypothetical example

Consider a hypothetical service team that repeatedly escalates unusual customer requests. It has a policy library and experienced staff. An AI assistant is proposed to help people find guidance and draft responses. Faster retrieval is a reasonable possibility to investigate, but the wider workflow raises other questions.

Does the library identify which policy is current? Do staff know who can authorise an exception? When a case changes hands, does the next person receive the original evidence and the reason for escalation? Does anyone review recurring exceptions and decide whether the policy itself needs to change?

Following a few contrasting cases could help distinguish these possibilities. If guidance is current but decision rights are unclear, clarifying authority may be a useful first intervention. If the problem concerns conflicting sources, the team may need to establish ownership and version control before testing retrieval assistance. Workload or an underlying service problem could still provide another explanation.

The example has no assumed outcome. It illustrates how a connected inquiry can turn a broad request for an AI solution into a specific question, an accountable next step and evidence to collect. The [Practice Review](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/tools/practice-review/) provides a structure for doing that work.

## Moving from knowledge to useful action

The ambition is to help an organisation identify a problem, make an informed change, and establish whether that change improved how it works.

That ambition needs practical resources alongside the research. The [OIBOK toolkit](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/tools/) provides a Practice Review, an AI Opportunity and Work Design Canvas, and an Outcome and Learning Record. Supporting maps make context, knowledge, decision rights and handoffs easier to examine together.

A team can begin with one recurring workflow, bring real cases, identify what remains uncertain and choose a bounded next step. When AI is being considered, the tools ask the team to compare it with credible alternatives and describe the surrounding work. The evaluation plan then establishes what to observe and what else might explain the result.

For leaders, this creates a more explicit basis for deciding where to put attention and resources. A proposal can state the intended outcome, the dependency it addresses, the person accountable and the evidence needed before extending it. Different teams can examine the same proposed change without assuming that they mean the same thing by better performance.

For people doing the work, the intended benefit is a usable record of what matters: which source to trust, who can decide, what the next person needs and how to handle an exception. OIBOK's [Organisational Memory, Reflection and Learning chapter](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/knowledge/organisational-memory-reflection-and-learning/) then asks how experience changes future understanding and practice, including whether the lesson remains available beyond the person or episode that produced it.

This makes the body of knowledge usable while generating questions that practice can take back to research. Confusing prompts, failed interventions and competing explanations can all improve the work when they are documented carefully.

## Open to use and correction

**Founded by Intent & Effect, developed by the OIBOK community.** OIBOK has its own publication identity and welcomes contributions beyond its founding work. Its original publication content is shared under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/licensing/), and contributors retain copyright in their original contributions.

The [research release](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/research/release-record/) is a literature-grounded working synthesis. Independent field validation of the complete model remains future work. The toolkit provides methods for piloting, with evidence and limitations to record as organisations use it.

The [contribution process](https://intenteffect.github.io/OIBOK/contribute/) welcomes corrections, stronger evidence, conceptual challenges and authorised field observations. The useful test for OIBOK is whether it helps people ask a clearer question, make a more informed change and preserve what they learn for the next occasion.

# Contribute to OIBOK

Help make Organisation Intelligence more useful, better evidenced and easier to challenge. Researchers, practitioners, students and organisations can contribute new evidence, corrections, conceptual criticism, translations, field observations and improvements to the practical tools.

**You do not need to agree with the current model to contribute.** Contrary findings and strong alternative explanations are valuable.

We are seeking one small-business team and one independent subject reviewer for the [Chapter 1 field pilot](/research/chapter-one-pilot/#join-the-pilot).

## Start with a question or a correction

Use the [OIBOK issue tracker](https://github.com/IntentEffect/OIBOK/issues) to suggest research, identify a problem or propose a substantial change. Include the page or chapter, its version, and the passage or claim you want to discuss. For a small correction, you can [submit a pull request](https://github.com/IntentEffect/OIBOK/pulls) directly.

If you prefer to start by email, contact [info@oibok.org](mailto:info@oibok.org) with the same details. Maintainers can help you prepare a contribution for review.

For a substantial conceptual change, open an issue before drafting a large replacement. This gives maintainers and other contributors a place to examine the evidence and agree the scope.

| Contribution | What to include |
| --- | --- |
| Research recommendation | Source title, authors, DOI or stable URL, publication date, methods, findings, limitations and relevant OIBOK claims |
| Conceptual amendment | Existing passage and version, proposed wording, supporting and contradictory evidence, counterexamples, and affected chapters or registers |
| Practitioner case | An authorised, de-identified account of the context, intervention, observations, timeframe, alternative explanations and limits of transferability |
| Tool feedback | Tool version, workflow type, what helped, what was confusing, changes made and what happened afterwards |
| Blog article | A clear organisational question, useful argument or practical guide, supporting sources and a proposed author credit |
| Correction | The error, evidence for the correction and a suggested replacement where possible |
| Translation or accessibility improvement | Source version, intended audience, scope and how meaning or usability has been checked |

## Show the evidence and its limits

Separate what a source reports from your interpretation. Say whether you inspected the full text or only an abstract. Identify study design, sample, organisational setting and relevant conflicts of interest. A persuasive example can suggest a useful hypothesis; it does not establish a general effect.

Check the existing source register and earlier issues before proposing research already covered. Explain what has changed if there is a replication, correction, retraction, new dataset or materially different finding. Map the proposal to the relevant chapter and proposition where possible. The [research methodology](/methodology/) explains the evidence standard in more detail.

## Blog articles

The [OIBOK blog](/blog/) welcomes perspectives, practical guidance and authorised accounts of using the ideas in real work. Propose a topic through the issue tracker or submit a draft with a short summary, intended audience, relevant OIBOK chapters and supporting sources. Suggest the author credit and disclose material AI assistance, relevant interests and any previous publication.

Distinguish opinion from research findings, and identify hypothetical examples clearly. Practical accounts should explain their context, evidence, competing explanations and limits. Only include organisational information you are authorised to publish. The existing contributor copyright and CC BY-SA 4.0 terms apply to original blog contributions.

Blog articles follow editorial review and can be corrected with a dated explanation. Publishing an article does not amend the released research chapters or establish that a method has been validated.

## How a contribution becomes part of the publication

1. **Submit.** Open an issue or pull request with a clear question, source or proposed change.
2. **Assess.** Maintainers examine relevance, evidence quality, competing explanations, conceptual coherence, rights and cross-chapter implications.
3. **Revise.** Contributors and reviewers clarify the proposal, address uncertainties and record remaining disagreements.
4. **Decide.** The decision is recorded as accept, accept with changes, defer, reject with reasons, or retain as an open question.
5. **Reconcile.** Accepted changes are propagated to affected chapters, glossary entries, propositions, diagnostic material, traceability and source registers.
6. **Release.** Material research changes enter a dated, versioned release with a description of their effect. Website corrections can be recorded separately in the changelog.

A submission or a merged website correction does not, by itself, establish a new canonical research edition. The [governance policy](/governance/) describes release authority and reconsideration of decisions.

## Copyright and the contributor licence

**You retain copyright in your original contribution.** When you intentionally submit original publication material for inclusion in OIBOK, you license that material to the project and its readers under [Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/) (CC BY-SA 4.0). The licence is non-exclusive, so you can continue using and licensing your work elsewhere. No copyright assignment to Intent & Effect is required.

Only submit material you have the right to share on those terms. Identify third-party material and its licence or permission separately. Referencing a paper does not permit copying its full text or figures. Obtain any necessary employer, client or co-author permission before submitting their material.

The publication licence covers original publication text, original diagrams, editorial material, guides, blank templates and worked examples, unless a specific notice says otherwise. Names, official marks, third-party material and software have separate treatment under the [licensing policy](/licensing/).

### Publication contributor declaration

By submitting original publication material for inclusion, you confirm:

> I have the necessary rights to submit this contribution. I have identified third-party content, material AI assistance and relevant conflicts of interest. I agree that my original OIBOK publication contribution may be published and reused under CC BY-SA 4.0, while retaining my copyright. I have not included information that I lack permission to disclose.

This declaration applies to material intentionally offered for the publication. It does not transfer ownership of unrelated work or require you to make private organisational records public.

### Website software contributions

A separate licence for original website software has not yet been adopted. Publication permissions do not extend to software merely because it shares this repository. Third-party software retains its own licence. You can report software issues now; maintainers must establish the applicable contribution and reuse terms before accepting original software contributions.

## AI assistance and accountability

Disclose material AI assistance in research, drafting, translation or analysis. Describe what assistance was used and how the resulting claims, citations and permissions were checked. Contributors remain accountable for the material they submit. A generated citation must resolve to a real source that supports the associated claim.

## Confidentiality and respectful participation

Keep confidential organisational records, personal data, credentials and sensitive complaints out of public issues and pull requests. Submit a practitioner case only when you have permission and the account is appropriately de-identified. The practical tools can be used privately without submitting completed worksheets to OIBOK.

Discuss evidence in good faith, credit other people's work and treat participants respectfully. Harassment, discrimination, threats, doxxing, spam and deliberate misrepresentation are unacceptable. Maintainers may edit, remove or restrict inappropriate public contributions, with a proportionate explanation where possible. Disagreement with an OIBOK claim is welcome and is not a conduct violation.

## Recognition

Accepted contributions are recognised through Git history, acknowledgements, contributor information or release notes, according to their nature. Substantial scholarly authorship is assessed by the actual contribution. Submitting a pull request does not automatically confer authorship of the whole publication.
